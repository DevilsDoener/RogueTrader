## The Imperium of Man

For 10,000 years the galaxy-spanning Imperium of Mankind has been the bastion of the human
race. With over a million worlds and a population running into countless thousands of billions,
it is the largest empire in the galaxy of the 41st millennium. For a hundred long centuries the
Imperium has endured, surviving disasters, heresies, and invasions from alien races intent on the
extinction of Humanity. It has resisted all of this through its greatest strengths — the immortal
Beneficent Emperor of Mankind and His vast armies and fleets.

Though the Emperor is a shattered, withered creature, He still watches over and guides humankind
from the restorative essences, elixirs, and billowing alchemical gases of the Golden Throne of
Terra. Through His vast psychic powers, the Emperor directs the Imperium's warships through the
nightmare realm of warp space. It is said that He foresees the many possible futures of the human
race, and steers humanity so that it may overcome the many trials and challenges ahead.

Without the Emperor, the Imperium would be unable to move its armies and fleets to combat its
enemies and enforce its rule. The Imperium would degenerate into hundreds of small empires
clamouring for power while aliens and other monstrous creatures devoured and destroyed Mankind.

The Imperium is inconceivably vast, spanning many thousands of light years — it would require months, even years, of
travel to traverse it. More than a million worlds and as many distinct cultures make up humanity, and
new star systems are constantly being discovered, feeding mankind's endless hunger for resources
and space. To many citizens, even those living close to Terra, the Emperor and the Imperium are
distant, almost mythical forces; some labour their whole lives scarcely aware the Imperium
exists except as a children's story, while for others it is a very real iron fist enforcing the
order mankind needs to survive.

It is an age of great upheaval: with every passing year, more people manifest powerful psychic
gifts. Untrained "rogue psykers" can unwittingly unleash forces that destroy settlements or even
whole worlds, though those with sufficient mental strength can be trained to use their powers in
the Imperium's service. Vast as it is, the Imperium does not rule the whole galaxy — mankind's
worlds are spread thin across some 200 trillion stars, and within its own vague borders lie
rebellious human enclaves, domains ruled by alien warlords, and primitive colonies that draw
little attention. The Imperium exists in a state of near-constant war, sometimes simply
continuing its wars of expansion, other times fighting for its very survival.

## Institutions of the Imperium

### The High Lords of Terra

The Imperium is governed by a body of twelve of the most powerful organisation heads, who rule
in the Emperor's name. On the fringes of the Imperium they are a distant legend, but their reach
and power extend even there.

### The Adeptus Terra (Priesthood of Earth)

Known also simply as the Adeptus, this vast institution of millions of servants and religious
followers interprets and enforces the Imperial will handed down by the High Lords, and actually
runs the Imperium — including its armies and fleets. It is divided into many departments and
sub-departments, some so secret their existence is unknown outside their own membership.

- **The Administratum:** by far the largest department of the Adeptus Terra, responsible for
  assessing and levying tithes, distributing Imperial resources, and countless other
  administrative functions. Its members are mostly scribes and petty officials — the hereditary
  slaves of a galaxy-spanning bureaucracy. Its heart lies within the Emperor's Palace on
  Terra, a vast complex of gothic towers and cavernous underground vaults. Probably the single
  most powerful organisation in the Imperium; it is divided into many departments, offices, and
  subordinate organisations.
- **The Adeptus Arbites:** a galactic police force armed and equipped as a military one. Its
  Arbitrators and Judges are a fanatically loyal elite devoted to enforcing Imperial law — even
  against a planetary governor who is late with his tithes, sends too little, or is slow to enforce an Imperial decree, the Arbites may be directed to honour him with a visit. Judges are equipped to wage a minor war, but their main objective is to maintain order.
- **The Adeptus Astra Telepathica:** recruits and trains psykers for service throughout the
  Adeptus Terra, most importantly Astropaths — psykers capable of sending telepathic messages
  across interstellar distances, since ordinary technological communication is useless between
  stars.
- **The Adeptus Astartes (Space Marines):** the warrior Chapters — genetically adapted,
  surgically altered, psychologically conditioned super-humans. Humanity's greatest warriors,
  reserved for the most deadly enemies.
- **The Adeptus Custodes:** the Emperor's Guard, defending the vast Imperial Palace; an elite
  inner corps of 300, the Companions, serve as the Emperor's personal bodyguards.
- **The Adeptus Mechanicus:** the Tech-Priests, guardians of Earth's ancient knowledge, who own
  and administer the factory planet Mars, source of most of the Imperium's weapons, spacecraft,
  and other advanced machinery. As much an arcane cult as a scientific body, its knowledge
  extends into techno-arcana beyond mere technology.

### The Adeptus Ministorum (Ecclesiarchy)

To countless billions the Emperor is a god to whom they devote their entire lives. This faith
has spawned the Adeptus Ministorum, headed by the Ecclesiarch, with its own crusading armies —
the Adepta Sororitas.

### Other institutions

- **The Imperial Guard:** the Imperium's main fighting force, far outnumbering the more
  specialised Space Marines, divided into many armies stationed throughout the Imperium against
  alien invasion and internal rebellion.
- **The Officio Assassinorum:** the Office of Assassins, an important tool of Imperial
  government — when a planetary governor proves rebellious, assassination may be the most
  practical remedy. Even the ranks of the Adeptus Terra are not safe from its attentions.
- **The Inquisition:** one of the most widely feared organisations in the Imperium, mandated to
  investigate and stop any threat to mankind by whatever means necessary. Individual
  Inquisitors are free-roaming troubleshooters bound by no law or authority, and their
  jurisdiction covers alien plots, administrative inefficiency, corruption, mutation, crime,
  secret cults, heresy, and unauthorised psychic activity — no world, organisation, or
  individual (not even a High Lord) is immune to their gaze. Any Imperial force may be
  requisitioned to serve an Inquisitor. Untrained or deviant psykers, however, occupy the
  majority of an Inquisitor's time, earning them the name "witch-hunter" across the galaxy.

## The Imperium of Man in Space

The galaxy is a vast spiral 90,000 light years across and 15,000 light years thick, containing
hundreds of billions of stars, only a fraction of which have habitable systems — and only a tiny
fraction of those have been explored by humanity or any other spacefaring race. The initial human colonisation of the galaxy lies in the distant past, lost, forgotten, and obscured by twenty thousand years of regression and rebuilding. Human worlds are scattered unevenly, with the greatest concentration in the galactic west around sacred Terra
itself; on the fringes, human worlds are rare and often isolated, and many have regressed into
primitive, barbarous states through long periods of isolation.

- **The Domains of the God-Emperor:** the Imperium's reach extends roughly to the limits of the
  **Astronomican**, the psychic beacon by which human vessels navigate the warp — though even
  within this area the Imperium cannot control every system, and many alien and independent
  human empires exist within its borders.
- **The Fringes:** beyond the light of the Astronomican and largely beyond the Imperium's easy
  reach. Contains both ancient human settlements and alien worlds, ranging from feral/barbarous
  to highly advanced. Agents of the Imperium continually explore here, spying out dangers and
  fighting wars beyond the Imperium's formal borders.
- **The Halo Stars:** beyond the Fringes, a ring of the galaxy's most ancient stars, mostly
  extinguished and circled by long-dead worlds whose entombed secrets and horrors are best left
  unexplored — even Rogue Traders often steer clear.
- **Wilderness Space:** vast uncharted regions, often rendered inaccessible by warpstorms,
  awaiting mapping by Explorator fleets; as storms abate, previously inaccessible zones can
  reveal ancient human settlements or alien empires.
- **Alien Worlds:** humanity is but one of many races in the galaxy, though few are so widely distributed or so numerous. Most aliens occupy only a single world or system and are comparatively primitive, peaceful, or powerless. Only a few possess power, aggression, and technology that rivals the Imperium's; among the most common are Orks and Eldar, though many more exist.

### Planetary governance

Each world is ruled by a hereditary **Imperial Commander (Planetary Governor)**, who holds his
world on the Emperor's behalf in exchange for an oath of loyalty and regular tithes, and is free
to administer and defend it as he sees fit. Most worlds maintain interplanetary fleets (lacking
warp engines) for system defence; some governors keep tight control over space traffic while
others are lax, and some even collude with pirates or anarchists for personal profit. Each
planet is responsible for its own defence — ground installations, spaceports, and whatever
fleet it can muster — and vulnerable or historically troubled systems may also host an Imperial
Navy base, independent of the Commander's own forces (both would be ready to meet an emergency). Navy ships may also be stationed in one system to patrol a number of nearby star systems.

## Planets of the Imperium (Classifications)

The Adeptus Terra classifies worlds by use or environment, often combining several
classifications for a single planet (e.g. a Hive World that is also a Night World or Death
World). The major classifications:

- **Hive Worlds:** surfaces rendered largely uninhabitable by centuries of industrial
  processing; population concentrated in towering hive-cities. Factories, mining, and
  atmosphere processing are the main industries; the population survives only through heavy
  food and water imports.
- **Agri-Worlds:** given over almost entirely to crops, hydroponics, or animal husbandry, with population spread thinly across the surface (some are so thinly populated that only a single company of troops is stationed there as a deterrent against pirate attack). Often clustered around Hive Worlds that depend
  on their food supply; some are worked largely by slave-serfs, servitors, or ancient machinery.
- **Civilised Worlds:** the majority of Imperial worlds, though the term could be misleading — even where war has not touched a world for generations, most subjects carry firearms. Self-sufficient, with starports and
  the means to defend themselves against most threats. Further subdivided into Cardinal
  Worlds, Garden Worlds, Mining Worlds, and countless other categories.
- **Dead Worlds:** show minimal or no life yet clear signs of a past civilisation, usually the
  result of ecological catastrophe or devastating war with no discernible Imperial or alien
  cause.
- **Death Worlds:** environments too dangerous for widespread settlement, from jungles full of
  carnivorous life to volcanic, ion-storm-wracked wastelands. Nearly impossible to colonise but
  often hold valuable resources, and the Imperial Guard highly values troops raised in such
  harsh conditions.
- **Research Stations:** orbital stations, asteroid emplacements, or facilities on Dead or Death
  Worlds devoted to research (animal breeding, weapons testing, gene-engineering) or serving as
  watch/listening posts for system defence.
- **Feudal Worlds:** at a technology level just before or after black powder, usually the result
  of centuries of isolation by warpstorms or recovery from war or disaster. The Imperium
  generally lets such worlds progress at their own pace.
- **Forge Worlds:** sovereign domains of the Adeptus Mechanicus, often planet-wide factories and
  frequently home to a Titan Legion — essential to the Imperium's supply of arms and armour.

## Language

- **Low Gothic (Base Gothic / Common Tongue):** the everyday language of most of the Imperium's
  peoples, derived from the speech of distant ancestors; it varies widely from world to world,
  and even within a single world, though enough shared basics usually allow travellers to
  communicate. Some worlds have further debased dialects (hive-bottom slang, asteroid-miner
  colloquialisms) requiring further study to master.
- **High Gothic (Principal / Archaic / Prime Gothic):** an ancient, highly evolved ceremonial
  language used where absolute clarity is required. It has remained virtually unchanged, letting
  speakers from very different cultures communicate complex ideas precisely.
- **Techna-Lingua:** the language of the Adeptus Mechanicus, rarely used outside the Tech-Priest
  orders, though elements bleed into the Low Gothic of highly industrialised cultures. The Magi of the Adeptus Mechanicus have preserved it since the founding of their institutions in the Dark Age of Technology. Full comprehension is reserved for the Priesthood of Mars, and tradition holds that none should
  speak its phrases who have not been sanctified by the Machine God.

## Culture, Mutation, and Abhumans

There are as many ways of life in the Imperium as there are planets, ranging from rarefied
nobility to grinding poverty — sometimes side by side, sometimes kept willfully separate.

**Mutation** arises from extreme environmental adaptation over generations. Attitudes vary
enormously: on many primitive worlds mutants are killed at birth; on more advanced worlds they
may live but rarely enjoy full citizens' rights, and are often segregated, outlawed, or
restricted to certain areas. They form a large, downtrodden portion of Imperial society whose
occasional uprisings are usually crushed swiftly and mercilessly; some mutations are not
immediately visible, letting a few mutants rise to social or military prominence despite
rumour. The Inquisition ruthlessly purges whole worlds where mutation is judged too corrupt to
tolerate.

**Abhumans** are creatures descended from human stock but altered or mutated to a stable,
recognised degree — unlike ordinary mutants, they breed true and conform to a known physical
standard, and are no more prone to further mutation than baseline humans. Millions live within
the Imperium, tolerated or exploited like the rest of the population; they rarely reach positions
of power, are often subject to derision or prejudice, and while recruited into the Guard, fleets,
and other services, they do not serve among the Space Marines and are often kept in segregated
units.

## Communication: Astropaths

The only means of interstellar communication. **Astropaths** send and receive telepathic
messages across space; an individual's range is only several light years, but relay chains of
Astropaths extend the network across almost the entire Imperium. The process is inherently
erratic — like the Astronomican, astropathic messages are affected by the warp's currents and
storms, and may take days or weeks to arrive, wander off course and be picked up by the wrong Astropath, or be delayed by many years, even decades — so that a plea for help may come to light long after any aid would be useful. A message need not be understood by the Astropath transmitting it, allowing secret
codes and ciphers to pass securely across the galaxy.

Astropaths are created through the **Soul Binding** ritual, which brings a trained psyker's mind
close to the Emperor's own psychic power and transfers some of that energy to him. The process
is traumatic — not all survive despite years of preparation, and not all survivors retain their
sanity. Astropaths are a common sight in the Imperium, easily distinguished by their flowing, hooded robes; they serve aboard ships and planets as communicators, and in the Imperial Guard, the Inquisition, the Adeptus Ministorum, the Space Marines, and throughout the Administratum, and the privacy of their sanctums is inviolable under Imperial law. Nearly all surviving Astropaths are left blind, their damaged eyes reduced to distorted,
sunken, empty sockets — though their heightened psychic senses largely compensate for the loss.

### The Imperium and space travel

Without space travel Mankind would have died millennia ago; the defence of the Imperium, trade, communication, and transportation all depend upon interstellar spaceships. A few warp-drive craft are owned by Imperial Commanders, Navigator families, or other independent organisations or individuals; the vast majority belong to the Administratum. All legally operating human ships, whether owned by the Imperium or not, are registered and policed by the Administratum.

## The Warp

The material universe (real space) and the **warp** (Immaterium, Empyrean, Sea of Souls) are
two separate, co-existing realities. The warp is best imagined as an endlessly broad, infinitely
deep sea of raw psychic energy, carrying within it the random thoughts, unfettered emotions, memory fragments, and unshakable beliefs of the beings who live in the material universe.

### Warp travel

A ship enters the warp by activating its warp engines, leaving real space for a corresponding
point in warpspace, where it is carried by the warp's tides and currents before dropping back
into real space several light years from the starting point — a process called a jump or hop, with entry/exit known as a drop,
shift, or translation. Short jumps of up to 4–5 light years — a **calculated jump**, plotted from
observed warp currents without a Navigator — are relatively safe; longer jumps are unpredictable
and dangerous, since the warp's currents shift in complex ways during the voyage. Only
**Navigators**, a mutant strain of humanity able to perceive and steer by the warp's currents
directly, can safely pilot much longer **piloted jumps**, typically up to about 5,000 light
years.

### The Astronomican

A psychic homing beacon centred on Terra, sustained by the continuous mental concentration of
a thousand psykers, detectable only from within the warp. It extends over a roughly spherical
area some 50,000 light years across, growing weaker with distance, and barely reaches the
galaxy's eastern reaches at all. It can be intermittently blocked by **warpstorms** — localised
turbulence in the warp comparable to a terrestrial storm — which can isolate star systems for hundreds of years at a time. A warpstorm also endangers ships travelling nearby: no spacecraft can venture within one and expect to survive, though there are tales of miraculous escapes and of ships thrown tens of thousands of light years off course.

### Warp navigation

Once a ship activates its warp drives it is plunged into a dimension very different from the material universe — imagine warp space as a relatively dense, almost liquid energy devoid of stars, light, and life. Once in the warp it is impossible to detect the spatial movement of warp space; the ship can only carry on blindly, and the longer it stays in warp space, the greater the chance of an unexpected current turning it off course. Navigation is achieved in two ways:

- **Calculated jump:** every warp drive incorporates navigational mechanisms that monitor the ever-shifting movements of that part of the warp corresponding to the ship's position, allowing a course, corrective manoeuvres, and journey time to be calculated on the assumption that the observed warp currents do not change significantly during flight. It is not safe to make a calculated jump of more than four light years at one go.
- **Piloted jump:** relies on a Navigator and the psychic beacon of the Astronomican (centred on Earth, controlled and directed by the psychic power of the Emperor). The Navigator picks up the signal and steers through warp space, compensating for current changes. A piloted jump covers a far longer distance; most are no more than 5,000 light years at a time, but longer jumps have been made.

**A typical voyage:** a cargo ship loads for days or weeks in orbit, then accelerates slowly under its main drives to the rim of the solar system, reaching approximately one percent of light speed. After several weeks it arrives at the **jump-point**, the invisible line around the star system where inter-planetary debris falls below maximum warp density; only beyond it is it safe to activate warp engines. A crew that activates them prematurely would be lucky to be hurled thousands of light years off course, and more likely the ship would be torn apart; re-entering space within the jump-point has the same result. A jump can be up to 5,000 light years; almost two weeks pass on board while over a year passes in the real universe. The ship re-enters real space just beyond the destination's jump-point (it is wise to allow a safe margin) and takes weeks or months to reach the inner planets at about one percent of light speed.

**Warpspace channels:** in theory it is possible to travel anywhere through warp space, but short jumps are always more accurate than longer ones, particularly for large fleets. Long-established, well-charted warp space channels connect star systems and whole regions, providing relatively predictable conduits for the majority of shipping.

### Time displacement

Time passes at different and highly variable rates in real space and in warp space; until a ship finishes its jump, its crew cannot know exactly how long the journey has taken. Time passing in real space is called **real time**; time passing on board a spacecraft is **warp time**. A crew may experience only weeks aboard ship while more than a year passes in real space, and it is impossible to know in advance how much real time a given jump will consume.

### Warp gates and warp portals

- **Warp Gates:** a point in real space linked by a tunnel through the warp to another point in real space, allowing a journey within a fixed time and in perfect safety. Their origin (natural
  or artificial) is unknown; sizes range from ship-scale gates at system edges to small portals
  on planetary surfaces, sometimes disguised or keyed to electrical or psychic signals. Some gates have been artificially enhanced, their entrances delineated by mechanical constructions of unknown function; others are mere black holes in space. All gates are rare, the smaller planetary types extremely so. Many gates appear defective and can dump a ship randomly into warp space; others lead to places that were once stars or planets but are now empty space, into a distant hostile alien star empire, or even beyond the galaxy. Planetary gates are often disguised or respond only to electrical, psychic, or other signals. All space-going races utilise gates when they find them. Because of their strategic value, gates are
  often heavily contested and defended.
- **Warp Portals:** a point where the warp and real space interface, forming a stable entrance into and exit from warpspace. No warp drive need be activated to enter, though any vessel without warp shields is unlikely to survive long. It does not lead to a tunnel — a spacecraft entering is cast to the chance currents of warp space, though careful manoeuvring may allow re-entry to normal space through the same portal from the other side. Its exact nature is not understood. Some aliens use warp portals to travel between warp space and real space, specifically the **Enslavers**, which live within warp space itself. Portals can appear on a planet's surface, may have a physical constituent or be invisible, and there are records of ships with damaged warp drives, trapped in the warp, suddenly finding a portal that let them return to real space.

### Warp creatures (daemons)

Malicious entities native to the warp can attack ships whose **Geller Field** — a protective
warding generated and maintained by Tech-Priests — fails or collapses. Consequences range from
system damage to possession or madness among the crew, or ships found drifting as derelict
"ghost ships" with no crew at all. Some warp entities possess greater sentience and actively
seek to enslave humans or whole communities in order to build a lasting bridge into the material
universe.

## The Stellar Fleets

The whole structure of the Imperium is founded upon the craft that transport its armies and officials across the galaxy; without its fleets the Imperium would soon collapse. Interstellar craft may be privately owned, but most operate on behalf of one of the Imperial organisations. The **Imperial Navy** is the largest, numbering tens of thousands of warships and cargo vessels, plus military spaceports, space stations, mining and factory ships, orbital research stations, and countless unmanned orbital satellites. So vast is the Imperium that the Navy is divided into five main segmentums, each functioning as an independent administrative unit; most higher Navy command comes from the priesthood, principally the Administratum. The Adeptus Terra also maintains its own ships (some permanently on Terra), and a further corps of ships lies under the direct control of the Adeptus Arbites. The Space Marines have their own interstellar transports and battlefleets; each Chapter has enough ships to act as a spacebound home base, and Chapter Masters may commission new ships or capture enemy ones. Ships owned by individuals, corporations, or mercantile families are a fairly small fraction of the total; most noteworthy are the Rogue Traders and the great mercantile families of Navigators, whose ships are mostly ancient family possessions.

Nearly all interstellar ships belong to, or are registered under, the **Administratum**; an
unregistered ship is automatically assumed hostile.

### The Segmentae Majoris

The Imperium is divided into five fleet zones, the **Segmentae Majoris**, each with a
**Segmentum Fortress** housing a **Lord High Admiral**: Segmentum Solar (Mars), Ultima Segmentum
(Kar Duniash), Segmentum Tempestus (Bakka), Segmentum Pacificus (Hydraphur), and Segmentum
Obscura (Cypra Mundi). Each Segmentum Fortress is the headquarters world and base of Imperial Navy operations within its Segmentum, controlled directly by a high-ranking official of the Adeptus Terra known as a Lord High Admiral. Although intended for fleet administration and shipping controls, the Segmentae have evolved into administrative divisions of the Adeptus Terra.

### Sectors and sub-sectors

A typical **sector** covers roughly seven million cubic light years (a cube some 200 light years
to a side); sectors are scattered like island chains across the galaxy, often thousands of light
years apart and linked by well-charted warp routes. **Sub-sectors** typically comprise 2–8 star
systems within a ten-light-year radius. Systems that fall between sub-sectors are called
**inter-sectors**, more commonly known as wilderness zones, forbidden zones, or frontier space.

### Fleet types and ship charters

Each Segmentum maintains its own **Merchant**, **Battle**, and **Civil** fleets — for example, Battlefleet Solar is the warfleet of Segmentum Solar; Merchant Pacificus is the merchant fleet of Segmentum Pacificus; Civilis Tempestus is the civil fleet of Segmentum Tempestus. All interstellar spacecraft are registered with one or more Segmentae fleets; a ship not registered in a Segmentum may only travel there with the special permission of the fleet authorities, and an unidentified, unregistered ship may be assumed hostile and attacked or destroyed.

- **Merchant Fleets:** roughly 90% of all Imperial spacecraft. Ships serve under a **Merchant
  Charter**, sworn as a feudal oath to the fleet authorities on the Emperor's behalf (a captain may not register his vessel until the oath has been sworn and recorded at the Segmentum Fortress for that zone and on Mars), in
  ascending order of prestige and security: **Fleet Charter** (least secure — a captain may be
  reassigned or deprived of command at any time, though he plies fixed routes), **Free Charter**
  (a Free Captain may trade freely within the fleet's Segmentum outside established routes,
  usually in less-populated or unexplored sectors), **Hereditary Charter** (an inheritable route,
  passed to a nominated successor on the captain's death or retirement), and **Hereditary Free
  Charter** — the rarest and most honoured, granting free trade throughout the entire Segmentum
  (or, for the oldest such charters, across several or even all five Segmentae).
- **Civil Fleets:** privately, family-, or cartel-owned ships operating under purchased route
  licences that must typically be renewed every hundred years; sizes range from a single vessel
  to several dozen (the largest known being the Navigator family Redondo, with 47 registered
  ships).
- **Illegal Shipping:** unregistered merchants, smugglers, and pirates, most lacking a Navigator
  and thus limited to short 4-light-year jumps; they typically operate from a hidden supply dump
  near a system's jump point, ferrying cargo there by sub-stellar ships; the dump's location must be kept secret and is often changed every few months. The illegal trader's greatest enemy is treachery, and some Imperial Commanders take a lax attitude to illegal shipping if it suits them. **Pirate ships** operate similarly but with sinister intent, lying in wait just inside a system's jump point to board departing craft and remove cargo before the ship jumps; pirates and unregistered traders often collaborate. **Enemy raiders** are interstellar craft belonging to enemy forces; Imperial Commanders may hire illegal ships to raid a rival's sub-stellar shipping, but attacking Imperial Navy vessels would invite immediate retribution.

### Imperial Navy rank structure

**Lord High Admiral** (one per Segmentum, five total, ranking equally though command of
Battlefleet Solar is considered most prestigious) → **Sector Commander** (an Admiral responsible for a sector's naval operations, based at the Segmentum Fortress and answering to both his Lord High Admiral and the Adeptus prefect in charge of the sector; his ships are divided into patrolling, permanently stationed, and reserve fleets, the reserve usually being stationed at the Segmentum Fortress) → **Group Commanders** (Commodores/Rear Admirals overseeing patrols of
sub-sectors and inter-sectors, typically commanding a sub-sector base and a couple of
squadrons) → **Squadron Leaders** (commanding a squadron, typically three ships) → **Captains**
(individual ships). **Temporary battlefleets** are assembled as needed from ships within roughly
50 light years of a threat (more commonly a dozen or so), taking days to weeks to muster; only
the largest, decades-long wars — such as the ongoing Tyranid Hive Fleet invasion of the galaxy's
south-eastern spiral arm — see the Imperium gather and dispatch truly massive combined fleets.

### Imperial spacecraft

Most Imperial ships are quite old — the majority at least a thousand years old, some as old as the Imperium itself, a full ten thousand years, and a very few claiming a pre-Imperial origin. Ships take many decades to build and, barring accident or destruction, live on practically indefinitely; each is kept running by maintenance crews numbering in the hundreds of thousands,
including onboard factories, forges, and even ore-smelting plants. Ships are powered by
**plasma drives** (for sub-light travel within a system) and **warp drives** (which may only be
activated beyond a system's jump point — premature activation within a system would tear the
ship apart or fling it thousands of light years off course). Roughly one third of a ship's bulk
is devoted to engines and power generation. **Sub-stellar ships** — lacking warp drives and
confined to a single system — include warships, cargo vessels, industrial ships, research vessels (such as the mineral prospector), space stations (built where bases cannot be placed on planets or asteroids), and **beacons** (small stations that broadcast navigational signals, monitor passing
traffic, and serve as emergency lifeboats for crippled ships).

## Imperius Dominatus (Map)

The book's double-page map of the Imperium shows the five Segmentae (Segmentum Solar around Holy Terra, Segmentum Obscurus, Segmentum Pacificus, Segmentum Tempestus, and Ultima Segmentum) with labelled worlds and regions, including Medrengard, the Ruins of Caliban, Cypra Mundi, Port Maw, Mordian, Nemesis Tessera, Cadia, Elysia, Valhalla, Joura, Chiros, Krieg, Luther McIntyre, Ryza, Baal, Bakka, Gryphone IV, Sable, Angelis, Desperation, Calderis, Attila, and Blackreach; regions such as the Ghoul Stars, the Gates of Varl, the Dominion of Storms, the Mordant Zone, and the Hell-Stars of the Garon Nebula; and the Arch Maniac of Calvera, the Arch-arsonist of Charadon, and the Overfiend of Octarius. The legend distinguishes Imperial Worlds, Inquisition Strongholds, Space Marine Homeworlds, Cardinal Worlds, Imperial Navy bases, Imperial Guard Homeworlds, Forge Worlds, and Daemon Worlds.

A **Koronus Expanse** inset lists Port Wander (Calixis Sector, Drusus Marches sub-sector; orbital distance 2.55 AU, gravity 1.02 G, temperature 26 C; planet grade Battlefleet Orbital Station; tithe grade Aptus Non; naval commander Larius Sans) and shows the Calixis Sector, Footfall, and the Maw.

Two quotations accompany the map: Rogue Trader Coros, prior to his ship's disappearance in the Hecaton Rift, says that amongst the herd he is one more dumb beast, but out in the darkness he is his own master; and an excerpt from *A History of the Calixis Sector* tells how, in the last years of Angevin's Crusade, the most holy Saint Drusus reached the far edges of Imperial space, was blocked by vast warp storms, ordered a Watch set upon the worlds along the Rim against the dread Halo Stars, and granted a measure of his Authority and Duty to a chosen few, empowered to travel beyond Imperial space to convert the Heathen, make war upon the Xenos, and return with the bounty of a thousand worlds — who, in the traditions of the Imperium, would be known as Rogue Traders.

## Enemies of the Imperium

The Imperium's battlefleets fight Ork raiders, Eldar pirates, Tyranid Hive Fleets, and other
alien invaders, but also enemies from within: rebellious systems (often the result of decades of
isolation by warpstorms), mutinous fleet commanders carving out their own petty empires, and
historic civil wars — most notoriously the **Horus Heresy**, when Warmaster Horus turned to the
worship of the Ruinous Powers and led half the Imperium's forces against the Emperor himself.
Only Horus's death and the banishment of his followers into the **Eye of Terror** ended the
uprising; even ten thousand years later, a constant vigil is kept around the Eye of Terror, from
which Chaos fleets still launch raids and, occasionally, major incursions into the Imperium.
