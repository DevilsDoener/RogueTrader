# Foreword

*Rogue Trader Core Rulebook, pages 6–10.*

## Foreword by Alan Merrett (book page 6)

The foreword is written by Alan Merrett (Intellectual Property Manager at Games Workshop
at the time of publication) and tells the story of how the name "Rogue Trader" came to be:

- A little over two decades earlier, Games Workshop had published a tabletop miniatures
  game with an unwieldy title that, largely unheralded, launched an entire dystopian
  universe — a grim, dark future in which mankind's fate was under constant threat from
  hostile alien races, monsters, mutants, daemons, and treachery. This was the release of
  *Warhammer 40,000 Rogue Trader*, an event that changed the course of modern tabletop
  wargaming and the destiny of Games Workshop itself.
- The origins reach back almost to the founding of Games Workshop and Citadel Miniatures
  at the end of the 1970s. Citadel was set up to produce fantasy miniatures for the
  growing roleplaying market riding on the success of *Dungeons & Dragons*, but it also
  produced models for many other subjects and interests besides.
- In 1983, Citadel released *Warhammer*, its own standalone tabletop fantasy mass-battle
  game. Its success spurred further attempts to link Citadel's miniatures to wargame
  rules; most failed, but one idea persisted: a spaceship combat game. Rules and a
  background setting were drafted and a name chosen — only matching spaceship miniatures
  were still missing. Even so, an advert for "Citadel's SF Role Play System ROGUE TRADER"
  appeared in the "First Citadel Compendium" in 1983.
- The idea was then broadened to embrace the growing science-fiction model range. Rick
  Priestley took over the project and steered it back toward being a tabletop miniatures
  game rather than a roleplaying game. By 1987 a new rulebook and a modest selection of
  new miniatures were ready for launch.
- Because science fiction was still seen as a gamble next to fantasy, and *Warhammer
  Fantasy Roleplay* was already established, the team decided to rename the SF game
  "Warhammer 40,000" (referencing the setting, and simply because it was a striking
  number). To keep the original working title from disappearing entirely, it was added
  as a subtitle at the last minute — giving *Warhammer 40,000 Rogue Trader* its full name.
- Over the following two decades or so, the *Warhammer 40,000* tabletop game lost its
  subtitle but grew into an international phenomenon, spawning countless miniatures,
  books, novels, computer games, comics, and card and board games set in the same
  universe.
- This rulebook is the second roleplaying game in that line (after *Dark Heresy*) and
  deliberately carries forward the venerable "Rogue Trader" title, in the belief that it
  lives up to the spirit of adventure and imagination of its namesake. Alan Merrett
  thanks Fantasy Flight Games for making it happen.
- Closing line: *"May all your endeavours bear profit!"* — Alan Merrett, Games Workshop.

## Setting prolog (book page 7)

Following the foreword is a single illustrated page (styled as a torn, hand-lettered
scroll) carrying the classic, literary Warhammer 40,000 introductory text about the 41st
Millennium: it describes the Emperor sitting immobile on the Golden Throne of Earth,
described as "a rotting carcass writhing invisibly with power" who is nonetheless master
of a million worlds; his battlefleets crossing the warp guided by the Astronomican; the
Adeptus Astartes, Imperial Guard, Inquisition, and Adeptus Mechanicus holding the line
against aliens, heretics, mutants, and worse; and the reader cast as an explorer aboard a
Rogue Trader ship, empowered by an ancient warrant of trade and a warp-capable vessel to
venture into uncharted voids in search of fortune and glory. The wording itself is not
quoted here beyond the single short phrase above — this is the well-known standard
scene-setting text used across other Warhammer 40,000 publications, and only its content is
summarized.

## Ambition Knows No Bounds – Introduction (book pages 8–9)

The book's actual introduction (headed "Ambition Knows No Bounds") welcomes readers to
*Rogue Trader*, a roleplaying game of exploration, risk, and savage might set in the
decaying far future of Warhammer 40,000:

- In *Rogue Trader*, the Warhammer 40,000 universe can be explored like never before.
  Vast fortunes await at the galaxy's edge for Explorers bold enough to find and claim
  them. Renown and riches reward the bold, while the unwary find only anonymous deaths. A
  Rogue Trader who wrests profit from a dangerous universe through luck, cunning, or sheer
  force of will enjoys something few humans in the 41st Millennium ever know: a life of
  adventure and true freedom. The path there is perilous — foul aliens, pernicious
  raiders, and even other Rogue Traders stand in the way; wits, luck, skill, and courage
  are tested to their limits, and survival only leads to greater challenges and greater
  power.
- **What is a roleplaying game?** In a roleplaying game (RPG), a player creates an alter
  ego — a fictional character acting as their avatar in the game world — and, together
  with friends, guides that character through a series of adventures, much like a crime,
  adventure, or war film that the players actively shape rather than merely watch. Rules
  describe how characters are created and used in play, cover situations such as
  shoot-outs, chases, or week-long investigations, and often call for dice rolls that
  determine success or failure — the element of chance is what makes success feel truly
  sweet. A Game Master (GM), chosen by consensus of the group, does not control a
  character of their own but instead runs the game: presenting the story and situations,
  voicing non-player characters, and shaping encounters and events; the GM is both referee
  and narrator. All other players control the so-called Player Characters (PCs); they
  describe their characters' intentions and actions to the GM, who then rules on success
  or failure and its effect on the story.
- **What do you need to play?** This rulebook; three or more people; a quiet place to play
  (a table is ideal); three to four hours of playing time; some ten-sided dice (at least
  two per player, ideally in different colours); paper; pencils and an eraser; optionally
  snacks and drinks. Ten-sided dice can be found in the same shop as the book or through
  online retailers; the character sheet at the back of the book may be photocopied for the
  playing group (or downloaded for free from Fantasy Flight Games). Suitable miniatures
  (such as from Games Workshop's Citadel Warhammer 40,000 range) can help visualize
  combat, but are not required.

## What's in this Book? – Chapter overview (book pages 9–10)

Per the introduction, the book is divided into fifteen chapters plus the introduction
itself. The book's own short description of each chapter:

- **Introduction** – the section just read; an overview of *Rogue Trader* and of
  roleplaying games in general.
- **Chapter I: Character Creation** – walks step by step through creating your own
  character.
- **Chapter II: Career Paths** – Career Paths are a building block of the character; they
  describe both the character's life before becoming an Explorer and their standing
  within the Rogue Trader dynasty. The chapter covers all Career Paths and how the
  character develops over the course of play.
- **Chapter III: Skills** – from Inquiry to Tech-Use, every character has learned Skills;
  the chapter describes them in detail.
- **Chapter IV: Talents** – every character also has special abilities such as "Bulging
  Biceps" or "Unshakeable Faith"; this chapter describes those Talents.
- **Chapter V: Armoury** – describes the tools, equipment, weapons, and armour an Explorer
  may use or encounter.
- **Chapter VI: Psychic Powers** – from astro-telepathy to the mysteries of the Emperor's
  Tarot, the supernatural powers of the mind can be as rewarding as they are dangerous;
  the chapter explains how an Astropath's powers differ from those of other psychically
  gifted individuals, and what happens when those powers go wrong.
- **Chapter VII: Navigator Powers** – Navigators are a special kind of mutant with a third
  eye able to perceive the immaterial Warp; the chapter covers their powers and mutations.
- **Chapter VIII: Starships** – a Rogue Trader never travels the void alone; the strength
  of their dynasty depends on the might of their starship.
- **Chapter IX: Playing the Game** – explains how to defend against a variety of alien
  foes, along with Skill Tests, using Fate Points, and other rules-related actions.
- **Chapter X: The Game Master** – the GM has a special role; this chapter offers advice on
  fulfilling it well, including rules for Corruption and Experience.
- **Chapter XI: The Imperium** – describes the Imperium of Man in the 41st Millennium in
  all its decaying, war-torn glory.
- **Chapter XII: Rogue Traders** – players take on the role of an Explorer, either as a
  Rogue Trader themselves or as a trusted ally/counsellor who accompanies them on their
  voyages; the chapter provides key background on these merchant princes.
- **Chapter XIII: The Koronus Expanse** – the Koronus Expanse as the game's central
  setting; an overview of the region plus detailed information on notable locations such
  as Port Wander, the Maw, and Footfall — material for GM campaigns.
- **Chapter XIV: Adversaries & Aliens** – the galaxy is full of deadly creatures; background
  and game statistics for various adversaries of the Koronus Expanse.
- **Chapter XV: Into the Maw** – an introductory adventure GMs can use to launch their
  Rogue Trader campaigns; explicitly for GMs only, not for players.

Following this is the "Dice" section with the basic rules for `1d10`, `2d10`, percentage
rolls (`d%`/`1d100`), and `1d5`, with worked examples (a fragmentation grenade dealing
`2d10` Explosive Damage; `1d5` knockback distance).
