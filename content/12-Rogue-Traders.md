## Rogue Traders — Basics

*"The armchair-admirals at the Navy academies will have it that combat in space is a predictable, scientific affair... I have hurled my warship at my foe as if it were a torpedo, and defeated him utterly."* — Rogue Trader Lucien Gherrit of the Clan Arcadius, at the victory banquet aboard the *Wotan* following the defeat of the traitor 88th Flotilla.

The million or more inhabited worlds the Imperium controls are but a tiny fraction of the galactic whole; beyond lie the fringes and halo zones, where the Astronomican does not reach and the only human settlers are renegades or pioneering groups whose ancestors were forgotten millennia ago.


Rogue Traders are free-ranging Imperial agents, licensed and often equipped by the Adeptus Terra, acting in the far regions beyond the reach of the Astronomican and in as-yet unvisited regions within its reach. Rogue Traders have even attempted to cross the voids of interstellar space, where even Astropaths' powers of communication are useless and whether such missions have succeeded is unknown.
They decide autonomously how to deal with alien cultures: annihilating dangerous species, making
contact with useful races, or plundering worlds rich in technology or minerals. If merely rich in technology or minerals, a planet may be plundered, and the Rogue Trader will return to Terra laden with alien artefacts, rare and precious minerals, and undreamed-of technology. This requires
substantial resources (ships, troops, technicians, settlers); his total responsibility may extend to dozens of spacecraft, often huge lumbering cargo vessels crammed with a small army, a full crew of technicians, and volunteer settlers. Most important are the fighting troops. Many come from high positions within
the Adeptus Terra, the Imperial Navy, the Imperial Guard, or the Inquisition; some are influential
civilians (particularly Navigators). Many Rogue Traders dispute the right of the Imperium to exercise authority over them, and once back within the Imperium they may come into conflict with the Adeptus Terra or the Inquisition; many in the Inquisition would take to marking the Rogue Trader out as a heretic. Rogue Traders are often regarded as outsiders — some pious,
some little more than pirates and scoundrels; usually charismatic, extravagantly dressed, with
exotic equipment (Jokaero digital weapons — miniature lasers and needlers worn as rings but as powerful as a pistol —, alien phase weapons that bypass armour, Ha'Kaidan neuro-stars, and the like), often with a weapon from military service such as a Naval cutlass or officer's sabre. They usually return to Imperial space every few years to unload their wares, re-supply, recruit, and rest.

## The Warrant of Trade

The Warrant of Trade is an actual, highly venerated document — comparable to an Inquisitor's rosette
— signed/sealed by the mightiest people in the Imperium (some old lineages claim it was signed by the
Emperor Himself). It is rarely carried openly, usually kept in stasis vaults. The book's sidebar "And Your Name Is, Sir?" notes that few Rogue Traders openly bear it; the Imperium is a feudal society in which few would dare question their so-called betters, and it is normally better to assume that the fellow with the fancy robes, hundreds-strong retinue, and large fleet in geo-stationary orbit over your palace is a duly appointed representative of the Imperium. The Warrant of Trade grants:

- Permission to travel beyond the borders of the Imperium and to negotiate there with any culture or
  being — with the full authority of the Senatorum Imperialis.
- Rank equal to Imperial Commanders, Inquisitors, and Space Marine Chapter Masters, with the power to deal with such peers as equals and to call upon what aid can be negotiated.
- The right to claim worlds and privileges by conquest; beyond the Fringes, the Rogue Trader defines
  the power structures themselves ("speaks with the authority of the Emperor").

**Backing:** Military resources come either directly from a high imperial office or from investors
who expect a share of the profits — some Rogue Traders "forget" this debt once they are outside the
Imperium (substantial rewards may be placed for their subsequent capture).

## Roots (Origins of Rogue Traders)

The Warrant issues from the highest of authorities, usually the office of the High Lords of Terra (and by extension the Emperor). Within the Imperium Rogue Traders move within the established power structures; outside it they define those structures themselves. Warrants are hereditary and passed down for as long as heirs exist; many lines die out and more are created.

- **Imperial Navy:** the most common origin — experienced captains/admirals, often "mavericks" with
  unconventional tactics; maintain contacts in their former branch of service; often dress in Navy
  dress uniform mixed with alien jewellery/weapons (a formal dress coat like a high admiral's, complete with medals and gold-braided epaulettes, yet perhaps hair braided in the fashion of a feral world tribe or facial tattoos in the style of a hive gang); many keep a network of contacts across the sector in which they served.
- **Imperial Guard:** command large ground forces, use the fleet for invasions; must recruit troops
  from conquered worlds when losses occur (sometimes feudal service obligation, sometimes coercion,
  rarely even alien mercenaries); practical rather than decorative armament.
- **Administratum:** Machiavellian power politicians who play subordinates off against one another;
  unassuming clothing, but always armed/protected (digital lasers, force fields).
- **Merchants:** trade and war are one and the same to many merchant leaders; the most skilled negotiators, equally capable as field commanders and at the
  bargaining table; opulent clothing with hidden weapons/sensors; ships like flying palaces.
- **Imperial Commanders:** deposed/dispossessed governors (e.g. after an alien invasion of their
  world), tasked with conquering a new world; their followers fight with particular tenacity
  (driven by loss); often preserve the culture of their lost homeworld to an exaggerated degree.
- **Inquisition:** rare (the servants of Inquisitors may also end up as Rogue Traders), usually the result of internal politics (a Warrant as an "honourable"
  alternative to something worse); usually from the Ordo Xenos, retain considerable influence.

### Reasons for Granting a Warrant

- **Military Service:** reward for successful field commanders/admirals after decades of crusades —
  an outlet for their restless energy.
- **Political Expediency:** crusade leaders who want to keep conquered regions for themselves are
  kept busy with ever-new conquest targets (a Warrant), until their strength is exhausted.
- **Self-Promotion:** a Warrant obtained through bribery/called-in favours, as the personal
  ambition of a power-hungry official.
- **Virtual Exile:** a Warrant as an "honour" with which political rivals dispose of an opponent grown
  too powerful — the person concerned cannot refuse; some return decades later seeking revenge.

**Scions of the Rogue Trader Houses (sidebar):** Descendants of old lineages must often first "serve their
time" (Imperial Navy, Guard, Administratum) before being considered worthy to take up the succession.

## Temperament (Character Types)

- **The Scoundrel:** a charming impostor, avoids violence as a first choice, takes great risks.
- **The Merchant Prince:** avoids military action as a waste of resources, prefers exclusive trade
  agreements; nonetheless defends his investments resolutely.
- **The Explorer:** restlessly curious and highly intelligent, throws himself into the most dangerous environments, happily
  delegates the administrative duties of his rank to subordinates.
- **The Missionary:** believes in the literal truth of the Imperial Creed and spreads it, often forcibly; sees xenos as something to be suppressed, often travels with a Ministorum force (often granted a Warrant to conquer regions that resisted the Missionarius Galaxia).
- **The Diplomat:** a pragmatist who negotiates even with xenos (e.g. Eldar), but knows exactly when
  words fail and weapons must speak.
- **The Psychopath:** borderline insane, judges by his own broken morality, likes to adopt grandiose
  titles (e.g. "Grand Conqueror"), often eventually vanishes without a trace.

## Trappings of Power

Along with the warrant to act outside the Imperium's laws, the Warrant of Trade often comes with many trappings. A newly appointed Rogue Trader who does not already possess a spaceship may be given a flagship, often a cruiser (capital ships take decades, even centuries, to construct and are not squandered). Alongside it he may be accompanied by frigates and other escorts, as well as cargo vessels, tenders, processing vessels, and other auxiliaries; if he is not versed in the skills needed to command a fleet and has appointed no servant who is, the authorities will find one qualified to handle the day-to-day running of the flotilla.
Entire Imperial Guard regiments may be seconded to him along with auxiliary units and advisors; though considered temporary attachments, in reality they are likely to prove permanent, and attached units often wear a tabard or sash in the colours and heraldry of their new lord. He may also receive a vast staff of bureaucrats, administrators, clerks, artisans, and advisors, and entire populations of colonists (volunteers or otherwise), and in some cases installs relatives or trusted followers as a new ruling class of a colony.

**Rogue Trader heraldry (sidebar):** on receipt of the Warrant many Rogue Traders are issued a personal banner, borne at official ceremonies and blessed by the highest officers of the Ministorum; some lock it away in a stasis vault on the Forge World of Belacane, famed for its stasis field technology. Household troops and vessels are often bedecked in the symbols of their liege.

## Conditions (Stipulations of the Warrant)

A Rogue Trader fleet is a significant military force, and the Warrant will often come with conditions attached; it would be a sure sign of virtual exile if none were attached, the authorities clearly hoping the recipient will get himself killed somewhere beyond their concern. Conditions invariably involve some form of military service or intervention. The most common types:

- **Trooping the Colours:** a mandatory visit to a world that has been isolated or unvisited by any authority for a generation, or whose tithes have been short or slow, as a demonstrative "state visit" (pomp, honour guard, banquets and high-level talks, acting as a diplomat or ambassador of the Imperium) to demand tribute/loyalty. Governors who resist or show reluctance may require a punitive strike.
- **Punitive Strike:** where a flag-waving mission has failed or a world has openly rejected Imperial rule, a hard-hitting show of force — a lance strike from orbit at key infrastructure, or at a location the Imperial Commander values (a luxurious winter palace, a rural hunting lodge). If that is insufficient a protracted campaign may be needed, and if the remit extends to it, dethroning the governor, whose more compliant replacement is chosen according to the political system that put him in power.
- **Settlement:** colonisation of a new world in the name of the God-Emperor, based on an Adeptus Mechanicus Explorator report that may have spent years or centuries in the Administratum's data-mills; on arrival the world may prove uninhabitable, devastated, claimed by aliens, or incorrectly categorised, leaving a colony ship and some thousands of settlers to dispose of. Very few colonies established under such circumstances last more than a year.
- **Reclamation:** bringing back into the fold, by overt military power, worlds lost to rebellion, alien invasion, or natural disaster. The Rogue Trader operates at the level of a Lord Militant leading a crusade, with additional forces seconded (sometimes Space Marines of the Adeptus Astartes, usually for a short time). He is often a figurehead, not in direct command, with an administrative body formed, often titled a "crusader council", in which no Imperial Navy Admiral, Imperial Guard General, or Space Marine Master would agree to squander his own resources.
- **Exploration:** exploration of a specific region at the behest of the Imperium's authorities — often without the exploitation rights that are otherwise standard, which causes resentment among Rogue Traders. The mission may follow fragmentary knowledge of natural resources, isolated human worlds, alien tomb worlds pre-dating Man, esoteric stellar phenomena, or planet-sized super-weapons, and is often accompanied by senior members of the Adeptus Mechanicus or the Ordo Xenos of the Inquisition.

**Compliance with the Terms of the Warrant:** newly appointed Rogue Traders may be tempted to sail off into the unknown without meeting their obligations, but the Imperium always puts at least one contingency plan in place. The most common method is to have very powerful representatives of the Imperium on hand (an Administratum prefect, an agent of a more obscure division, even the Inquisition), sometimes accompanied by one or more Space Marine vessels. Compliance may also be assured by subtler means, such as the promise of further powers or additional assets at various stages of a voyage, or by placing agents of the Imperium among the ship's crew; actual assassins are sometimes emplaced aboard the flagship as insurance against non-compliance or betrayal.

## Lineage (Descent and Succession)

There are two broad classifications of Rogue Trader: those personally granted the Warrant of Trade, and those who have inherited it from a forebear. A granted Warrant can establish a powerful noble line that may grow to equal the most ancient Navigator clans and merchant houses. Many scions of these houses hold the title Rogue Trader and wield the authority of the Warrant, yet in the main these powers reside with the line's titular head, who bears the duty of maintaining the Warrant and the line's continued existence. Warrants are hereditary and are passed from generation to generation; when some lineages die out, new ones arise. Some Houses
are torn by internal power struggles over succession (up to and including assassination), but usually
maintain an outward appearance of propriety so as not to show weakness to rivals.

## Revocation and Forfeiture

Considering the enormous freedom granted by the Warrant, it takes a great deal for a Rogue Trader's actions to be considered beyond the scope of his powers; the authorities often overlook even the most questionable actions so long as the Imperium's best interests are served. Yet a Warrant can be revoked (revocation means the effective death of a Rogue Trader line, its powers and privileges lost for all time) if a Rogue Trader oversteps the limits of his power:

- **Empire Building:** building up one's own small realm is permissible in itself (as long as tithes
  are paid) — but if hubris and greed lead to taking total power and rejecting the Imperium, a crusade fleet, sometimes led by a newly appointed Rogue Trader, is dispatched to crush the traitor.
- **Acting Against the Imperium:** becoming embroiled in local wars/trade feuds that inadvertently
  work against imperial interests.
- **Consorting with the Xenos Terribilus:** contact with xenos systems marked as "perdita"
  (forbidden) — entire star systems are declared perdita, their outer limits marked by sentinel-buoys that transmit warnings, on the authority of the Segmentum Fortress or even the Inquisition. Contact is taboo even for Rogue Traders, and those who survive it have later faced the wrath of the Inquisition and forfeiture of the Warrant.
- **Overstepping the Bounds:** a Rogue Trader fleet returning unannounced can be misinterpreted by
  the Imperial Navy as a threat and lead to armed confrontation. A Rogue Trader flotilla is a sizeable military force with enough firepower to raze many Imperial worlds; a single vessel returning rarely causes concern, an entire fleet often will. Such incidents might be cause to forfeit the Warrant, if the Rogue Trader is still alive at the end.

## Rewards

The greatest rewards are wealth (through trade or conquest, often both at once), fame beyond one's
own homeworld, and above all the chance to establish a lineage, with oneself as primogenitor, of a noble house that will endure throughout the ages. Few men in the 41st millennium can hope their name will be spoken of beyond the limits of their birth world or the span of their own lifetime.

## Sidebar Accounts

- **The Battle of Jade Reach:** in 691.M41 the Imperial Navy cruiser *Wrote for Luck* was on routine patrol near the Koronus Expanse with a squadron of escorts. Near the Drusus Marches at the trailing edge of the Calixis Sector, it answered an Astropathic distress call from an Adeptus Mechanicus survey vessel and met a significant Ork incursion; its master called for aid, three entire Rogue Trader flotillas answered, and three days later the combined force crushed the Ork fleet. It was the first recorded Navy/Rogue Trader cooperation of its kind in over two centuries; the after-action reports are required reading for Imperial Navy officer cadets in the Calixis Sector.
- **Paradise Lost, Found, then Lost Again:** in 298.M41 Rogue Trader Salgut Falkin entered the Koronus Expanse with 30,000 surplus colonists from the Hive World of Clove in the Calixis Sector, and left them on a verdant tropical world. Twelve years later, his fleet decimated by encounters with the Eldar, he found a thriving colony, New Clove, and set himself up as its Imperial Commander; the Eldar, who had tracked him and found their long-lost Maiden World, demanded the humans' withdrawal, invaded, and ejected them, and Falkin and his remaining flotilla disappeared into the Expanse.
- **The Folly of the Prefect Majoris of Dalthus:** Rogue Trader Lady Sun Lee received her Warrant in 538.M41 on the condition that she put in at the mining world of Dalthus in the Hazeroth Abyss, whose ruling mining interests were late paying dues. She broadcast the glory of the Imperium for the week her flotilla took to reach the inner system, the panicked Prefect Majoris raised a cargo vessel's worth of the world's trinkets in tithes, and after three weeks she departed with the gifts, reminding him to pay the tithe when the Emperor's counting boat arrived in a year; the prefect disappeared, unable to meet it.
- **The Admiral Militant:** Rogue Trader Admiral Dallactarius IV was drawn into the pacification of the world of 47 Kapella in the Drusus Marches in 482.M41 while en route to the Koronus Expanse; a twelve-year war of attrition reduced his forces to a few dozen household guard. His last recorded sighting was at the Battle of Hill 44427, leading an assault on an enemy bunker; he was declared dead a decade later, though his household maintains he lives, and he never reached the Expanse.
- **The Fate of Colony XK-119:** in the closing months of the 40th millennium Rogue Trader Kinker Drub received the Warrant and the obligation to reclaim Colony XK-119, a small facility on a satellite of an unnamed gas giant several weeks' travel into the Koronus Expanse, retaken by an alien race. With a composite Adeptus Astartes task force of a dozen squads from as many Chapters attached, Drub crushed the alien fleet, found the colony built in the ruins of an alien city and colonists tampering with alien technology, and set out to hunt the remaining xenos; Drub and his Astartes were never seen by any human again.
- **One Man's Junk Is Another Man's Fortune:** Rogue Trader Baxilik Kim was granted his Warrant on condition that an Adeptus Mechanicus Explorator fleet accompany him for the first three years. The Explorators sought only pre-Imperium human artefacts and rejected alien ones, which Kim collected, sold to collectors of proscribed items on his return, and then went back to the Koronus Expanse with private backing to resume harvesting the explorators' cast-offs.
- **The Reward of Galfit's Treachery:** Lord Galfit of the Line of Magnum served five decades on the fleet general's staff and made powerful enemies in the Calixis Sector high command, who engineered his Warrant expecting him to overstep his authority. Three years after entering the Koronus Expanse he claimed a newly discovered world for himself and quietly disposed of the Imperial servants with him; a decade later an Adeptus Mechanicus survey fleet found his vessel in orbit crewed only by shrivelled corpses, Galfit seated upright on the command throne, killed by poison released into the environmental systems. A single lifeboat had been jettisoned; no clue to the assassin was found.
