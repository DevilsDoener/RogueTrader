## Introduction

The Koronus Expanse is the name given by the imperial authorities to a dangerous, unexplored region
of the Halo Stars beyond the Calixis Sector. Access is via the Koronus Passage, a treacherous but
navigable route through the Great Warp Storms that block the way to the Halo Stars beyond the
waystation of Port Wander. Like the Calixis Sector itself, the Expanse was untouched by the
God-Emperor's campaigns millennia ago — it is a realm of terrifying xenos, unimaginable treasures,
heathen human worlds, and the echoes of ancient corruption.

In the 41st Millennium, the Koronus Expanse is a scattered, only partially explored region with a
handful of young colonies and vast, largely untapped natural wealth. Rogue Traders compete
ruthlessly for known resources, while tentative imperial colonisation follows in their wake. Pirates
and servants of the Dark Gods have likewise seeped into the Expanse, eager to destroy the work of the
God-Emperor's faithful and to live off what they can tear from the dead — while the Expanse itself
holds secrets and native inhabitants that are no less dangerous.

For centuries, Rogue Traders have ventured into the Koronus Expanse despite great dangers and the
treacherous warp passage, yet their efforts have barely scratched its secrets. Bases have been
established near the few reasonably stable warp routes into the region: resource-rich worlds are
exploited, xenos ruins are excavated, trade envoys meet with heathen princes, colonies are attempted
on protected worlds. This has been enough to bring wealth and fame to a lucky few — and to turn the
rest into corpses. The gateway to the Expanse is littered with wrecks and stories of the vanished.
Beyond these human conflicts lie truly dark and dangerous voids, full of rumours of horrors,
undiscovered stars, and human worlds that have never known the God-Emperor. There are no established
warp routes, no safe paths through the swirling Empyrean — here lurk the terrible Orks, the
treacherous Eldar, and strange ruins beneath the light of dying stars.

The warp of the Koronus Expanse is largely treacherous and unknown; the partially explored regions
are islands of imperial activity amid a vastness of danger and mystery. Navigating it differs
fundamentally from travelling the established warp routes of the Calixis Sector. Most of the
Koronus Expanse is known to the Imperium only through legend, revelation, and hearsay. The Rogue
Trader who ventures into this unknown risks his soul on the talent of his Navigator and the quality
of the scant information he has obtained from those who came before him.

Beyond the partially explored regions, past depths full of pirates and dark xenos, lie many worlds
and strange phenomena that exist only as the stuff of dark legend. Some of these unknown stars have
been visited by individual Rogue Traders, whose stories are dismissed as lies by rivals; others are
known only from dubious and apocryphal sources, such as the Thulean data-vaults recovered from the
voids of Dolorium in 741.M41, or the notorious prophetic visions of the Seven Witches of Footfall.
Further contradictory fragments of knowledge come from even less reliable sources — muffled
astropathic whispers in the eddies of the warp, the falsehoods of the Eldar, or ancient myths of
heathen worlds that have fallen to darkness for millennia. Such regions bear, on old charts, only the
inscription "Here be monsters".

> **The Calixis Sector (Sidebar):** The Calixis Sector lies in the Segmentum Obscurus and is a
> relatively young sector, founded from worlds conquered during the Angevin Crusade (which ended in
> the early centuries of the 39th Millennium). It is governed by Sector Governor Marius Hax from his
> seat on the central hive world Scintilla; his realms stretch from the coreward worlds of the
> Markayn Reaches, the Josian Reach, and the Hazeroth Abyss to the Malfian Sub-sector and the Drusus
> Marches, which border the unexplored vastness of the Halo Stars and from which Rogue Traders and
> Explorators set out into those haunted, uncharted worlds. The Calixis Sector is the setting of
> *Dark Heresy*; further information can be found in the *Dark Heresy* rulebook and its supplements.

## The Great Warp Storms of the Halo Margins

Along the rimward edge of the Calixis Sector, immense warp storms have raged since ancient times,
blocking passage into the Halo Stars. This wall of storms has claimed many lives among those who
sought new paths into the unexplored vastness. They ebb and surge with a savagery and malice that can
lure even the most experienced Navigator to ruin in a sudden, tearing tidal wave. Although one speaks
of "the" warp storms, it is in fact a collection of individual storms that collide, overlap, and
occasionally consume one another. Passage is all but impossible outside a few stable routes that run
like narrow, calm threads through the storms.

Some storms have persisted for so long that they have earned an infamous name among Navigators and
travellers of the Sea of Souls. The most famous are the **Void Dancer's Roil** and the **Screaming
Vortex**, which frame the stable passage known as **the Maw** like demonic guardians at the gates to
a waiting hell.

- **Void Dancer's Roil:** a mass of subtly gliding currents and beguiling warp streams that can lead
  astray even the most experienced Navigator and tear apart the hulls of ships that dare to ride its
  tides. Ghost ships are said to have been sighted adrift in the Void Dancer's Roil, their hulls pale
  as chiselled stone.
- **Screaming Vortex:** a seething mass of warp turbulence and whirlpools that rage unceasingly
  against one another, producing a constant psychic howl perceptible to warp-sensitive beings — hence
  the name. Its character resembles that of a raging beast whose teeth grind in an endless search for
  food; void-born say it has swallowed entire fleets.
- **Deathveil:** a smaller, permanent warp disturbance near the two main storms — a strange cascade of
  unnatural beauty that draws the mind of the unwary into its soft, silent embrace, from which there
  is no return.
- **Whispering Storm:** a pocket of stillness amid the fury, where the fingers of the dead caress the
  hull and forgotten voices whisper with crackling static in the minds of the living.

These and many other warp phenomena swirl together to form a wall in the Sea of Souls that cannot be
crossed except via a few stable warp routes. The largest and most easily navigable of these routes is
the **Koronus Passage**, more commonly called "the Maw", which leads through the Great Warp Storms
into the dark unknown of the Koronus Expanse.

## Port Wander: Gateway to the Expanse

Port Wander is a void station at the very edge of the Drusus Marches of the Calixis Sector and is
rightly regarded as the last bastion of the Emperor's rule this side of the Koronus Expanse. A place
of desperate hopes and vain dreams: Port Wander teems with a transient population of traders, spies,
trade factors, pilgrims, and missionaries, among whom Administratum functionaries and servants of
the Mechanicus move — all feeding off the wealth that flows from the realms beyond the warp storms
into the Koronus Expanse. All who travel into the Koronus Expanse share one common experience: they
pass through Port Wander, be they the pious who carry the Emperor's light into the darkness, or
black-hearted monsters seeking the keys to forbidden dreams.

Port Wander was founded by the Imperial Navy in 917.M40 as a staging area to investigate numerous
ship losses at the edges of the Drusus Marches. With the discovery of the Koronus Passage at the end
of the 40th Millennium, the station grew in importance thanks to its proximity to the passage; its
original role as a military base fell into oblivion, and Port Wander became the waystation for all
who dared the passage into the Koronus Expanse.

### The Structure of Port Wander

Seen from a distance, the port resembles a small cityscape with towering spires and cathedrals and a
huge Aquila as a mark of fidelity to the God-Emperor. Numerous long piers with spider-like docking
apparatus jut out from its sides, and smaller shuttle bays are scattered across the station. Deep
fissures along the station reveal the slow layers of decades of expansion and the patchwork nature of
many of its components — including the almost intact hull of the *Solstice Imperialis*, welded to one
side of the station centuries ago. On the underside are the main repair yards with sealed dry-dock
bays for major work; along the keel lie elaborate, mysterious installations and probes with which the
Adeptus Mechanicus studies the nature of the warp passage through the Great Warp Storms.

Spread across the full length and breadth of the station are lance emplacements, weapon batteries,
torpedo launchers, and void shield generators, all kept at maximum efficiency and ready for use. In
addition to these static defences, squadrons of heavily armed monitor craft are stationed at Port
Wander, and warships of the Battlefleet Calixis patrol past regularly — Port Wander is a guarded
gate. At any given time, at least half a dozen Rogue Traders lie at anchor at the station, along with
countless trading vessels, huge transports, and numerous smaller craft. Further ships haul off fuel
gases from the system's two larger planets, or minerals and ice from its more distant reaches.
Numerous asteroids within the same system have been converted into fuel depots, palatial estates,
makeshift habitats, warehouses, shipyards, and other facilities.

### The Rubycon II System

Rubycon II is the star system around a swollen red star, nearly a thousand times larger than Sol,
burning deep crimson. It is a dead system of two large gas giants with pale surfaces, across which
vast storms sweep — the **Ruby Brothers**. Between the two planets lies a broad, dense asteroid
field, presumably the remains of a smaller sibling planet whose destruction lies long in the past.
This shattered asteroid belt is a popular hiding place for anyone wishing to avoid imperial
attention, and a meeting point for shady agencies who nonetheless dare the Koronus Passage into the
Expanse. Beyond the orbits of the two twin planets lies Port Wander itself, embedded in a cluster of
asteroids, many of which have been converted into installations, shipyards, living quarters, research
stations, repair docks, and warehouses. Beyond this island of life lie the comets — uncounted hundreds
of thousands of chunks of ice and soot — and finally true vacuum, quiet but for the silent drone of
powerful plasma drives from passing ships.

## The Koronus Passage: "The Maw"

The Koronus Passage is a stable but dangerous warp route through the Great Warp Storms between the
Calixis Sector and the Koronus Expanse. It was discovered in the distant past by a Magos Explorator
of the Machine Cult, lost for millennia, and rediscovered at the end of the 40th Millennium by the
Rogue Trader **Purity Lathimon**. The passage connects Port Wander and the Drusus Marches with the
great star Furibundus and the void colony Footfall — and beyond Footfall beckons the Koronus Expanse.
Superstitious voidfarers call the route "the Maw" — to them, the Maw is a monster of warp storms,
cunning and malicious, whose crushing gullet must be passed by anyone who would advance into the
Koronus Expanse. Many have died in the attempt, and crossing the Maw remains gruelling despite
centuries of accumulated experience among Navigators and Rogue Traders.

The possibility of a passage through the Great Warp Storms was first recorded by **Abenicus**, an
insane Navigator of House Benetek, and has since gripped the hearts and minds of Rogue Traders. Over
time, cautious forays into what would later be called "the Maw" laid the foundation for the
**Stations of Passage**, which marked the way through the Maw. During the Mistaken Age in the early
41st Millennium, Navigators learned to "read" the Maw and its moods — to interpret the signs in the
warp and avoid the sudden vortices that had destroyed earlier explorers. The Stations of Passage fell
into obscurity, but have since served as refuges during unexpected warp upheavals and as covert
meeting places for scheming Rogue Traders. Today the Maw is a rite of passage for Rogue Traders of
the Calixis Sector: a Lord-Captain may bear the most magnificent Warrant of Trade Port Wander has
ever seen — yet until he has crossed the Maw and glimpsed the blazing light of Furibundus at its far
end, he is regarded by his peers as little better than a common Free Trader.

### The Stations of Passage

The Stations of Passage are places in realspace where ships can safely drop out of the warp while
crossing the Maw. Rogue Traders avoid some stations out of tradition; some retain a bad reputation to
this day. Many stations are simple voids, energised gas streams, or the edges of dead systems —
others are more fascinating:

- **The Temple:** a dead system at the outermost edges of the Great Warp Storms, a short warp jump
  from Port Wander. A strangely symmetrical, almost artificial-looking system of perfectly spherical
  rocks several hundred metres in diameter, orbiting a star extinguished into dense matter — nothing
  else, no dust, no gas streams, no worlds. Named after the "The Temple" card of the Sybillan
  Emperor's Tarot, which symbolises the beginning of a blessed undertaking, but also the leaving of a
  known sanctuary.
- **The Witch-Cursed World:** a large, solitary rocky planet with no star of its own, drifting in deep
  vacuum like a jewel discarded by a deity, its atmosphere frozen into glaciers. Whether it is an
  exile from an earlier system or arose in the lightless void is unknown. No crew endures long in this
  ill-omened place.
- **The Battleground:** according to legend, already an old wreck-field before the Rogue Traders
  Trame and Ettimus Lathimon brought about each other's destruction here in a dispute over the Ragged
  Worlds. A vast, slowly drifting debris field, largely of imperial origin.
- **The Hermitage:** a tiny Ministorum sect paid Trame Lathimon enormous sums during the Mistaken Age
  to carry them and their void station, the Hermitage, into the Halo Stars. Lathimon eventually
  dropped the hermits off at the Conclave (then a station where ships regularly met to trade rumours
  of the beyond). The outer sections of the Hermitage have collapsed and lie open to vacuum, yet some
  hermits still live there. Rogue Traders have long left coded messages for allies at agreed spots on
  the station; the richly decorated central nave occasionally serves as neutral ground for covert
  trade, before an altar of blue stone, a platinum Aquila, and immaculately preserved banners
  depicting the victories of Saint Drusus.

### Furibundus

Furibundus marks the far side of the Great Warp Storms and the beginning of the Koronus Expanse — an
immense, primordial mass of a star, far brighter and more energetic than a star ought to be. Its
fires rage so fiercely that huge blisters of burning plasma swell its shape, as though monsters were
fighting within it; its outer shell constantly tears and undulates, hurling vast masses of flame into
the nearby voids. Furibundus is surrounded by shells of expanding residual gas, remnants of its
outermost stellar layers.

Imperial presence around Furibundus is concentrated in two places: the stone void settlement
**Footfall** and the Adeptus Mechanicus temple **Altar-Templum Calixis-Ext-17**. Footfall's ornate,
anchored structures orbit the star at a distance sufficient to be safe from the lethal plasma bands.
Further in, near the primordial roar of Furibundus, lies a planet in a racing orbit, repeatedly burnt
to molten rock — visible only through its shadow cast on the passing plasma bands. This is
Altar-Templum Calixis-Ext-17: a heavily shielded fortress-chapel in which tech-priests study the
secrets of this raging star.

### Footfall

Once known as "Dewain's Footfall" and now simply called **Footfall**, this settlement is an anchored
network of hundreds of stone structures floating in the voids around Furibundus — a mass of
buttressed temples and plasma-scoured shrines with towers jutting in every direction, connected by
stone tunnels, arched bridges, and massive steel chains. At its centre stands a huge statue of the
God-Emperor, anchored in place and larger than many a warship. Some of Footfall's structures could
just as easily stand on a planetary surface; others, spiralling labyrinths of unsupported stone,
would collapse under gravity's weight. Parts of Footfall are weightless, other parts have wavering
artificial gravity — the few structures with their own stable gravity generators are highly coveted
prizes, fought over by powerful factions and changing hands with alarming regularity, over the
corpses of their previous owners.

Over the centuries, Footfall has become a den of villainy and intrigue — the descendants of the
original stonemasons and Rogue Trader vassals have long since been outnumbered by less honourable
newcomers. Here religious fanatics rub shoulders with assassins, spies, fugitives from imperial
justice, narco-tribesmen, boisterous ship crews on shore leave, and a broad range of shady traders.
Beneath this lurks an even darker world: heretechs, cultists, unrestrained criminals, unsanctioned
psykers, and worse. Here almost anything can be traded — from a starship to a human soul — for the
right price. For precisely these reasons, many of the great powers of the Calixis Sector maintain
secret agents in Footfall: the Administratum, the Battlefleet Calixis, the Great Houses of the
imperial nobility, the disciples of the Dark Gods, the Ministorum — and perhaps even the Holy Ordos
of the Inquisition. For Rogue Traders and their agents, Footfall also serves as a place to learn more
about rivals, use illegal services, send crew on shore leave, find new recruits, take part in deadly
intrigues, and repair ships in relative safety — through the countless cargo and repair services that
swarm around every arriving ship, including shady heretechs whose "no questions asked" expertise is
worth its high price.

## Winterscale's Realm

Winterscale's Realm is one of the most explored and exploited regions and has brought both legendary
riches and early death to the incautious in equal measure. The region is defined by the stars that
**Sebastian Winterscale** explored and charted in the early centuries of the 41st Millennium —
consisting of a handful of coveted worlds that lie like islands in the darkness of the unknown.
Rogue Traders have crossed the breadth of the Realm a hundredfold, yet its more distant reaches and
turbulent warp regions remain uncharted. Although only a handful of worlds within Winterscale's Realm
have ever been even partially explored, they have proven rich in treasure — gems, precious minerals,
exotic death-world beasts, xenos artefacts, and much more have flowed into the coffers of trading
cartels and noble backers in the Calixis Sector and beyond, thanks to the tireless explorers of the
Realm.

### The Thousand Charts

Winterscale's Realm is named after Sebastian Winterscale, who was the first to explore and chart many
of its stars. Those who venture into the Realm do so above all for the sake of its legendary wealth,
but also because it is one of the most chaotically charted regions of the Koronus Expanse. Numerous
Navigator clans each possess their own, partly contradictory charts; the few points on which they
agree are considered fairly reliable, while the rest is regarded as illusion or even a deadly trap.
Some whisper in the obscura-dens of Port Wander that all these charts are true and that, combined
correctly, they would reveal the true extent of Winterscale's discoveries and the hidden wealth of
his realm.

### Blood and False Gold

Winterscale's Realm's proximity to the Koronus Passage, the legends of its wealth, and the relative
abundance (however unreliable) of navigation charts attract explorers, trading concerns, and
renegades alike, all willing to fight for fortunes that may prove to be mere fantasy. Winterscale's
Realm is soaked in the blood of rival claimants, and every gleaming treasure brought back to the
Calixis Sector is bought with death and slaughter. Weaker Rogue Traders, wary of greater risks,
sometimes come here to grow slowly wealthy while remaining relatively close to the light of the
Imperium — but those who arrive unarmed and unprepared will never see the lights of Port Wander
again. The graves of the naïve, the arrogant, and the unlucky litter the stars of Winterscale's Realm
as silent testimony to this untamed cauldron of death and greed.

### Burnscour

A death world of roaring storms, jungle, and strange beasts — no place for humans, since the steaming
rain corrodes metal and cultivates strange fungus on exposed flesh, and the sap of its plants is
lethal or violently toxic. Nonetheless the beast trade has taken hold on Burnscour: Rogue Trader
ships and illegal, unsanctioned trading vessels bring saurian leapers, gargantipedes, and other
horrors of fang and claw at exorbitant prices to feed the hungry fighting pits of the distant Calixis
Sector. Hunting retinues in thick suits of vulcanised rubber roam the jungles in search of exotic
xenos predators.

There are no permanent structures on Burnscour's surface — only the slowly decaying metal wrecks of
crashed landers, a few melted ruins of foolish builders, and the ever-growing jungle beneath the
corrosive rain. From the topmost canopy to the ground, the jungle is a choking mass of countless
plant species: trees with waxy, dark leaves and thorny trunks weeping thick, bile-coloured sap, pale
fungal blooms, thick vines, and blossoms in the colours of livid bruises that, when touched, open
fronds filling the air with a stupefying scent. Beasts of every size prowl the nightmare jungles,
perfectly adapted to the hellish environment — from beetle-like creatures that gnaw through flesh or
bark, to six-legged stalkers the size of three grown men that slip silently and unseen through the
mid-canopy. Almost all of them can kill any human who sets foot on Burnscour's surface — this same
lethality is both a curse to any settlement and the very treasure that draws so many. Hunting parties
are usually dropped in and stay as briefly as possible before summoning their waiting lander by
beacon; others strip the jungle with anti-plant bombs and heavy flamers to create short-lived
clearings. Even the feared Beast House of the Calixis Sector is said to have invested a small fortune
to secure a steady supply from Burnscour.

### The Egarian Dominion

Once a densely populated xenos domain spanning a handful of nearby stars, the alien civilisation
there fell many millennia ago to a nameless doom that left behind only empty, barren worlds and
crumbling structures. The Dominion's chief worlds are dry, cool desert planets covered by tightly
packed structures forming a vast three-dimensional labyrinth — walls and corners hundreds of metres
high, spread across thousands of kilometres. These claustrophobic xenos complexes are buried under
wind-driven sand and dry soil, their edges rugged cliffs at the rim of lowland deserts. The Egarians'
building material shimmers with an oily, rainbow sheen even as it crumbles with age, and inexplicably
conducts the light of the Egarian stars — even the deepest reaches of the xenos hives are lit by an
unsettling, shifting glow. The passages are cramped for humans; the hive-labyrinths are empty, as
though the xenos and almost all their works simply vanished overnight. Only the moan of the wind
through the narrow labyrinth passages and across the desert flats can be heard.

### The Murdered World of Jerazol

Jerazol is a barren world of ash and charred bone — according to legend, murdered out of greed and
malice. Discovered by a pious Rogue Trader whose name has not survived, Jerazol was once fertile and
green and home to a human population with a primitive tribal culture. The nameless Rogue Trader
wished to lead the population back into the light and rule of the God-Emperor and began to instruct
and civilise them, while purging them of any deviation or corruption. Shortly after Jerazol's
discovery, other explorers also found the world, convinced that the primitive people were hiding
lost technological wonders in underground passages built by their forgotten ancestors, who had once
come from beyond the stars. When the nameless Rogue Trader stood against them, they destroyed his
ships and let their wrecks fall onto Jerazol's surface like a god's burning tears. Afterwards — so it
is said — the murderers bombarded the world, burned its surface to ash, and choked its atmosphere
with smoke. There is no agreement as to whether the destroyers found the technological treasures they
sought: some say they rose thereby to the highest levels of power in the Imperium, others say they
found only ash, bone, and mud, and cursed the dreams that had led them through vacuum and madness to
murder a world for nothing. Jerazol remains a testament to the price that can be paid in the search
for the riches of Winterscale's Realm.

### The Foundling Worlds

The Foundling Worlds lie beyond the **Cauldron**, a smaller warp storm whose ominous churning may
serve as a warning against further exploration. By a strange quirk of fate, this star cluster —
although relatively close to the Koronus Passage — was not visited by imperial ships until several
centuries after the Koronus Expanse was opened. Among voidfarers, the Foundling Worlds are considered
a cursed place of sudden warp storms, temporal distortions, and strange stellar phenomena; nothing
undertaken within their bounds is said to come to a good end. This has not stopped some from
exploring the mysteries of the Foundling Worlds or from trying to tame them through colonisation and
exploitation — yet even after a route was established, most of its stars and planets remain
unexplored. The few attempts to found settlements or harvest wealth have ended in catastrophe or
misfortune.

**Lost to the Storm:** It is extremely difficult to navigate a course into the Foundling Worlds, even
along a charted route. The warp rears and writhes as if trying to knock a ship off course, and sudden
furious storms tear at Gellar fields. At other times, strange pockets of stillness form that hold
ships motionless. Even established routes are unreliable, sometimes vanishing entirely or suddenly
leading somewhere else. Many ships have been lost attempting to press into the Foundling Worlds, and
with every lost ship the region's evil reputation grows. Some Navigator Houses privately speculate
that the region is hidden and protected by something that does not wish its worlds to be violated by
human presence.

**Cursed Endeavours:** The Foundling Worlds are strewn with the dead remains of hopeful attempts to
wring profit from their stars — abandoned space stations unmanned for centuries, shattered outposts
inhabited by burned and desiccated corpses, feral human colonies that have turned away from the
Emperor's light. With every passing decade, a new bold generation scoffs at the stories of older,
more cautious explorers and lays its plans in the Foundling Worlds — some thrive for a time, but
fate is inescapable, and the malice of the Foundling Worlds is patient: in time, all are undone.

- **Grace:** a storm-lashed world wrapped in whirling clouds and hurricanes. Persistent storms carry
  the spores of its simple fungal life far and wide amid lightning and frozen hail. Beneath the
  storms, the peaks and valleys of Grace's jagged surface form a stark, beautiful landscape, once
  lined with the proud buildings of a colony founded under the authority of the Rogue Trader
  **Aspyce Chorda**. Grace was a colony that served not the expansion of the God-Emperor's realm but
  the greed and arrogance of Chorda: she brought rich and influential imperial exiles (secretly
  transported by the Cold Guild in frost-vaults and revived in the depths of Port Wander) to the
  world, had armoured palaces built for them, supplied them with illegal slaves from Footfall and the
  finest foods, and let their spies and agents pass in the holds of her ships — a paradise for the
  damned that did not last. Chorda's supply ships were swallowed by a warp storm that suddenly arose
  and sealed off the passage to Grace; the world itself could not produce its own food. After a
  period of false hope, the exiles and criminals turned on one another, plundering and burning down
  each other's palaces, until only a few remained — ugly fortresses against the predatory raids of
  the rest. When even that no longer sufficed, they began to eat their dead — at first the slain,
  then the still living. So it is that the few remaining colony palaces today house cannibals who are
  always hungry; some send distress calls into the void, hoping for unsuspecting travellers as food.
- **Rain:** a colony world of wet grasslands and highlands within the Foundling Worlds, whose flora
  and fauna were inedible but otherwise harmless. Small prospecting and bio-augur colonies were
  founded by several Rogue Traders in the late 8th century of the 41st Millennium. They found little
  of interest in the ecology and even less in mineral wealth. The last astropathic reports spoke of
  structures in the dense forest regions and a strange sound made by the rain near them. After that,
  nothing more was heard from the colonies except a single confused astropathic transmission,
  babbling of pale shapes in the rain and lithe forms in the clouds.
- **Iniquity:** a world of towering mountains rising from a sea of acid, orbited by a shattered moon.
  In dark caves dug into the rock of the mountains dwell thousands of renegades of the worst kind. On
  Iniquity, no law holds except that of the strongest; its brutal society is split into brotherhoods
  bound by blood and oaths sworn in unholy tongues. These brotherhoods control mines and smelters in
  the depths beneath the mountains and guard the labouring armies of prisoners, whose lives are best
  kept short. Chaos raiders supply Iniquity with metal and provisions and bring captured slaves to
  labour. There is no peace on Iniquity save the rule of strength and murder; mines established there
  in the past were never again brought under control after an uprising of forced labourers who
  slaughtered their overseers. To this day, no one has managed to cleanse Iniquity.

### Charnel Stars

The charred embers of the Charnel Stars lure explorers from the Foundling Worlds with the promise of
dead worlds and unknown treasures. No Rogue Trader has yet found a route through the writhing,
malicious warp of this region; many of the boldest have perished attempting to approach its seemingly
unreachable void.

### The Accursed Demesne

It has long been believed that all evil and all corruption in the Koronus Expanse originates from the
Accursed Demesne: warp energies that swept life from worlds, star clusters that seemingly died
together in unnatural catastrophes, and the ravenous Ork. Wise Rogue Traders still avoid these voids
today, and little is known about the worlds deep within the Accursed Demesne. Ordinary voidfarers
live in fear of the Demesne and the misfortune that emanates from it — they whisper stories of xenos
graves on Lathimon's Death, cursed Dolorium, and the realm of the terrible Ork beyond.

**Stars and Courses Uncharted:** The Accursed Demesne is a vast, uncharted region into which few have
travelled and returned. There are hardly any routes into it, and the presumably reliable ones are
jealously guarded by the Navigator clans who first found them. Other navigation fragments are highly
inaccurate and can cost both Navigator and ship their lives if followed blindly.

- **Lathimon's Death:** orbits one of the Cineris Malificum, a chain of stellar embers surrounded by
  shells of thinned stellar matter from their ancient demise. On this cold world, where Faith Lathimon
  and a hundred others died in ways never recorded, huge cyclopean structures, columns, and avenues
  rise across the darkened surface, so weathered and dust-covered that they resemble hills and
  valleys. Only a few have visited this world of horrors, including Balastus Irem (later killed by the
  Inquisition) and Rafe Longinus and Eduard Majessus, who vanished without a trace into the dark
  regions of the Koronus Expanse.
- **Processional of the Damned:** a system of a few barren worlds around a bright, restless star.
  Closer to the sun's energy lies the Processional itself: a thin, orbiting chain of debris, warp-
  crushed space hulks, and dead ships of hundreds of different xenos origins — were every shipyard of
  the Segmentum Obscurus to launch a new fleet simultaneously, that fleet would come close to matching
  the scale of the Processional. The currents of the warp have carried these uncounted wrecks here,
  perhaps for longer than humanity has traveled the stars — perhaps for longer than humanity has
  existed. Ghosts and other warp echoes circle the Processional, bound to the wrecks and the dust of
  their remains. The Rogue Trader **Wrath Umboldt** discovered the Processional in 746.M41 with his
  ship *Righteous Crusader*: presences stalked the crew, portent patterns swirled across the auspex
  grids, the *Crusader*'s machine spirits grew restless, and the ship's astropaths had to be sedated.
  Salvage crews Umboldt sent into the Processional returned some with great treasures and strange
  xenos artefacts, some mad and gibbering, and most not at all — swallowed by the Processional of the
  Damned or lured into freezing void by auspex ghosts and failing technology.

### Undred-Undred Teef

A region of systems at the heart of the Accursed Demesne, nest and breeding ground of the Ork. Only
a few of the boldest (or most reckless) explorers have ventured here; those who returned tell of
worlds embraced by refuse and wreckage, surrounded by clouds of debris from which Ork ships rise like
flies, their brutal prows bristling with weapons and skull war-paint. Beneath their mantles of scrap
and makeshift defences lie worlds poisoned by the spoils of Ork industry, on which the Ork constantly
slaughter one another over plundered scrap in order to build weapons, plate-clad fortresses, and vast
machine effigies. From these worlds, the Ork have in the past launched raids for plunder and raw
materials for their growing armies. The warlike Ork of Undred-Undred Teef are organised into gangs,
war bands, and clans, led by ever more powerful Ork bosses who constantly seek to outdo one another.

Undred-Undred Teef is unusual among Ork-dominated systems, as it harbours a far greater number of
rich, arrogant Ork known as **Flash Gitz** than is typical — some suspect this to be an evolutionary
response by the Ork to the opportunities of the Koronus Expanse. Flash Gitz are notorious for their
greed for treasure and are always interested in opportunities for robbery and plunder; they shrink
from neither betrayal nor murder nor other underhanded strategies in order to accumulate wealth and
more powerful wargear, and some even hire themselves out to other xenos races. Many of these Ork
become **Freebooterz**, which is why Undred-Undred Teef is a stronghold of numerous Freebooterz
hordes that dominate the greenskin hordes of this region. From time to time, powerful **Kaptins**
emerge, cunning beasts who lead their followers out of Undred-Undred Teef across the Koronus Expanse
in search of new plunder for their clans — so far, however, these few powerful individuals have only
been able to unite a fraction of the Ork of Undred-Undred Teef under themselves.

At the centre of Undred-Undred Teef lies the world where the Ork breed in the greatest numbers and
where the fighting between them is fiercer than anywhere else; mighty bosses and Freebooter Kaptins
gather here to measure their strength in the Expanse's greatest battle. In the Ork's rough tongue,
this place is called **Tusk**, the true heart of Undred-Undred Teef. A vast space hulk orbits Tusk's
surface, under whose direction millions of Ork and their lesser kin, guided by so-called **Meks**,
install engines and weapons plundered from the ships of other races or built in workshops scattered
across Undred-Undred Teef. No single Ork knows what drives them here, yet every cell of their flesh
senses that a **Waaagh!** is brewing — and all that it awaits is an unchallenged boss to emerge from
the crucible of Ork warfare. The Ork stand on the edge of a vast Waaagh! of destruction that, should
it not be stopped, will shake the Expanse and the realms beyond it.

**Ork Freebooterz:** Some claim the Ork are no great spacefaring race, their ships and weapons merely
unreliable constructions of scrap and plundered parts from other races — others point out that Ork
are found in every corner of the galaxy, and that the supposed limits of their technology have not
stopped them from ranking among the galaxy's most feared and effective pirates. Their ships are
massively armed and crammed with battle-hungry Ork; Ork rarely organise beyond the level of a single
ship, but small fleets can form around particularly charismatic or successful leaders (Kaptins). In
the Expanse, Ork are most often encountered in small raiding ships roughly the size of an imperial
escort vessel — deceptively small, for the ship is always packed with a green tide of Ork, so that any
boarding attempt can only go one way. Ork Freebooter attacks are usually brutal and direct: the ships
charge the enemy with all weapons firing before ramming and boarding. This does not preclude
cleverness — Ork often lurk in asteroid fields at the edges of systems to ambush convoys from hiding,
or raid inhabited worlds and stations, plundering and burning them before withdrawing into a debris
field. When aiming for a big fight, they will brutalise a world or station and simply wait to see who
or what appears to defend it.

**Morgaash Kulgraz and Da Wurldbraka:** An up-and-coming Kaptin seeking to unite other Freebooterz
under a single banner, with the goal of raising himself to overlord of Undred-Undred Teef. Morgaash
Kulgraz first appeared aboard a heavily damaged space hulk, the *Fist of Gork*, crammed with loot from
other parts of the galaxy — no one knows how long the hulk had drifted in the warp before that. As a
cunning master of ship-to-ship combat, Morgaash seized control of the largest and most powerful ship
in the system, the mighty battlekroozer **Da Wurldbraka**, and crewed it with the strongest, most
aggressive Boyz of other Kaptins, including as many psychic Ork Weirdboyz as he could find. Da
Wurldbraka quickly became as legendary as its Kaptin — a nightmarish sight that spreads panic among
smugglers and Rogue Traders alike. Morgaash strikes fast and with full force in every raid, deliberately
leaving few survivors to escape and spread his reputation across the Expanse. Most ships in the
Expanse can only hope to withdraw in time before the dark xenos ship ends the battle by crippling its
opponent and its Kaptin personally appearing on the enemy bridge.

## The Heathen Stars

The Heathen Stars are a diffuse region of ancient, dark-burning stars whose worlds have long been
inhabited — human societies and communities long separated from the rest of humanity, who know
nothing of the God-Emperor's divine light and preserve strange technologies from humanity's lost
past. It is possible that these scattered human communities are the remnants of one or more greater,
long-vanished empires — scattered enclaves like flotsam of a retreating tide. Thin routes have been
established from Winterscale's Realm to **Naduesh** and **Zayth** in the Heathen Stars; the rest of
these fallen worlds remain a subject of mystery and rumour. Rogue Traders have barely touched the
treasures of the Heathen Stars and have not yet brought the God-Emperor's word — a billion heathen
souls await the arrival of missionary zealots and great auto-temples.

- **Agusia:** a grave world orbiting a dark red star. A vanished human civilisation transported its
  dead to Agusia over millennia, turning it into a necropolis world. Agusia's vast ruin-deserts and
  tower-mountains are barren, eroded, or half-buried in wind-driven dust — every chamber an ancient
  tomb. Agusia remains almost untouched by explorers, save for a small expedition of the Disciples of
  Thule (an Adeptus Mechanicus sect), which pushed into the catacombs beneath the icy deserts of the
  northern polar zone and found ornately slanted grave shrines, huge dormant prayer-mechanisms,
  mausoleums efficiently packed with biers, impassable walls of black-green metal with silvered
  magnetic runes, and still-active holographic artworks.
- **Naduesh:** a world inhabited by humans, of dry plains and vast, decayed mega-cities that testify
  to an impressive technological achievement. The mega-cities are labyrinthine structures beneath
  huge, pillar-supported domes. Most of the population lives as a tribal society away from the
  cyclopean mega-cities, following herds of herbivores while waging bitter war on each other for
  honour and bloody sport. The population has little understanding of the technology left behind and
  cannot fully rebuild its society; the language and structure of Nadueshi culture point to a distant
  origin in High Gothic. The most influential part of the population lives in **Marajur**, a vast ruin
  complex 30 km wide and 3 km tall with its own weather system — despite its grandeur, a shadow of its
  former self, with ruined base structures and collapsing upper vaults. Naduesh serves many human
  renegades and outlaws as a supply point, trading weapons and slaves for livestock and fresh recruits
  from the planet's feral warriors.
- **Zayth:** a world deeply scarred by war. Vehicles the size of cities plough Zayth's single
  continent, each a fortress and weapons platform armed with fearsome war engines. Within them lives
  Zayth's human population, protected from the radiation and poisons of centuries of warfare. Zayth's
  surface has been barren for millennia, devastated by shellfire, greedy exploitation, and the passage
  of the hive-vehicles. Despite their armament and extraordinary vehicle-cities, the people of Zayth
  have fallen far behind their ancestors' knowledge in everything but war; the knowledge needed to
  build their hive-vehicles has long been lost. Generators and engine vaults are guarded by hereditary
  engineer orders that preserve the traditions and culture of each clan-fortress.
- **Raakata:** a ruin world reachable only through a shifting, treacherous warp region. Its collapsed,
  empty hives are said to be laden with untouched relics of the Dark Age of Technology, while vox
  transmissions in alien tongues, mangled binary code, and static noise can be heard in the vacuum
  around Raakata. Its human population consists of feral, vicious savages who paint themselves with
  ash and rusty powder; the knowledge of their ancestors, who built the crumbling hives and the
  treasures rotting within them, has long been lost to them. Everything known about Raakata comes from
  the reports of **Toros Umboldt**, who claimed the discovery for himself but failed to find the world
  again on two later expeditions — all traces of his earlier visit had been erased by the shifting
  warp, considerably diminishing his standing among his peers.
- **Vaporius:** Everything known about Vaporius and its strange people comes from rumours in Port
  Wander and Footfall. Vaporius is said to be a world of red deserts, shimmering turquoise seas, and
  great cities of copper towers, enamelled domes, and buildings covered in colourful glass, metal, and
  ceramic tiles. Its human population is large, with proud, almost feline features and brightly
  cyan-coloured eyes; they wear shimmering robes that subtly change colour. Rule over Vaporius is said
  to lie in the hands of **Priest-Kings**, who control the distribution of water, venerated as divine
  life-force. It is said that decades ago a group of missionaries travelled to Vaporius to break the
  rule of the Priest-Kings — nothing was ever heard of them again, save rumours of torture, slaughter,
  and blood.

## The Unbeholden Reaches

Beyond the greater nebulae of the Koronus Expanse lie regions visited only by the taciturn Disciples
of Thule and a few others. Tales are told of ghost ships, beautiful but abandoned worlds, dust nebulae
that claim the souls of the damned and the outcast — and, of course, immeasurable riches awaiting the
bold Rogue Traders who dare to claim them.

- **Concanid:** a distant, unvisited star that has become a place both alluring and repellent through
  its connection to the dying words of a psyker. In 633.M41, an astropath in Footfall was driven to
  madness and slow death by terrible visions; before his death he spoke of "dark worms beneath a
  green-eyed star", the "sea of molten gold", and other horrors. Some scholars of the arcane suspect
  that Concanid, in the distant Unbeholden Reaches, is the "green-eyed star" — Concanid regularly
  flares green for reasons unknown to the tech-priests of the Divine Astrometricum. The star remains
  unvisited.
- **Illisk:** The Disciples of Thule possess records of a strange machine-world deep in the Koronus
  Expanse — bizarre cogitator arrays packed beneath every part of its crust, in vast vaults extending
  to the limits of geo-auspex probes. The surface is lashed by immense storms and laid waste by
  ancient strip mining; huge towers channel geothermal heat through massive shafts into the turbulent
  atmosphere, driving raging storm belts. Ten citadels rise from the crust, each as massive as a hive,
  empty but for the whispering dust of dead xenos; kilometre-long corridors are filled with niches
  holding desiccated xenos corpses, their flesh still threaded with filaments leading into the vast
  machines.
- **Orn:** Everything known about Orn comes from the Disciples of Thule, who were drawn to Orn's
  web-forests by emanations from a dormant warp engine in a half-buried xenos ship. There the
  Explorators discovered a warlike, almost feral xenos race using the strange ship as a kind of city
  or nest; the xenos used tech-devices as relics and hunted, tore apart, and devoured survey servitors
  dropped from orbit. The Thuleans marked the world with the rune of unyielding refusal, declared it
  anathema to humanity, and moved on.
- **The Rifts of Hecaton:** an unnatural darkness in the far depths of the Koronus Expanse, like the
  wrath of long-fallen gods cast over the worlds there. Their presence casts a long shadow over the
  Expanse; the Rift-stars are extinguished and dead, and the bold Rogue Traders who travel there do
  not return. The Rifts once swallowed the mighty Lord Inquisitor and Rogue Trader **Kobras
  Aquairre**, and it is suspected that the Disciples of Thule who followed him met the same fate. The
  stars in the shadow of the Rifts are ill-omened places about which little more than myth and
  prophecy is known.
- **Melbethe:** an angry, seething star near the Rifts of Hecaton, surrounded by debris and flare
  energies. The Disciples of Thule are among the few explorers to have ventured this far into the
  Expanse and returned; they report that Melbethe was once home to a strange xenos civilisation that
  carved black palaces from the asteroids and left behind vast obsidian sculptures in the void — long
  vanished, with only shattered wrecks remaining.
- **The Far Corpse Stars:** Many corpse stars lie in the far reaches of the Koronus Expanse — the
  Seven Dooms, the Pyres, and the shredded, nameless embers at the outermost edges of the Rifts of
  Hecaton. These stars were extinguished and died long ago in a vast catastrophe; shrouded in the
  cast-off gases of their demise, they await the arrival of humanity. Ancient powers, dead for aeons,
  may still stir on the devastated worlds of these distant regions, and the warp is uneasy.

## Denizens of the Koronus Expanse

The Koronus Expanse is an abyss of darkness in which the few facts known to humanity stand like
flickering candles. Within its vast boundaries wait worlds and secrets whose discovery would require
generations of researchers, and in these reaches of the unknown and uncharted, other powers move
with inhuman intentions. From the long-dead builders of the Halo Artefacts to the treacherous Eldar,
many powers hold claim to or interest in the Koronus Expanse — and all these motivations frequently
bring them into contact with the Rogue Traders, who are only the youngest among them.

### The Ork Menace

The Ork are a race of brutal, hideous aliens with an insatiable thirst for violence — a green-skinned
plague of the galaxy that constantly multiplies, gathers in vast numbers, and strikes through the
stars like a bloody fist, plundering and destroying whatever stands in its way. "Might is right" is
the undeniable mantra of all Ork, understood in every cell of their being; they fight constantly
among themselves, and only the strongest survive in Ork society. Whoever gains authority over other
Ork (a "da Boss") does so because he has proven himself to be the meanest and toughest. An average
Ork is as tall as a human, but hunched with muscle and would tower over most humans if it stood fully
upright — long arms laced with plate-like muscle, a thick, low-set skull with a heavy brow and
glittering red eyes, and a mouth full of jagged fangs. Ork physiology makes them extraordinarily hard
to kill; they can shrug off wounds that would kill a human, and even survive having limbs severed and
crudely reattached.

Ork speak a rough, guttural language that mirrors their physical appearance and blunt worldview.
Despite primitive technology, they possess an innate understanding of machines and weapons — this
racial knowledge manifests in so-called **Oddboyz**, Ork with special abilities. Most important to
the Ork war machine are the **Mekboyz**, ingenious but unreliable craftsmen, masters at cobbling
together scrap and plunder into terrifyingly effective starships, war machines, and weapons — even
if they do not always work exactly as intended.

**Ork Freebooterz** (see Undred-Undred Teef above) dominate the greenskin presence of the Koronus
Expanse; their attacks are brutal and direct, their greed for plunder boundless, their organisation
beyond the level of individual ships rare, except around particularly charismatic Kaptins. Even the
relatively well-explored systems near the Maw have felt the sting of Ork piracy — Footfall itself has
in the past been attacked by Ork Kaptins commanding enough gunships and kroozers.

### The Stryxis

The Stryxis are a sparse, nomadic race with a reputation as unreliable traders, wanderers, and
occasional slavers and pirates. Rarely encountered in the Koronus Expanse, their reputation is dark.
Physically, the Stryxis are hideous beneath ragged, tawny cloths and trinkets, described by human
observers as gaunt, many-eyed creatures resembling a human-sized, skinned canine embryo. Yet they
communicate effortlessly with willing humans through a shared language of greed, curiosity, and
self-interest. As collectors and compulsive hoarders, they possess a wealth of technology stolen and
traded from countless races. They love trade, judge things solely by their perceived rarity, and
appear to care about neither conquest, territory, nor even their own species — driven purely by
avarice and petty intrigue. Nevertheless, they should not be underestimated: they are extremely
dangerous and cunning, hiding sharp intellects behind eccentric behaviour, and do not hesitate to
betray those they trade with when a large profit presents itself. The Stryxis trade with almost
anyone, human or xenos, even followers of the Ruinous Powers — but the Eldar despise them and kill
them wherever they can.

The Stryxis inhabit wandering caravans of ships, often salvaged patchwork vessels and hollowed-out
asteroids, equipped with stolen or traded technology. These caravans contain relatively few adult
Stryxis, but larger numbers of slaves, mercenaries, "acquisitions", and genetically altered servant
creatures. Nothing is known about young Stryxis, sex variants, or other cultural aspects — when
asked, the Stryxis only tell endless, often contradictory lies. Stryxis caravans interest Rogue
Traders for their diverse trading opportunities, and because, as tireless wanderers and collectors,
they often possess hoards of secrets, legends, and information whose contacts reach beyond the
Koronus Expanse — provided the Rogue Trader knows how to separate truth from lies.

### The Chaos Gods and their Servants

Beneath the surface of reality lurks the warp, a realm of unspeakable horror and insane chaos,
inhabited by the Dark Powers. Since time immemorial, humanity has believed in spirits, gods, and
demons — otherworldly powers to be appeased, feared, and bargained with. In the 41st Millennium,
these things are all too real. Of all the Dark Powers, the four Chaos Gods — Khorne, Tzeentch,
Slaanesh, and Nurgle — cast the longest shadow, though many venerate them as a combined pantheon.

- **Khorne** — the Blood God, Skull Lord, the Master of Battle: god of murder, rage, and war. His
  brass throne stands atop a mountain of bones above an endless sea of blood. He is the hunter of
  souls and herald of war; his glory is shown in the flesh-tearing burst of a bolter shell or the wet,
  rending teeth of a chainsword.
- **Tzeentch** — the Changer of Ways, the Master of Fortune, the Great Conspirator, Architect of
  Fate: god of change, mutation, and sorcery. Father of Paradoxes and master of intrigue, he twists
  the threads of fate toward unfathomable ends and blesses his followers with arcane power and
  terrible mutations.
- **Slaanesh** — the Pleasure Lord, the Prince of Pleasure, Despoiler: god of hedonism, temptation,
  and excess. The Golden-Haired God watches over the writhing pleasure-pits and ecstatic rites of his
  slaves.
- **Nurgle** — the Lord of Pestilence, Great Corruptor, the Master of Plague, the Fly Lord: god of
  decay, disease, and entropy. Grandfather Nurgle embraces all within his rotting embrace.

**Slaves to Darkness:** Within the warp exist dark, malevolent intelligences shaped from the darkest
wishes and fears of sentient beings — the Dark Gods, Ruinous Powers, or Chaos Gods. Whoever submits to
them forfeits their immortal soul and becomes a slave to darkness.

**Chaos Pirates and Renegades:** The servants of the Dark Gods take many forms in the Expanse — most
dangerous are the piratical renegades, corrupted men, some fled from the Imperium, some from worlds
deep in the Expanse where the light of the Emperor's truth has never reached. They command hijacked
or renegade imperial ships or mysterious vessels of unknown origin, and are no longer fully human;
better dead than forced into their slave crews. They raid any convoy or world that cannot defend
itself, seeking slaves, murder, and wealth — through sorcery and the favour of demons bought with
blood and souls, some cross the warp and roam the Koronus Expanse as single ships or in packs.

- **Chaos Space Marines:** once mighty warriors of the Adeptus Astartes who forswore the Imperium and
  became monstrous warriors obedient only to their own dark desires and the Chaos Gods. From hidden
  bases and desecrated starships, they wage war against the Imperium they once protected. Rumours say
  that war bands of renegade Marines have been lured into the Expanse by something that could be an
  apocalyptic weapon or a fragment of lost knowledge — these fallen Astartes subjugate the lesser
  servants of the Dark Gods like proud, bloody lords.
- **The Saynay Clan:** a dynasty of corrupted monsters who fled the rotted world of Dusk after
  Inquisition agents discovered the corruption in their blood. In the Koronus Expanse they still
  exist aboard isolated ships, their interiors hung like slaughterhouses with the dismembered corpses
  of their raids; the clan and its vassals feed on the dead, paint the hulls of their ships with vile
  runes, and perform grisly sacrificial rituals to summon demons into desecrated dead flesh.
- **The Reavers of Karrad Vall:** Karrad Vall's name is a dark curse to all who venture into the
  Koronus Expanse. No one has ever seen him, yet the barbed prows of his ships and the baying of his
  scarred warriors are enough to make captains scuttle their own ships rather than be brought before
  Karrad Vall, the Faceless Lord. Some whisper he was once an Adept of the Munitorium, a tithe-master
  who fell to the Dark Gods; others claim he was a trusted admiral of the Battlefleet Calixis. What is
  known is that Karrad Vall crossed the Maw in 794.M41 and immediately bombarded Footfall and battled
  Calligos Winterscale — his small fleet broke through the assembled defences. He then vanished
  without a trace for over a decade. Vall's fleet, called **"the Wolfpack"** (not to be confused with
  the imperial ship class of the same name), consists of a brutalised selection of captured or
  salvaged imperial ships, steered by the arts of vassal witches and powerful demon-pacts. Known
  ships of Vall's fleet include the *Excruciation*, *Gift of Despair*, *Mortis Ex Astra*, and
  *Optimus Nemesis*. All actions by the Reavers so far have aimed at gaining resources to expand
  Vall's pirate fleet: ships, weapons, and slaves.
- **Followers of False Gods:** There are worlds in the Koronus Expanse whose inhabitants have fallen
  to false gods that are merely a mask for the Ruinous Powers — proud, decadent civilisations that
  build temples to the Dark Gods and hold their societies together with sorcery, terror, and
  atrocity. Where these worlds lie and what devilries they harbour remains to be discovered from the
  Expanse's dark unknown — were such a world found, truly faithful servants of the Emperor would
  utterly destroy its people and the lie of their gods.

### The Treacherous Eldar

The Eldar are an ancient, highly advanced alien race that crossed the stars long before humanity
could even comprehend such a thing. An extraordinarily long-lived people, physically human-like but
with longer, slenderer limbs, elegant features, pointed ears, and piercing eyes — possessed of
inhuman grace, heightened intensity of experience, and psychic potential. The Eldar have a long,
complex history as a spacefaring race and once ruled an empire that spanned the galaxy. That era of
dominance ended in a catastrophe known as **the Fall**, which caused the collapse of Eldar
civilisation. Most survivors fled the death of their worlds aboard vast starships called
**Craftworlds**, larger than the greatest ship of the Imperium, drifting aimlessly through the void.

Though a dwindling race, Eldar technology remains highly advanced, based largely on the manipulation
of psychoplastic materials through psychic energy — breathtaking architecture, light yet highly
effective armour, and lethal weapons. Their ships are fast, sleek vessels driven by solar sails and
disguised by holofields that conceal their position and confuse sensors. Under the command of a
master of void warfare, an Eldar ship is exceptionally manoeuvrable and almost without equal.

> **The Webway:** The Eldar overcome the vast distances of space through a labyrinth of tunnels and
> passages through the warp, known as the Webway. Access is via portals; the tunnels beyond are
> stable passages, unaffected by the dangers of normal warp travel, that can shorten impossibly long
> journeys to a matter of hours. The size of the tunnels and portals ranges from ones passable only
> on foot by human-sized beings to ones that allow Eldar starships to pass through quickly and
> relatively safely.

**Outcasts from the Path:** For Eldar living on the Craftworlds, the heightened intensity of Eldar
life is strictly controlled by following a "Path" that sets limits on an individual's experiences.
Occasionally, an Eldar turns their back on the Path and lives without restriction — such Eldar are
called **Outcasts**. This decision is dangerous, as it makes the Outcast more vulnerable to the
nemesis that has pursued them since the Fall, and risks being consumed by their experiences and
falling to darkness. The **Eldar Corsairs**, who hunt other races in the void, are Outcasts from the
Eldar Path — highly dangerous and unpredictable, capable equally of generosity and indiscriminate
slaughter.

**The Predations of the Eldar within the Koronus Expanse:** Several Eldar Corsair fleets are active
in the Koronus Expanse, ranging from single ships to large, dangerous fleets. Some hunt weak,
unprotected ships like predators stalking sick prey; only rarely do Corsair ships openly attack the
strongholds of other races, preferring the predator's silent approach. Two particularly feared
Corsair bands are the **Children of Thorns** and the **Crow Spirits**.

- **The Children of Thorns:** merciless warriors clad in gleaming, beetle-black armour who slaughter
  their enemies to the last man in battle. They are led by an Eldar woman who long ago turned from
  the Eldar Path to lead the Children of Thorns through bloodshed across the Halo Stars — her true
  name has long been forgotten; she is known only as the **Mother of the Shadows**. In void attacks
  their pitch-black ships rely above all on stealth; only after achieving total surprise and perfect
  attack position do they strike with savagery and precision. Despite their fearsome reputation, the
  Children of Thorns have done business with several Rogue Traders, even forming pacts or fighting as
  mercenaries — these bonds presumably provide them with the phenomenally accurate information that
  allows them to attack human ships near the Maw with such precision.
- **The Crow Spirits:** grim, cold killers who seem intent not on piracy but on the deliberate
  extermination of humanity in the Koronus Expanse. Clad in glittering, moonlit armour, they fight in
  silence, led by witches wrapped in pale rags with tall helms of gleaming bone. Their ships appear
  like pale dead things, driven by a cold, broken will; they strike from the depths of the void at
  ships, colonies, and stations in the Foundling Worlds, the Accursed Demesne, and the Unbeholden
  Reaches, disguised and unseen until it is too late. They kill with cold precision and then withdraw
  without taking plunder. Who leads them and what their purpose is remains unknown; some suspect they
  guard something or prevent its discovery. At least one Inquisitor of the Ordo Xenos suspects a
  connection to a notorious Eldar Corsair of the Calixis Sector, **Ulthyr Ellarion**.

> **The Ghost Ship — the Whisper of Anaris:** Aside from Karrad Vall's Wolfpack and Da Wurldbraka, no
> legend of the Koronus Expanse is as notorious as the *Whisper of Anaris*. This ghost ship has been
> raiding the Expanse for centuries; its appearance is considered an omen of unholy misfortune.
> Tattered, ethereal sails drive the Eclipse-class cruiser out of the depths of the void, concealed by
> holofield technology far more effective than that of most Eldar ships. Its powerful pulsar lances
> have shattered ships throughout the Expanse; on some worlds, a sighting of the Whisper of Anaris
> triggers a wave of prayer and offerings to drive the ghost ship away. The first reported encounter
> comes from the Rogue Trader **Aspyce Chorda**, on a supply run to Lucin's Breath. The only certainty
> about the Whisper of Anaris: it follows an unfathomable agenda of its own — the ghost ship has on
> occasion helped Rogue Traders, only to attack them at another encounter.

### The Rak'Gol Marauders

The Rak'Gol are a relatively new threat to the Koronus Expanse, first encountered a little over a
century ago in the distant stars beyond the Alenic Depths. A xenos race about which factually little
is known: crudely hewn, irregular, stony reptilian beings, eight-limbed and over three metres long,
chalk-white in colour and mantis-like in build. Rak'Gol warriors favour cybernetic augmentation to
enhance their abilities and replace lost limbs. Their place of origin is unknown, as is the
motivation for their sporadic attacks on human worlds and ships — beyond indiscriminate slaughter and
the theft of minerals and weapons. Their shrieking language remains uncomprehended, no successful
communication between Rak'Gol and humanity has ever been recorded, and no Rak'Gol has ever been taken
alive — even their name derives from children's stories of mythical monsters from Footfall's slums.
Rak'Gol technology is rated by the Magos Explorators as inferior to Imperial Standard, yet what they
lack there they more than make up for in savagery, determination, and considerable individual combat
skill. Many mysteries surround them, not least the location of their homeworld (already marked for
destruction by many Rogue Traders, should it be found) — some suspect a hidden xenos empire deep
within the Expanse, others consider them locust-like nomads. Another mystery: how they navigate the
warp despite no known psykers among them — some adepts with dark knowledge see similarities between
their distorted symbols and the blasphemous iconography of Chaos.

### The Disciples of Thule

Archmagos Paracelcus Thule's immense Explorator fleet is a significant power within the Calixian
Mechanicus; nonetheless, he and his followers vanish beyond the sector's borders for decades at a
time. In 528.M41, a Thulean sub-fleet under Magos Solus Kanceme pushed into the Koronus Expanse and
scattered far and wide, avoiding xenos worlds in search of pre-imperial artefacts sacred to the
Omnissiah. Little is known of Kanceme's fate. The Disciples of Thule remain a mystery to Rogue
Traders — vanished for centuries into distant voids, uncommunicative and fixed on their own goals.
Many believe the Thuleans are lost, swallowed by the countless dangers of the Expanse. Should the
disciples of Paracelcus Thule still come and go through the Koronus Passage, they do so covertly,
avoiding both Port Wander and Footfall. A Rogue Trader contesting a treasure of ancient human
technology with Thuleans should regard them as an opponent as great and ruthless as any xenos race.

### The Yu'vath

The Koronus Expanse is rich in unnatural clusters of dead stars, each a dark ember, the remnant of a
vast catastrophe in which a star, in its death throes, hurled a shell of burning outer matter into the
void. The dark outer reaches of the Rifts of Hecaton were shaped long ago by the violent deaths of
dozens, perhaps hundreds, of stars — some great evil must once have swept across the Expanse,
murdering stars in clusters. Dead xenos worlds orbit these stellar remnants, burned to molten rock in
ancient death throes and then frozen beneath the pale light of a stellar ember. Their ruins are
cyclopean, their underground passages labyrinthine, and their ominous symbols — where not already
weathered to dust — warn of xenos sorcery and ancient corruption: they speak of the **Yu'vath**.

The Yu'vath were a plague upon the Calyx Expanse in the era of Lord-Militant Angevin, before the
founding of the Calixis Sector in the fourth century of the 39th Millennium. The warp-worshipping
Yu'vath enslaved worlds through abominable xenos sorcery and consumed the lives and souls of corrupted
human slaves to build, on the homeworlds of the Calyx, horrors that the chroniclers of the Angevin
Crusade refused to record. The warp-saturated Yu'vath and their tormented slave armies ground down the
Crusade's forces for decades before finally being annihilated by General (later Saint) Drusus and the
Adeptus Astartes. Even today, the chronicles of the Angevin Crusade's victories over the Yu'vath are
sealed as secret works, and only a few scholars know what happened before the blessed founding of the
Calixis Sector.

### Halo Artefacts

Halo Artefacts (also called Halo Devices) have been known for millennia to select archheretics,
apostates, and the Holy Ordos of the Inquisition, ever since respected Explorators and Rogue Traders
of antiquity returned from the Halo Stars with these devices, along with stories of corpse stars and
disturbing pict-recordings of dead xenos worlds. The Artefacts resist all tech-analysis and are nearly
immune to age and damage. The corrupt among the imperial masses long ago discovered that some of these
xenos devices can grant eternal life through dark transformation of body and mind — the body is
restored to youth, and time no longer weakens the flesh. Other Halo Artefacts possess stranger,
greater powers, whose unholy might lies behind the rise of some notorious archheretics. Over the
centuries, perhaps a few dozen Artefacts have changed hands for vast sums, and the opening of the
Koronus Expanse in the 41st Millennium has further increased their number. Halo Artefacts take many
forms, most small and easily mistaken for strange jewellery, pieces of armour, or abstract miniature
sculpture — some irregular spheres, some segmented ovoids, some beetle-like objects; all unmistakably
of xenos origin, made of unidentifiable metallic material, with surface patterns resembling insect
carapace or reptile skin.

**The Transformed:** Whoever seeks the power of a Halo Artefact bonds physically with the device and
is, over time, transformed into an immortal, inhuman monster — the powers granted come at the cost of
body, mind, and soul, corrupted by ancient alien technology. Whoever merges with a Halo Artefact is
ultimately doomed, a victim of xenos cravings and nightmare states that eventually dominate every
moment. Many of these transformed monsters flee into the depths of the Koronus Expanse, where their
transformation and the unnatural cravings that accompany it can remain hidden beneath undiscovered
suns. Some within the Ordo Xenos regard Rogue Traders who spend much time unobserved in the depths of
the Expanse, avoiding the company of others, with suspicion, wondering whether they too might have
become something other than human.

**The Risk and the Reward:** A Rogue Trader may, through trading a single Halo Artefact from a dead
world of the Koronus Expanse, gain a fortune that eclipses his noble inheritance — yet he risks life
and legacy, to be burned alive, the fate of all those discovered by the Ordo Xenos in contact with
such abominable corruption. Or worse: the Artefact might call to him, merge with him, and drag him
into an eternity of corruption — the nightmare legacy of long-vanished xenos builders. Every Rogue
Trader of standing knows the bloody consequences that befell the Lord-Captains **Balastus Irem, Rafe
Longinus, and Eduard Majessus** in the fifth century of the 41st Millennium, on suspicion of trading
in Halo Artefacts. Wealth in a hundred other forms exists in the Koronus Expanse — crux gems, precious
ores, xenos beasts for the circuses, and worlds full of heathen riches — yet dead stars and eldritch
grave-shrines on lifeless xenos worlds sing their siren song, and there are always some who listen.

### The Kroot

The Kroot are a predatory, often mercenary, bird-like alien race that fights with savagery and feeds
on the dead. The Kroot maintain a nomadic society, strangely divided between wild barbarism and
skilled use of technology. Their homeworld is **Pech**, fully part of the Tau Empire. Nonetheless,
many Kroot fight as mercenaries for others, which contradicts the Greater Good and is something the
Kroot wish to keep secret from the Tau — these mercenary kinship groups fight for whoever pays the
right price. They are thinly scattered across the galaxy and cross the void in imposing "warspheres",
yet abandon almost all of their technology once they settle on a habitable world, in order to live and
fight there in a wild state. Most disturbing of all: the Kroot devour the flesh of other species with
greedy, selective appetite, and the distorted forms of future generations thereby take on some traits
of those consumed. Much of their culture is driven by these unholy acts, carried out on the
instruction of their leaders, beasts known as **Shapers**. Each warsphere contains numerous Kroot
kindreds, diverse and often corrupted by the past choices of their Shapers in victims — some clearly
human. Marauding Kroot warspheres cross the Koronus Expanse only very rarely; no one knows where they
come from or where they are bound. Some Rogue Traders have taken Kroot mercenaries into service,
others destroy them on sight, wary that this predatory species might gain a foothold in the region.

### Rogue Traders Known Within the Expanse

- **Aspyce Chorda:** ruthless in negotiation as in battle — a dangerous woman who does not hesitate to
  cruelly remind others why they should fear her. Form and elegance matter to her; human life scarcely
  at all, except when it fills her coffers. When, in 754.M41, she raided the pirates of Iniquity to
  conclude the Harvest of Reavers, survivors were crudely lobotomised and shipped as slave-serfs to
  Chorda's holdings. In a dispute over resources with Calligos Winterscale in 754.M41 (note: the text
  cites 785.M41 for the conflict itself), she sparked a violent war, ruthless of the cost to her crews.
  The Chorda line is of piratical origin; her descendants differ from the pirates hunted by the
  Battlefleet Calixis only by their Warrant of Trade and noble birth. Legend has it that a young
  Aspyce Chorda once captured her own siblings and forced them into hidden cryo-vaults to secure her
  sole rule over the Chorda Warrant — there the unfortunates remain to this day, frozen souls awaiting
  a revival that will never come. (Chorda also founded the colony of Grace, see Foundling Worlds
  above, and had the first reported encounter with the ghost ship Whisper of Anaris, on a supply run
  to Lucin's Breath.)
- **Calligos Winterscale:** a man of passions, ruled by his notorious temper — not to be crossed
  lightly. His mood can shift within minutes from convivial friendship to screaming rage, and he does
  not hesitate to unleash his anger to enforce his will. Winterscale respects strength of will — even
  if he does not show it amid his outbursts; whoever yields is, to him, a weak subordinate. Even his
  allies and closest confidants fear his intimidating, stormy moods. Calligos Winterscale is a
  forceful battle commander and commands a mighty ship, the *Emperor's Vow*. He possesses a gift for
  leading the common rabble and hardened voidfarers, and draws dangerous men into his crew — many of
  his servants and envoys are barely a step removed from murderers, muscular, intimidating men who
  could have stepped straight out of an imperial prison.
- **Jonquin Saul:** Some nobles look down on the origins of the Saul fortune, built through trade
  dealings between established worlds like a common Free Trader or Chartered Captain. Jonquin Saul
  embodies this legacy: a strong-willed, charismatic soul, more captivated by trade and the tides of
  markets than by the leap into the unknown — every opportunity is a trading chance, and it is
  considered a crime to let a meeting between Rogue Traders end without a deal being struck. Saul
  regards the Heathen Stars as the true grail of the Koronus Expanse: their billion souls will one day
  be connected to the Calixis Sector by trade routes and a vast flow of goods. In this endeavour, Saul
  has powerful allies within the Ministorum of the Drusus Marches, which is eager to bring the words
  of the God-Emperor to the lost souls of the distant voids.
- **Wrath Umboldt:** has seen horrors in the Koronus Expanse — places that shrivel the soul, where
  once the Yu'vath raged and where the dead still gather. He discovered the Processional of the Damned
  in 746.M41 (see above) and caught a glimpse of the Alenic Depths, where souls are stolen. In the
  decades since that terrible journey, Umboldt has searched less within the Expanse than within his
  own heart, seeking a deeper connection to the God-Emperor and a higher purpose than mere wealth. His
  successes as a Rogue Trader have in any case been modest, with numerous disappointing and costly
  expeditions by his small fleet into the nearby Expanse. Umboldt believes himself under a penance of
  misfortune meant to lead him to enlightenment.
- **Tanak Valcetti:** presents himself as a man of refinement, for whom the hardships of expeditions
  into the dangerous Expanse are simply not something one discusses. A noble, he believes, must appear
  unconcerned by danger, possess knowledge like a sage, and stand far above the crude urges of lesser
  individuals — this at least is the philosophy of the Valcetti line, which has served it well so far:
  centuries of cultivated, distant courtesy and mildly acknowledged respect for the endeavours of
  others have earned Tanak Valcetti few enemies. Behind the mask of noble courtesy hides a brave,
  capable man with a genuine love for the finer things in life, who expects the best from his
  followers. The wealth of the Valcetti family is currently sustained through steady exploitation of
  worlds in the Expanse and large investments in trade between the Scarus and Calixis Sectors. Tanak
  Valcetti and his sons depart for years at a time into the Koronus Expanse, leaving the administration
  of his fortified estate and his investments in Port Wander to his seneschal.

### Lost Lineages of the Koronus Expanse and its Environs

Many Rogue Trader lines have fallen victim to the Koronus Expanse or to their own corruption. Great
families survive the loss of their patriarch or a single scion, but smaller lines can be wiped out
entirely by a single death:

- **Aquairre Lineage:** extinguished with the notorious **Kobras Aquairre**, who earned a reputation as
  a genocidal madman and scourge of those seeking to escape the Imperium's judgement. Vanished in the
  Rifts of Hecaton in 201.M41.
- **Deed Lineage:** the Ordo Xenos burned **Amphian Deed** at the stake in 803.M41 for his role in the
  Halo-of-Darkness cult, ending his line.
- **Dewain Lineage:** died out on the deathbed of **Parsimus Dewain** in 498.M41, as this great man
  left no heir.
- **Haarlock Lineage:** destroyed from within by the blood-stained **Erasmus Haarlock**, who vanished
  in 703.M41. Of all the lost lines, the Haarlock line is the most shrouded in dark, terrible myths.
- **Irem Lineage:** ended by the murder of **Balastus Irem** and his entire family by the Holy
  Inquisition in 443.M41, as just punishment for trading in xenos corruption.
- **Kail Lineage:** ended through assassination just days after **Fenton Kail** declared he had
  discovered an intact Standard Template Construct, in 388.M41.
- **Urussalin Lineage:** lost in the void with **Roodmar Urussalin**, presumably slain by Ork in
  718.M41.
- **Renuka Lineage:** **Veronique Renuka** was declared outlaw for her involvement in an uprising
  attempt on the Calixian hive world Cyclopia in 179.M41. Her descendants are said to still operate as
  pirates at the edge of the Unbeholden Reaches.
