# Chapter IX: Playing the Game

> Source: Rogue Trader Core Rulebook, Chapter IX, book pages 229–284 (PDF pages 233–288).
> Body text is condensed where noted; rules values, modifiers, and tables are captured in full as a reference.

## Tests: The Basic Mechanic

**Core mechanic:** Determine the Skill/Characteristic → apply modifiers → roll `1d100` → result ≤ the modified value = success, otherwise failure.

**Skill Tests:** Every Skill is governed by a Characteristic. With training, the full (modified) value counts. For an **Untrained Basic Skill**, the governing Characteristic is **halved (round down)**, with situational modifiers applied afterward. An **Untrained Advanced Skill** cannot be tested at all.

### Table 9-1: Skill Tests

| Skill Type | Effect |
|---|---|
| Untrained Basic Skill | Test at half Characteristic (round down) |
| Untrained Advanced Skill | Cannot perform a test with this Skill |
| Trained Basic or Advanced Skill | Test at full Characteristic |
| Mastered Basic or Advanced Skill | Test at full Characteristic **+10 or +20** |

**Characteristic Tests:** If a task is not covered by a Skill, the GM instead tests a Characteristic directly (`1d100` against the — possibly modified — value). Weapon Skill and Ballistic Skill, despite their names, are also Characteristics.

### Table 9-2: Characteristics Tests (Examples)

| Characteristic | Example Tests |
|---|---|
| Weapon Skill | Make an attack with a melee weapon |
| Ballistic Skill | Make an attack with a ranged weapon |
| Strength | Break down a door, restrain a captive, push over a Grox |
| Toughness | Resist poison or disease, tolerate temperature extremes, stave off hunger |
| Agility | Maintain balance on a narrow surface, navigate treacherous terrain |
| Intelligence | Recall an important detail, identify a familiar face, solve a puzzle |
| Perception | Notice a hidden enemy, locate a secret door, gauge another person's attitude |
| Willpower | Resist a Psychic Power, torture, or Fear |
| Fellowship | Make a good impression, inspire confidence, seduce a target |

## Degrees of Success and Failure

For every full **10 points** by which the (modified) value is beaten on a success, one **Degree of Success**; for every full 10 points it is exceeded, one **Degree of Failure**.

## Extended Tests

Some tasks require more than a single successful Skill Test; the GM decides how many successes are needed and how much time a single test represents (depending on the Skill involved and the task).

## Opposed Tests

Both sides test normally. Whoever succeeds wins; if both succeed, the higher number of Degrees of Success wins; on a tie, the higher Characteristic Bonus; on a further tie, the lower dice roll. If both fail, the GM decides: either a stalemate (nothing happens) or both roll again until there is a clear winner.

## Assistance

A character may assist another with a test (with GM permission): only the character performing the test rolls; each assisting character reduces the difficulty by one step. If the test succeeds, the performer gains an additional Degree of Success.

**Limits on Assistance:**
- For a Skill Test, the assisting character must be trained in the skill.
- The assisting character must be **adjacent** to the one performing the test.
- No Assistance on Reactions or Free Actions.
- No Assistance on tests against Disease, Poison, Fear, or anything else the GM deems inappropriate.
- No more than **two** characters may assist a single test.

## The Role of Fate

Every Explorer begins with a number of **Fate Points** determined at character creation. Spent Fate Points reset at the start of the next game session (or mid-session at the GM's discretion).

**Spending a Fate Point** is a Free Action, usable at any time (even as a Reaction), and allows one of the following options:
- Reroll a failed test once (the reroll's result is final).
- +10 bonus on a test (chosen before rolling).
- An additional Degree of Success (choosable after rolling).
- Count as a "10" result for Initiative.
- Immediately remove `1d5` Damage (has no effect on Critical Damage).
- Immediately recover from being Stunned.

**Burning Fate:** If a normal Fate Point expenditure is not enough to avert death, a Fate Point can be **burned** (permanent loss): the character survives what would have been a fatal event, but only barely (for example, a lethal lascannon hit instead leaves him badly burned and unconscious at 0 Wounds). A Fate Point can also be burned even if one has already been spent that same session.

**Starships and Burning Fate:** If a ship is destroyed, all Explorers aboard may **jointly** burn one Fate Point each to escape certain death — this only works if **every single** character aboard burns a Fate Point. The GM determines the exact manner of the rescue (typically a successful withdrawal from combat).

**Additional Fate Points** are awarded at the GM's discretion, for example at milestones or for exceptional heroics, cleverness, or good roleplaying.

## Combat: Basics

This section covers combat between individual characters/small groups. Ship/vehicle combat: Chapter VIII. Large battles with dozens/hundreds/thousands of combatants: Chapter X.

**Narrative Time vs. Structured Time:** Narrative Time gives only a rough sense of time (minutes, hours, weeks) and is mostly used outside of combat. Structured Time counts precisely in **Rounds**, **Turns**, and **Actions** and is used for combat and other time-critical situations.

- **Round:** Every participating character takes a Turn; a Round lasts about **5 seconds**, regardless of the number of participants.
- **Turn:** Each character gets one Turn per Round, in which they perform one or more Actions. Turns overlap slightly but are resolved in **Initiative Order**.
- **Actions:** A character may perform multiple Actions per Turn; the order can matter (e.g., drawing a weapon before shooting).

**Combat Abstractions:** The default difficulty for all Combat Tests is **Challenging (+0)**, unless a special rule states otherwise (all combatants are implicitly assumed to be constantly moving/seeking cover).

### Combat Sequence

**Step 1 – Surprise:** The GM determines at the start of combat whether characters are surprised (only possible once). A surprised character loses their Turn in the first Round. If no one is surprised, proceed directly to Step 2.

**Step 2 – Roll Initiative:** At the start of the first Round, every character rolls `1d10` + Agility Bonus (the tens digit of Agility). The result applies for all subsequent Rounds of the combat. The GM rolls for NPCs; a single roll suffices for a group of identical enemies.

**Step 3 – Determine Initiative Order:** The GM arranges all Initiative values in descending order; this is the order in which Turns proceed each Round. Ties are broken by higher Agility, then by a dice roll (higher first).

**Step 4 – Combatants Take Turns:** In order, each character becomes the *active character* and performs their Action(s).

**Step 5 – Round Ends:** Effects lasting "until the end of the Round" end here.

**Step 6 – Repeat:** Further Rounds until the combat ends. New combatants immediately roll their own Initiative and are slotted in.

**Tactical Combat Maps (optional):** Grid/hex maps or miniatures (1 inch = 1 metre) are an option, but not required — purely narrative description works just as well.

### Surprise (Detail)

The GM considers: a successful Concealment (possibly Opposed against Awareness), a successful Silent Move (possibly Opposed against Awareness), masking circumstances (rain, noise, explosions), and distractions. A surprised character loses their Turn in Round 1 entirely (standing about dumbfounded); attackers who are not surprised gain **+30** on WS/BS attack tests against surprised targets. After the Surprise Round, everyone rolls Initiative normally and combat proceeds as usual.

### Actions — Overview of Types

- **Full Action:** requires full attention; one Full Action per Turn, after which no Half Actions remain (example: Grappling).
- **Half Action:** two different Half Actions per Turn are possible (not the same one twice); example: readying a weapon, Standard Attack.
- **Reaction:** one per Round, only usable outside your own Turn (example: Dodge, Parry).
- **Free Action:** negligible effort, usable any number of times alongside other Actions (example: dropping an item, a short shout); the GM sets sensible limits.
- **Extended Action:** takes more than one Round; progress is lost if interrupted or aborted.

Per Turn: **one Full Action OR two different Half Actions.** Reactions always occur outside your own Turn, Extended Actions always span multiple Rounds.

### Detailed Action Descriptions

- **Aim:** Half Action = +10, Full Action = +20 on the next attack (Melee or Ranged). The bonus is lost if the next action is not an attack or if a Reaction occurs first.
- **All Out Attack:** +20 WS, but no Dodge/Parry possible until the character's next Turn.
- **Brace Heavy Weapon:** Heavy Weapons fired unbraced suffer -30 BS. If the weapon is braced, the shooter may not move further (a 45°+ swing, depending on the mount, remains possible). Melee/Thrown/Pistol/Basic Weapons gain no benefit from bracing.
- **Called Shot:** Announce a target location (Head/Body/Left Arm/Right Arm/Left Leg/Right Leg), Hard (-20) WS or BS; on success, the normal Hit Location roll is skipped and the called zone is hit.
- **Charge:** The target must be at least 4 m away but within Charge movement; the final 4 m must be in a straight line. +10 WS on the attack at the end of the Charge. Unarmed: a Grapple attempt instead of damage.
- **Defensive Stance:** no attacks of your own, but an additional Reaction until your next Turn; all opponents suffer -20 WS against this character.
- **Delay:** the Turn ends, but a Half Action is reserved for later and can be used any time up to the start of your next Turn (otherwise it is lost). If multiple characters want to use their delayed Half Action at the same time, an Opposed Agility Test determines the order.
- **Disengage:** leave melee + Half Move, without opponents gaining free attacks (see Fleeing).
- **Dodge:** Reaction, after a hit is scored but before damage is rolled; on success, the attack counts as missed. Works against both Melee and Ranged. Against area attacks (e.g., a flamer), a successful Dodge moves the character to the edge of the effect area, by no more than their Agility Bonus in metres — if that isn't enough, the Dodge automatically fails. Against Semi-/Full-Auto Bursts, each Degree of Success negates one hit.
- **Feint:** Opposed WS Test; if the attacker wins, their next attack against the same target cannot be dodged/parried — this is lost if the next action is not a Standard Attack.
- **Fleeing:** Voluntary flight allows Disengage, Move, or Run; forced flight (Fear, a Psychic Power, etc.) requires Run. Anyone who leaves melee without Disengaging takes a free Standard Attack from every opponent (in addition to their regular attacks).
- **Focus Power:** Action type varies per Psychic Power (see Chapter VI).
- **Full Auto Burst:** requires a fully-automatic-capable Ranged Weapon; +20 BS; 94–00 = Jam. Each Degree of Success scores an additional hit, limited by the weapon's Full-Auto RoF. Additional hits may be distributed to other targets within 2 m (no harder to hit than the main target). For multiple hits on the same target, Table 9-5 determines further Hit Locations, starting from the first (reverse-rolled) hit location. With Pistol/Basic class weapons, the shooter may also move up to their Agility Bonus in metres — but then without the BS bonus and with -10 BS.

### Table 9-5: Multiple Hits

Lookup table based on the location of the **first** hit (reverse-rolled, see "The Attack"):

| First Hit | 2nd Hit | 3rd Hit | 4th Hit | 5th Hit | Further Hits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Head | Head | Arm | Body | Arm | Body |
| Arm | Arm | Body | Head | Body | Arm |
| Body | Body | Arm | Head | Arm | Body |
| Leg | Leg | Body | Arm | Head | Body |

**Scatter + Full Auto Burst combined:** If fired at Point Blank Range, extra hits from `Scatter` and from the Full-Auto bonus are **calculated separately and both applied**.

### Grappling (Actions During an Ongoing Grapple)

Grappling is begun through its own action (details p. 246 in the original); the following Actions apply to an **already ongoing** Grapple.

**Controller Options** (the controller must re-declare `Grapple` as a Full Action first each Turn, or the Grapple ends immediately):
- **Damage Opponent:** Opposed Strength Test (Assistance possible); on a win, `1d5-3+SB` Damage (Primitive) to the Body plus 1 Fatigue Level; if the opponent wins instead, they remain grappled (no damage).
- **Throw Down Opponent:** Opposed Strength Test (Assistance possible); on a win, the opponent becomes prone.
- **Push Opponent:** Opposed Strength Test (Assistance possible); on success, push 1 m plus 1 m per Degree of Success in a chosen direction (up to the Controller's Half Move); the Controller must move along to maintain the Grapple, or let go (ending the Grapple, the Controller keeps their position).
- **Ready:** ready your own item, or at the GM's discretion, an item belonging to the opponent.
- **Stand:** if both are on the ground, the Controller may stand up and optionally, via an Opposed Strength Test (Assistance possible), pull the opponent up as well.
- **Use Item:** use a readied item.

**Grappled-Target Options** (the target must first declare `Grapple` as a Half Action):
- **Break Free:** Opposed Strength Test (Assistance possible); on a win, free and may perform any Half Action.
- **Slip Free:** Challenging (+0) Contortionist Test; on success, free and may perform any Half Action.
- **Take Control:** Opposed Strength Test (Assistance possible); on a win, the target becomes the Controller (and vice versa) and may immediately perform a Controller option, but no further Half Action.

**Size Differences:** If a grappler is larger (Size category, p. 249), they gain **one additional Degree of Success** per category difference on all Opposed Tests within the Grapple.

### Further Action Descriptions

- **Guarded Attack:** -10 WS, but +10 on Dodge and Parry until your next Turn.
- **Jump or Leap:** Full Action, Movement subtype; opponents engaged in melee receive a free Standard Attack when this is done.
- **Status Conditions (Brief Overview):**
  - *Helpless:* no actions possible; ends at the GM's discretion (waking up, being freed, etc.).
  - *Stunned:* no actions possible; ends after a fixed duration (usually 1–2 Rounds) or by spending a Fate Point.
  - *Unaware:* no actions possible; ends as soon as the character is attacked (regardless of whether the attack hits).
- **Knock-Down:** Half Action, Opposed Strength Test. Win = target prone (must use Stand to get up again); win with 2+ DoS additionally `1d5-3+SB` Damage (Armour counts double) plus 1 Fatigue. If the target itself wins the test by 2+ DoS, the attacker becomes prone instead. Moving first via a Half Action before the Knock-Down gives +10 on the test.
- **Manoeuvre:** Half Action, Opposed WS Test; on success, push the opponent 1 m in any direction (not into obstacles/other characters), optionally moving 1 m yourself as well.
- **Move:** Half Action = movement equal to Agility Bonus in metres; Full Action = twice that. If movement ends adjacent to an opponent, melee engagement applies; moving away from an engaged opponent triggers their free Standard Attack.
- **Multiple Attacks:** Full Action; requires the Swift Attack/Lightning Attack Talent or a second weapon (Two-Weapon Fighting).
- **Overwatch:** Full Action; defines a kill zone (45° arc in the direction faced) and a pre-declared action (Full Auto Burst, Semi-Auto Burst, or Suppressing Fire) along with a trigger condition; if the condition is met before your next Turn, the attack happens immediately. If two Overwatch actions trigger simultaneously, the higher Agility Bonus acts first (on a tie, an Opposed Agility Test). Targets in the kill zone must additionally pass a Hard (-20) Pinning Test or become Pinned. Any of your own Actions/Reactions (except Free Actions) ends your own Overwatch immediately.
- **Parry:** Reaction, Challenging (+0) WS Test with a parry-capable Melee Weapon; on success, the melee attack counts as missed. No Skill/Talent required, only works against melee attacks.
- **Ready:** Half Action; draw a weapon, retrieve an item from a pocket, apply a medi-patch, inject a drug, apply poison, etc. Possible twice in the same Turn if it involves two different weapons/items.
- **Reload:** Half/Full/Extended Action depending on the weapon (see Chapter V); reloading spread across multiple Rounds counts as an Extended Action.
- **Run:** Full Action, movement at the Run movement rate (Table 9-30); until your next Turn, -20 BS against Ranged attacks on the runner, but +20 WS for Melee attacks against them.
- **Semi-Auto Burst:** like Full Auto Burst, but +10 BS (instead of +20), Jam on 94–00, one extra hit per **two** Degrees of Success (instead of per 1), limited by the Semi-Auto RoF. Movement with Pistol/Basic class weapons is possible (up to Agility Bonus in metres), but then entirely without the BS bonus.
- **Stand/Mount:** Half Action; get up from the ground, or — if already standing — mount a steed/vehicle.
- **Standard Attack:** Half Action; a melee attack (WS Test) or a ranged attack (BS Test). Unarmed, a Grapple may be attempted instead.
- **Stun:** Attack (unarmed or with a Melee Weapon) with a Hard (-20) WS Test instead of normal damage. On success: the attacker rolls `1d10` + Strength, the target rolls `1d10` + Toughness Bonus + 1 per Armour Point on the head (unarmed or with a Primitive Weapon: Armour Points count double). If the attacker's value is equal to or higher, the target is Stunned for as many Rounds as the difference, plus 1 Fatigue Level.
- **Suppressing Fire:** Full Action, requires a fully-automatic-capable weapon. Defines a kill zone (45° arc) like Overwatch (or uses an existing one), immediately fires a Full Auto Burst (ammunition is expended). All targets in the zone: Hard (-20) Pinning Test or become Pinned. Additionally a Hard (-20) BS Test by the shooter determines whether anyone (friend or foe) in the zone is hit at all; 94–100 = Jam. On success, a random target in the zone is hit, with an additional random hit for every two further Degrees of Success (max. Full-Auto RoF); Cover/Armour apply normally. This test cannot be intentionally failed. Suppressing Fire is a distinct Full Action separate from Full Auto Burst and does **not** gain its +20 bonus.
- **Tactical Advance:** Full Action; move from one cover position to the next (up to a Full Move); remains protected by the cover left behind for the duration of the movement.
- **Use a Skill:** Half/Full/Extended Action depending on circumstances; usually a regular Skill Test.
- **Other, unlisted actions:** the GM decides the Action type and appropriate test (Characteristic/Skill/Opposed); a Round lasts only a few seconds.

## The Attack

Prerequisites: Melee attacks require melee engagement with the target; Ranged attacks are only possible in melee with Pistol-class weapons. The attacker must be aware of their target (see "Spray and Pray").

**Five-step sequence:**

**Step 1 – Apply Modifiers:** Melee = WS Test, Ranged = BS Test. Multiple bonuses/penalties are added together. **Maximum +60 bonus or -60 penalty** in total (regardless, some actions may simply be impossible).

**Step 2 – Roll the Test:** As usual; success = hit (subject to Dodge/Parry). A successful Dodge or Parry by the target completely negates the hit and damage.

**Step 3 – Determine Hit Location:** The digits of the (successful) roll are **reversed** (32→23, 20→02, etc.) and checked against Table 9-6.

### Table 9-6: Hit Locations

| Roll (Reversed) | Location |
|---:|---|
| 01–10 | Head |
| 11–20 | Right Arm |
| 21–30 | Left Arm |
| 31–70 | Body |
| 71–85 | Right Leg |
| 86–00 | Left Leg |

**Step 4 – Determine Damage:** Roll the weapon's Damage (+ modifiers); for Melee, also add Strength Bonus. The attacker may replace the result of **one** Damage die with their number of Degrees of Success (if there are multiple Damage dice, only one of them).

**Righteous Fury:** If any Damage die shows its natural maximum result (a natural 10 on a `d10`, likewise a natural 5 on a `d5`), a **second, identical attack roll** (same modifiers) is made. If this hits, an additional Damage roll is added to the total. If this Damage roll also shows the maximum result, the process repeats as long as at least one Damage die shows its maximum.

**Step 5 – Target Applies Damage:** Toughness Bonus and the Armour Points of the hit location are subtracted from the total Damage. Result ≤ 0: no effect. Remaining damage is recorded as Damage; if Damage reaches/exceeds Wounds, the excess is recorded as Critical Damage (table depends on Damage Type, hit zone, and Critical Damage amount, see "Critical Damage").

### Spray and Pray

If someone fires blindly into the dark (without being aware of a target), this is **not** a regular attack — the GM determines the plausible outcome situationally (e.g., a wild blind shot fired toward a known lurking monster plausibly hits it).

## Unarmed Combat

Requires melee engagement. Challenging (+0) WS Test, or Hard (-20) if the opponent is armed. On success: `1d5-3` Impact Damage + Strength Bonus; counts as Primitive. If the hit deals damage ≥ the target's Toughness Bonus, an additional 1 Fatigue Level. On Righteous Fury in Unarmed Combat, a 10 counts as a **5** for damage purposes. Unarmed attacks can be parried.

### Grappling (Starting One)

A Grapple attempt is a melee attack via Charge or Standard Attack (normal WS Test); the target may evade with a Reaction (e.g., Dodge). If the attack succeeds, both grapple, with the attacker in control. The Controller may end the Grapple at any time as a Free Action.

**While a Grapple is in effect:**
- Participants cannot use Reactions.
- Participants are considered engaged in melee.
- Participants may only perform the Grapple Action.
- Other attackers gain **+20 WS** against any target engaged in a Grapple.
- Only two characters can conduct the same Grapple, but up to two more may assist in certain situations (see Grapple Action options above).

## Two-Weapon Fighting

- Any one-handed Melee or Ranged weapons can be combined.
- Attacks with the **off-hand** suffer **-20** to WS/BS (without Ambidextrous).
- With the **Two-Weapon Wielder** Talent, the Multiple Attacks action is possible with both weapons, -20 per weapon (with **Ambidextrous** only -10).
- With at least one Melee weapon, a regular Parry Reaction remains possible per Round (not an attack, so **no** off-hand penalty).
- With Two-Weapon Wielder: a Melee weapon + pistol can be used simultaneously (Multiple Attacks; WS for melee, BS for the pistol, resolved separately).
- Two Ranged weapons may be fired with different fire modes (e.g., one Full-Auto, one Semi-Auto); with two fully-automatic weapons, only **one** Suppressive Fire zone may still be established at a time.
- Two different targets are possible, as long as they are within **10 metres** of each other.

## Combat Circumstances: Cover

Attacks against partially covered targets suffer **no** BS penalty, but the shot may instead strike the cover. The Explorer determines which body parts are hidden behind cover (rule of thumb: Body and Legs behind typical cover). If the hit would strike a covered location, the damage is first resolved against the **Armour Points of the cover**, with excess damage passing normally to the target.

### Table 9-7: Cover Examples

| Cover Type | Armour Points |
|---|---:|
| Armour-glas, Generatoria pipes, thin metal | 4 |
| Flakboard, storage crate, sandbags, ice | 8 |
| Cogitator bank, stasis pod | 12 |
| Rockcrete, hatchway, thick iron, stone | 16 |
| Armaplas, bulkhead, Plasteel | 32 |

**Damaging Cover:** Any hit against cover whose damage exceeds its Armour Points permanently reduces the cover's Armour Points by 1 (the excess itself does not additionally pass through).

| Hard | -20 | Shooting into melee combat. Dodging whilst Prone. Making an unarmed attack against an armed opponent. Melee attacks in darkness. Shooting at a target in fog, mist, shadow or smoke. Shooting a Puny target. Using a weapon without the correct Talent. |
| Very Hard | -30 | Attacking or Dodging in deep snow. Firing a heavy weapon that has not been Braced. Shooting a Minuscule target. Shooting a target at Extreme range. Shooting at a completely concealed target. Shooting at a target in darkness. |

- **Darkness:** WS Tests in darkness are Hard (-20), BS Tests are Very Hard (-30); while the character themself is concealed by darkness, Skill Tests (e.g., Concealment) are Routine (+20).
- **Difficult Terrain:** WS and Dodge Tests in difficult terrain (mud, etc.) are Difficult (-10); in arduous terrain (deep snow, ice) they are Very Hard (-30).
- **Engaged in Melee / Shooting into Melee:** BS Tests against a target engaged in melee are Hard (-20); if at least one melee participant is Stunned, Helpless, or Unaware, this penalty is dropped.
- **Stray Shots (optional rule):** If a shot into a melee narrowly misses its target (1 Degree of Failure or less), it instead hits another target in the same melee. With Semi-/Full-Auto Bursts, multiple hits may need to be distributed among different melee participants.
- **Extreme Range:** Target farther than three times the Weapon Range away: BS Test Very Hard (-30).
- **Fatigued:** all tests (including WS/BS) -10.
- **Fog, Mist, Shadow, or Smoke:** BS Tests against targets concealed within are Hard (-20); Concealment Tests within are Ordinary (+10).
- **Ganging Up:** a group outnumbering an opponent 2:1 → WS Tests Ordinary (+10); at 3:1 or more → Routine (+20).
- **Helpless Targets:** WS Tests against sleeping/unconscious/helpless targets succeed automatically. Damage is rolled **twice** and added together; if either roll shows a natural 10, the normal Righteous Fury chance applies; if **both** show a natural 10, Righteous Fury occurs automatically (no second attack roll needed).
- **Higher Ground:** an attacker on elevated terrain: WS Tests Ordinary (+10).
- **Long Range:** target farther than twice the Weapon Range away: BS Test Difficult (-10).
- **Missing / Scatter (for thrown weapons):** On a failed BS Test, the GM rolls `1d10` on the Scatter diagram for direction and `1d5` for the number of metres the weapon flies in that direction. In Zero Gravity: roll twice (X and Y axes).
- **Pinning:** With Suppressive Fire (even from covered/harmless hits), a Hard (-20) Willpower Test (Pinning Test) must be passed, or the character becomes **Pinned**. A Pinned character may only perform Half Actions, suffers -20 to all BS Tests, may not leave covered positions except to retreat (further into cover), and must — if uncovered — seek cover on their next Turn or move away from the attacker if no cover is reachable. At the end of a Turn, a Willpower Test may be attempted to escape Pinning (Easy (+30) if no one has fired on them since the last Turn). Melee engagement automatically ends Pinning. Certain Talents, Skills, Psychic Powers, drugs, or intimidating commissars can also free a character from Pinning.
- **Point Blank Range:** target ≤ 2 m away: BS Test Easy (+30) (does not apply if attacker and target are engaged in melee). For weapons with Short Range < 3 m, Point Blank Range is 1 m less than their Short Range.
- **Prone:** WS Tests against prone targets are Ordinary (+10); BS Tests against them are Difficult (-10), except at Point Blank Range. A prone character themself suffers -10 to WS and -20 to Dodge Tests. Lying down is a Free Action, unless engaged in a Grapple.
- **Short Range:** target closer than half the Weapon Range: BS Test Ordinary (+10).
- **Size:** see Table 9-9.
- **Stunned Targets:** WS/BS Tests against Stunned targets are Routine (+20).
- **Unaware Targets:** WS/BS Tests against unaware targets are Easy (+30) (typically at the start of combat for Surprised characters).
- **Weapon Jams:** an unmodified result of 96–00 is an automatic failure **and** a Jam. A jammed weapon cannot fire until cleared (Full Action, BS Test); on success it is cleared, but the weapon must be reloaded and the remaining ammunition is lost; on failure it remains jammed (may be attempted again next Round). Some weapons (plasma, grenades, rockets) have their own, more dangerous special rules (Chapter V). Semi-/Full-Auto fire and Suppressing Fire further increase the Jam chance (see the respective action).
- **Weather and Unnatural Conditions:** WS/BS Tests under adverse weather/unnatural conditions (heavy rain, ash storms, knee-high fungal growth) are Hard (-20).

## Injury

### Wounds and Damage

**Wounds** are a threshold, not a shrinking pool — they do not decrease from injuries. Damage taken is instead recorded as **Damage** and is cumulative until removed by natural healing or medical treatment (p. 262).

Incoming damage is reduced by: **Toughness Bonus** (always, unless stated otherwise) plus the **Armour Points** of the hit, armoured location (if any). Some damage sources explicitly ignore Armour and/or Toughness. As long as Damage ≤ Wounds, the body functions normally. Once Damage exceeds Wounds, **Critical Damage** begins.

**Damage Types:** Energy, Explosive, Impact, Rending. No stated type = Impact. The type primarily determines which Critical Effect table applies.

### Critical Damage

Once total Damage exceeds Wounds, the excess is recorded as **Critical Damage**, and a Critical Effect is read from the appropriate table (by hit zone Arm/Body/Head/Leg and Damage Type) — indexed by the **current Critical Damage total**. Critical Damage is likewise cumulative; with each further Critical Damage increase, the effect is redetermined based on the new total.

**Sudden Death Critical Damage (optional rule):** For nameless henchmen/minions, the GM may rule that they die instantly on any Critical Damage instead of resolving the full table. Not intended for Explorers or important villains.

### Fatigue

Fatigue measures non-lethal exhaustion, measured in **Levels**. A character can endure as many Fatigue Levels as their **Toughness Bonus** without suffering more than the flat penalty. Each Level of Fatigue (starting from the first) gives **-10 to all tests**. If Fatigue exceeds Toughness Bonus, the character is knocked unconscious for **`10 - TB` minutes** (minimum sensibly positive); afterward their Fatigue drops back to a value equal to their Toughness Bonus.

**Reducing Fatigue:** Every hour of normal rest (no combat, no Psychic Powers, no strenuous activity; unconsciousness does not count) removes 1 Level; **8 consecutive hours** of rest removes all Fatigue Levels.

### Characteristic Damage

Some Psychic Powers, poisons, diseases, and other hazards bypass Wounds entirely and damage a Characteristic directly. Characteristic Damage cannot reduce a Characteristic below 0 and also affects the associated Characteristic Bonus.

**Removal:** Unless stated otherwise, Characteristic Damage is **not permanent** — automatic recovery of 1 point per hour, regardless of activity. The GM may allow medical treatment to speed this up.

## Conditions and Special Damage

In addition to normal damage, the following conditions may arise:

- **Amputated Limbs:** Loss of a body part (other than the head = death) causes Blood Loss and must be treated. Without the Medicae Skill, there is only a 20% chance the stump heals on its own (otherwise death from infection after `1d10` days). Regardless of treatment, Damage healing does not begin until `1d10+2` days have passed. Follow-on effects: **Hand Lost** — `-20` on two-handed tests, no two-handed weapons, `-20` on attacks with the off-hand if the primary hand was affected (can be reduced by 100 XP per -10). **Arm Lost** — as Hand, but no shield can be equipped. **Eye Lost** — BS permanently `-10`, `-20` on sight-based tests; both eyes = blind. **Foot Lost** — movement permanently halved (round up), `-20` on movement Actions and mobility-based tests. **Leg Lost** — as Foot, plus Dodge is no longer possible.
- **Blinded:** automatic failure of all sight- and BS-based tests, `-30` to WS and other sight-dependent tests.
- **Blood Loss:** 10% chance per Round of dying if untreated. If conscious: Difficult (-10) Medicae Test per Round to stop the bleeding (Very Hard (-30) if simultaneously performing another strenuous action); otherwise another character may attempt the test.
- **Deafened:** automatic failure of all hearing-based tests.
- **Fire:** At the start of each Round (from the second onward) in contact with fire: Challenging (+0) Agility Test or catch fire. While burning: `1d10` Damage (no armour reduction) plus 1 Fatigue per Round; all fire damage counts as Energy Damage for Critical Effects. To act: Challenging (+0) Willpower Test per Turn (otherwise only running about/screaming as a Full Action). Extinguishing: go prone + Hard (-20) Agility Test as a Full Action.
- **Falling:** `1d10` + 1 per metre of fall height, counts as Impact Damage; armour does not protect. Hit zone per Table 9-6.
- **Stunned:** WS/BS Tests against Stunned targets are Routine (+20); no Actions/Reactions possible; not identical to Helpless/Unaware.
- **Suffocation:** Holding breath for TB minutes (at rest) or 2×TB Rounds (exertion); afterward a Challenging (+0) Toughness Test per minute/Round, otherwise 1 Fatigue. If Fatigue > TB: unconscious for `10-TB` minutes. Without a new oxygen source once this expires, automatically unconscious; unconscious and without oxygen: `1d10` Damage/Round (unreduced) until death.
- **Unconsciousness:** default duration `10-TB` minutes unless stated otherwise; counts as Helpless, no actions possible.
- **Useless Limbs:** a Challenging (+0) Toughness Test (with Medicae support +20) prevents permanent loss; on success, the limb is in a sling and useless for `1d5+1` weeks. On failure: amputation is required (Difficult (-10) Medicae Test; if this fails, an additional `1d10` Critical Damage to the limb).
- **Vacuum:** Sudden vacuum: TB Rounds unharmed, then `1d10+3` Explosive Damage/Round from decompression (armour ineffective) plus Suffocation; in space, additionally a Challenging (+0) Toughness Test per Round against `1d10` Energy Damage from cold. A dead body freezes after 5 Rounds and shatters on further damage. Gradual vacuum: 2×TB Rounds unharmed, then a cumulative `-10` Toughness Test/Round: success = `1d5` Explosive Damage, failure = `1d10` Explosive Damage (unreduced by armour).

## Healing

A character passes through three states depending on accumulated Damage:

- **Lightly Damaged** (Damage ≤ 2×TB): natural healing of 1 Damage/day; with a full day of bed rest, TB Damage.
- **Heavily Damaged** (Damage > 2×TB): natural healing of 1 Damage/week; with a full week of rest, TB Damage. If Damage drops to ≤ TB, the character becomes Lightly Damaged again.
- **Critically Damaged** (Damage > Wounds): does not heal on its own — requires medical treatment, 1 point of Critical Damage per week of care. After full healing, the character becomes Heavily Damaged.

Medical treatment (Medicae Test, medicines, psychic powers) can speed healing or provide instant healing (see Medicae Skill, p. 83). Rules for cybernetic/bionic replacement limbs can be found in Chapter V: Armoury.

## Exploration

### Climbing

- **Simple Climbs** (fences, hills, trees): automatically successful with free hands, except under time pressure/distraction/attack — then a Strength or Climb Test, standard Challenging (+0). Success: half Half-Move speed (round up) + 1m per DoS. Failure: fall from the starting position.
- **Sheer Surfaces** (smooth walls, overhangs): always requires a Climb Test, usually Challenging (+0), depending on the surface. Same success/failure rules as above. Equipment (Chapter V) provides bonuses.
- **Abseiling:** Challenging (+0) Agility Test — success: 10m/Round; failure: 5m/Round; failure by 2+ degrees: Challenging (+0) Strength Test or fall (automatic fall without a climbing harness).

### Jumping and Leaping

- **Standing Vertical Jump:** about 0.5m high without a test; reach for climbing over = height + ~1m arm length + 0.5m. Pulling up on a grasped edge: Challenging (+0) Strength Test. Safe jump down up to AB metres: Challenging (+0) Agility Test — success = no damage; at greater distance, Falling Damage for the excess, landing prone. Failure: full Falling Damage, prone.
- **Running Vertical Jump:** with a running start (at least 4m) — Challenging (+0) Agility Test; success: + half SB (round up) + 0.5m per DoS; for every further 4m of run-up, +10 to the test (max. +30).
- **Standing Horizontal Leap:** Difficult (-10) Agility Test — success: SB metres + 0.5m per DoS; failure: half SB (round up), each degree of failure further reduces this distance by another half metre (minimum 0.5m). Jump height = ¼ of the distance (round up).
- **Running Horizontal Leap:** with a run-up, Challenging (+0) Agility Test, otherwise as above; run-up bonus as for the vertical jump.

### Swimming

Under normal circumstances no test is required; under hazardous conditions (rough water, bound hands, fighting) a Challenging (+0) Swim Test as a Full Action — success: move up to half SB in any direction (or tread water); failure: no progress. Armour/weight: Swim Tests become Very Hard, with automatic 1 Fatigue. Unconscious/paralysed characters automatically go under. **Narrative Time:** TB hours of swimming without issue, then a Toughness Test/hour (cumulative -10), failure = 1 Fatigue; an unconscious character in water risks drowning (Suffocation).

### Lighting

Three levels of light: **Bright** (normal vision), **Shadow** (twilight, the edge of light sources, fog), **Darkness** (absence of light). Movement through Shadow is unpenalised; through Darkness only at half speed or less — faster only with a Hard (-20) Perception Test, otherwise random drift. Combat effects, see Darkness (p. 247) and Fog/Mist/Shadow (p. 248).

### Flying

Three altitude levels (on Earth-like worlds): **Hovering** (up to 2m, melee and ranged combat possible to/from ground units), **Low Altitude** (beyond melee range but within most ranged weapon ranges — ground shooters suffer -10 BS against Low Altitude targets), **High Altitude** (beyond the range of anything except other High Altitude fliers). Changing altitude: 1 level per Move Action, 2 levels with Charge/Run. Flying/hovering requires a Move Action each Round, otherwise a crash: from Hovering unharmed, from Low Altitude as a 15m fall, from High Altitude as a 25m fall (or more, at the GM's discretion). Traits **Hoverer** (max. 2m altitude) and **Flyer** (any altitude) from Chapter XIV.

### The Effects of Gravity

- **Low Gravity:** AB +2 for movement/Initiative; SB+TB sum +4 for carrying/lifting/pushing limits; SB +2 for throwing distance; jumping/leaping distances doubled.
- **High Gravity:** AB -2 (min. 1); SB+TB sum -4 (min. 0); SB -2 for throwing distance (min. 0); jumping/leaping distances halved.
- **Zero Gravity:** counts as Difficult Terrain (movement halved); Charge/Run requires a Hard (-20) Agility Test or uncontrolled drifting (treat as falling/prone); movement requires contact with a solid surface to push off from.

**Gaining and Losing Profit Factor:** There are three things that affect Profit Factor:

- **Endeavours:** major undertakings by the PCs — the main way in which Profit Factor is increased.
- **Misfortunes:** setbacks. Profit Factor is not normally expended when used (it represents wealth and power far too great to be dented by ordinary purchases), but it can be reduced by Misfortunes.

### Acquisition

An Explorer who wants to acquire equipment/resources makes an **Acquisition Test**: `1d100` against their PF (like a Characteristic Test), modified by the Availability, Craftsmanship, and Scale of the object. Success = the acquisition is secured; failure = the item remains out of reach for now.

- **Automatic Success/Failure:** if bonuses raise the effective PF to 100+ before rolling, the test automatically succeeds; if penalties reduce PF to 0 or below, the test automatically fails.
- **Combining Acquisitions:** for combined items (e.g., a weapon + accessory), use the greatest Availability penalty among the components as the base, with an additional -5 for each further component.
- **Character Creation:** a starting character may choose, once, a single item with a total modifier of +0 or better without making a test (weapons only with the matching Weapon Training Talent).
- **Unscrupulous Traders:** the GM can shift the total modifier by -30 to +30; the Evaluate Skill helps to gauge the true modifier.
- **Commerce and Acquisition:** an Explorer can make an Opposed Test of Commerce (their own) vs. Commerce/Scrutiny (the other party) before the Acquisition Test — each degree of victory gives ±2 PF for that one test (Command/Charm/Barter may substitute for Commerce situationally).
- **Consumables** (ammunition, drugs, grenades): once acquired, a "Steady Supply" applies — replenished on return to the ship. Alternative: **Rule of Three** (a flat three reloads/grenades/drug doses per type).
- **Starships and Components:** for components, Table 9-36 is used in place of Scale (modifier by system type: Etheric, Drive/Power, Structure, Weapons/War); when acquiring an entire ship, the modifier equals the negative Ship Point value of the hull (see Chapter VIII), with no further modifiers except from a Commerce Test.
- **Other Acquisitions** (resources, favours, trophies): Availability per Table 9-38 (Acquisition Rarity: Ubiquitous through Abundant/Plentiful/Common/Average/Scarce/Rare/Very Rare/Extremely Rare/Near Unique/Unique), Craftsmanship per Table 9-37 (Poor/Common/Good/Best), Scale per "A Matter of Scale" (Negligible to Vast). Unique/Near-Unique objects get no Scale bonus.
- **Upkeep Tests:** on consumption, damage, attacks on property, or setbacks, the GM may call for an Upkeep Test (like an Acquisition Test). On failure: discard the item (reacquire only at a higher PF), lower Craftsmanship by one level, lower Scale by one level, or overstretch PF (-5 to all further Acquisition Tests until PF rises or the item is given up/downgraded).

### Influence

**Influence Test:** an Opposed Test of the Explorer's own PF against the PF of the person/organisation to be influenced (see Table 9-3: Test Difficulty for GM modifiers). For each degree of victory: +5 on Interaction Skill Tests with that party for the remainder of the current encounter (or longer, at the GM's discretion); for each degree of defeat: -5. Alternatively, as a one-off roll to try to convince the person or organisation to grant a favour, free passage, or important information.

## Endeavours

An **Endeavour** is a major undertaking (exploration, trade, conflict, etc.) intended to increase the Explorer group's PF — the main way to increase Profit Factor.

### Structure of an Endeavour

- **Requirements:** necessary prerequisites (resources, a ship, allies, etc.), some already at hand, some still to be acquired.
- **Size:** **Lesser** (1–2 sessions, +1–2 PF), **Greater** (several sessions, +3–4 PF), **Grand** (many parts/obstacles, +5+ PF) — as a guideline; the GM can scale freely.
- **Objectives:** typically 3 sub-goals that must be achieved in sequence.
- **Achievement Points:** a total point value depending on the Endeavour's size (Table 9-39: Endeavour Scale), divided among the Objectives (evenly or weighted). Points are awarded for overcoming challenges (Table 9-40: Achievement Point Awards, staggered by difficulty). Surplus points within an Objective carry over to the next; for every 100 surplus points at the end of the entire Endeavour, +1 additional PF.
- **Objective Themes:** Military, Criminal, Exploration, Trade, Creed — determine tone and relevant Talents/Ship Components.
- **Execution:** either in the foreground as a full adventure, or in the background during downtime through NPCs/subordinates (usually slower and less profitable).
- **Failing an Objective** does not necessarily prevent overall success — missing points can potentially be compensated for by the remaining Objectives.

## Misfortunes

Misfortunes are setbacks that reduce PF — the counterpart to Endeavours. These are plot hooks and obstacles representing events that damage the Explorers' Profit Factor, which the GM can use to simulate the vast and varied interests, allies, and influence of the Explorers' dynasty, either as a way of representing an event (player-generated or not) that harms the dynasty's power and wealth, or as part of complications arising around an Endeavour. When the GM feels the Explorers are at risk of a Misfortune, he can roll on **Table 9-41: Misfortunes** to determine if one occurs and its severity. To keep players on their toes, the GM may choose to roll once on this table at the start of any session in which they are involved in one or more Endeavours.

If a Misfortune occurs, regardless of its size, the GM then rolls on **Table 9-42: Misfortune Details** to find the nature of the problem. Alternatively, the GM can choose whether a Misfortune occurs and its details to reflect his own plots.

**Overcoming Misfortunes:** When a Misfortune occurs, the Explorers can either deal with it or ignore it. It then falls to the GM to decide if they acted swiftly and decisively enough to overcome the Misfortune; if so, he should allow them to recover any Profit Factor lost to it.
