## Availability

Every item and every service has an **Availability** ranging from Ubiquitous to Unique. Whether something is actually on offer locally also depends on the Technology Level; the GM may make the search harder, or make acquisition impossible outright.

Finding an item requires a successful **Commerce or Inquiry Test**. The baseline applies to settlements of up to 1,000 inhabitants. Against this baseline, the Difficulty is increased by one step for settlements of 100 or fewer inhabitants, reduced by one step for up to 10,000 inhabitants, and reduced by two steps for 100,000 or more inhabitants.

### Availability and Time

The time given covers both the search and the closing of the deal by experienced servants, factors, and negotiators. In dangerous circumstances the GM should roll the test secretly. An assisting PC can reduce the time by one step (for example months to weeks, or weeks to days) with a **Challenging (+0) Commerce Test** or another skill the GM deems suitable. Manufacture, transport, and installation may take further time once the purchase itself is complete.

## Craftsmanship

Unless stated otherwise, items and service providers are of **Common Craftsmanship**. For goods, quality differences outside the Weapon and Armour rules given later are mostly descriptive; the GM may ease Tests made with particularly good equipment. For services, Craftsmanship can represent the skill, diligence, and loyalty of retainers.

## Wealth, Profit Factor, and Ammunition

Local currencies range from coins and ledger-data to ammunition, precious metals, slaves, or xenos trade goods. **Throne Gelt** serves as the imperial reference currency in the Calixis Sector. Rogue Traders, by contrast, manage their means through **Profit Factor**; the actual Acquisition rules are found in Chapter IX, pp. 270–272.

Acquiring a weapon includes an ongoing supply of its standard ammunition. On expeditions a character may carry as much as deemed necessary and resupply at the operations base. Unusual Ammunition and upgrades are acquired separately.

## Weapons

### Profile Values and Classes

- **Melee:** used in Close Combat; Strength Bonus is added to Damage.
- **Thrown:** thrown by muscle power; Strength Bonus is added, except for Explosives. Without an additional Melee class, thrown weapons count as Improvised Weapons in Close Combat.
- **Pistol:** one-handed and usable in Close Combat. In melee no Range or Targeting bonuses/penalties apply; Scatter counts as Point Blank but does not gain the +30.
- **Basic:** normally two-handed; one-handed at -20.
- **Heavy:** always two-handed and must be braced. Without Brace, -30 applies and Semi-Auto/Full Auto are not possible.

**Range** is the normal range; Short Range is half, Long Range double, and maximum range quadruple (bonuses/penalties p. 247). **RoF** lists Single/Semi-Auto/Full Auto and ammunition consumption; a "—" means that mode is not possible. Some weapons have multiple RoF modes; the firing mode is chosen before the attack. **Damage** uses E (Energy), X (Explosive), R (Rending), or I (Impact). **Pen** reduces Armour Points, not Fields, but does reduce Cover. **Clip**, **Rld**, **Special**, **kg**, and **Availability** complete the profile.

### Weapon Craftsmanship

- **Poor ranged:** gains Unreliable; if the weapon was already Unreliable, it jams on every failed Hit Roll.
- **Poor melee:** -10 to Attack and Parry.
- **Good ranged:** gains Reliable. Unreliable and Reliable cancel each other out.
- **Good melee:** +5 to Attack Tests.
- **Best ranged:** never jams or overheats; a roll that would trigger this counts only as a Miss.
- **Best melee:** +10 to Attack Tests and +1 Damage.

## Weapon Special Qualities

- **Accurate:** additional +10 BS when Aiming. A Single Shot fired from a single Accurate Basic Weapon while Aiming deals `1d10` extra Damage per 2 Degrees of Success, to a maximum of `2d10`.
- **Balanced:** +10 to Weapon Skill Tests made to Parry.
- **Blast (X):** all targets within X metres are hit; roll Hit Location and Damage separately for each target.
- **Customised:** Reload Time is halved and rounded up to the nearest full Action; a Half Action remains a Half Action.
- **Defensive:** +15 when Parrying, -10 when Attacking.
- **Flame:** a 30° cone out to Range; no BS Test needed. Targets test Agility or are hit. Anyone who takes Damage tests Agility again or catches fire. Cover offers no protection. If the shooter lacks the appropriate Weapon Training Talent, targets in the area of effect gain +20 to their Agility Test; if the weapon is additionally an unbraced Heavy Flame Weapon, the bonus rises to +30. Hits always strike the Body; a 9 on a Damage Die causes a Jam.
- **Flexible:** cannot be Parried.
- **Inaccurate:** Aiming grants no bonus.
- **Overheats:** on an Attack Roll of 91+, the wielder suffers the Weapon's Damage as Energy, Pen 0, to the arm holding it (or a random arm if used two-handed), or may drop the weapon as a Free Action. The following Round is spent cooling down; the weapon can only be fired again the Round after that. Jam effects cause an Overheat instead.
- **Power Field:** increases the weapon's Damage and Pen; these bonuses are already included in the profile. When successfully Parrying a weapon without a Power Field, there is a 75% chance of destroying it. Warp Weapons and Natural Weapons are immune.
- **Primitive:** Armour Points are doubled against the hit unless the Armour is also Primitive; Pen is subtracted only afterwards.
- **Recharge:** requires a full Round to recharge after each shot; effectively fireable only every second Round.
- **Reliable:** on a Jam, roll `1d10`; only on a 10 does it actually Jam, otherwise it is a Miss.
- **Scatter:** at Point Blank, every second Degree of Success scores an additional hit. At Long/Extreme Range, AP are doubled against the hit.
- **Shocking:** if the hit causes at least 1 Damage after Armour and Toughness, the target tests Toughness with +10 per AP at the Hit Location. On a failure it is Stunned for a number of Rounds equal to half the Damage suffered.
- **Smoke:** creates a Smokescreen `3d10` metres in diameter at the point of impact, lasting `2d10` Rounds, shorter in bad weather.
- **Snare:** a hit target tests Agility or is immobilised and counts as Helpless. On its Turn it may only attempt to break free using Strength or Agility.
- **Storm:** doubles the number of hits scored, up to the weapon's normal RoF limit.
- **Tearing:** roll an additional Damage Die and discard the lowest result.
- **Toxic:** after dealing at least 1 Damage, the target tests Toughness at -5 per point of Damage. Failure causes `1d10` Impact Damage immediately, ignoring Armour or Toughness.
- **Twin-linked:** +20 to Hit, double ammunition consumption, an additional Hit on 2 or more Degrees of Success, double Reload Time.
- **Unbalanced:** -10 when Parrying.
- **Unreliable:** Jams on 91+ even during Semi-/Full Auto fire.
- **Unstable:** after a Hit, roll `1d10`: 1 = half Damage, 2–9 = normal Damage, 10 = double Damage.
- **Unwieldy:** cannot Parry.

### Weapons Without the Matching Talent

Without the appropriate Weapon Training, a -20 penalty applies to the relevant Test; Ranged Weapons additionally count as Unreliable. For Flame weapons the target instead gains +20 to its Agility Test.

### Recharging Power Packs (Las Weapons)

Las Power Packs can be recharged on-site from most energy sources: a **Tech-Use Test** is sufficient if a suitable source is available; charge time depends on that source's output and is typically several hours (GM's discretion). Alternatively a pack can be recharged over an open fire — this takes at least a day, **permanently** halves the Clip size the first time it is done, and removes the `Reliable` Quality (or grants `Unreliable`, if the weapon previously had neither Reliable nor Unreliable). Every recharge done this way also carries a **30% chance** of rendering the pack permanently unusable.

### Supplementary Weapon Rules and Patterns

The individual descriptions of the weapons listed in Table 5-4 are condensed here to their rules-relevant statements; origin, appearance, and typical users are flavour and are not repeated individually.

- **Hand Cannon:** without a two-handed grip or Recoil Glove, -10 to Ballistic Skill Tests.
- **Lascarbine:** when fired one-handed, only -10 applies instead of the usual -20 for Basic Weapons.
- **Shotgun Pistol:** without a two-handed grip or Recoil Glove, -10 to Ballistic Skill Tests (recoil).
- **Naval Pistol:** loses the `Tearing` Quality without its special ammunition; this ammunition cannot be combined with other special types.
- **Naval Shotcannon:** must be mounted or fired from a braced position.
- **Plasma – Maximal Mode:** +10 m Range, +1d10 Damage, and consumes 3 shots. The weapon gains `Recharge`; if it has `Blast`, its value increases by 2.
- **Sling with Grenade:** replaces Damage with the grenade's effect, Range drops to 7 m; a Jam forces an additional roll following the grenade misfire rules (p. 126).

## Grenades and Missiles

Grenades are thrown using Ballistic Skill (no special Talent required). Their Range is three times the Strength Bonus; Launchers use their own profile. On a missed throw, Scatter rules determine where the grenade lands (p. 248).

**When a grenade "jams":** when throwing a grenade or firing one from a launcher, a Jam can occur instead of a normal failure. Roll `1d10`: on any result except 10 the charge is a dud, and nothing happens. On a 10 it detonates immediately at the location of the thrower/shooter. If it was fired from a launcher, it detonates in the barrel, with normal effect **and** destruction of the weapon.

## Exotic Weapons

Exotic Weapons are extremely rare, sometimes of xenos manufacture, and sometimes do not even fit a human hand. Each type requires specialised training and must be mastered individually with its own Talent (`Exotic Weapon Training`).

**Digital Weapons (Digi-Weapons):** miniature weapons worn like a ring on a finger, but possessing the effect of a full-sized pistol. Each weapon can only be fired **once**; reloading is extraordinarily difficult. A character may wear up to **one Digi-Weapon per finger** (not the thumb), and a Digi-Weapon can be fired **even if the hands are occupied with other things**. Digi-Weapons may be used in melee like pistols.

## Armour

Armour Points (AP) apply only to the covered Hit Locations. Armour with AP 7+ causes -30 to Concealment and Silent Move.

- **Flak:** if the wearer is not hit directly at the point of impact of a Blast Weapon, Flak counts as AP 5.
