# Chapter III: Skills

> Source: Rogue Trader Core Rulebook, Chapter III, book pages 73–88 (PDF pages 77–92).

## Gaining Skills

New Explorers start with Skills from their **Home World** and **Career Path**. Further Skills are bought with Experience Points; which Skills are available and when is determined by the Career Path (Chapter II).

### Training and Skill Mastery
- On the **first** purchase, a Skill is considered **trained**.
- On the **second** purchase of the same Skill: **Skill Mastery** → **+10** to all Tests using that Skill.
- On the **third** purchase: the Skill Mastery bonus rises to **+20**.
- A Skill can be purchased **at most three times**.

## Basic and Advanced Skills

- **Basic Skills** can be attempted by anyone, even untrained. Untrained, the test uses **half** the governing Characteristic (rounded down); trained, it uses the **full** value.
- **Advanced Skills** require specific training and **cannot** be attempted untrained.
- **Treating Advanced Skills as Basic Skills:** Some Traits (usually from the Origin Path) allow an Advanced Skill to be used as though it were a Basic Skill — that is, Tests against **half** the governing Characteristic even without training. This is different from actual training in that Advanced Skill.

## Skill Descriptors

Some Skills fall into broad categories with shared rules: **Crafting, Exploration, Interaction, Investigation, Movement, Operator**.

- **Crafting Skills:** Creating items from parts or raw materials. Requires materials, tools, a workspace, time and skill (GM's decision). The Skill Test is an **Extended Action**. On success the item is created; quality, workmanship and usefulness increase with each Degree of Success. **Failure by 3 degrees** = part of the material is ruined; **failure by 5+ degrees** = the entire batch of material is ruined.
- **Exploration Skills:** usable in **Exploration Challenges** (p. 263) — long, difficult tasks best handled as a group effort.
- **Interaction Skills:** dealing with others, so usually **Opposed Tests** (p. 293). Difficulty depends on the disposition of the target (see Chapter X).
- **Investigation Skills:** gathering information; often require **Extended Tests** (durations per p. 264).
- **Movement Skills:** everything related to physical agility — leaping across gaps, climbing, moving through/over/between the environment.
- **Operator Skills:** piloting means of transport, from ground vehicles to starships.

### Skill Groups
Some Skills comprise groups of related Skills. Each specialisation must be **acquired and used separately** — Pilot (Flyer) and Pilot (Space Craft) are two different Skills.

## Skill Descriptions

### Acrobatics (Advanced, Movement) — Agility
Supplements natural agility with feats less athletic people would not even consider (leaping between gantries in the enginarium, vaulting over smaller foes). Difficulty depends on the feat; more Degrees of Success give a more stylish/dramatic result.
**Skill Use:** Full Action unless otherwise noted.
- **Special Use — Disengage:** When performing the Disengage Action in combat, a successful Acrobatics Test can reduce the Action to a **Half Action**.
- **Special Use — Jump & Leap:** Acrobatics replaces the Agility Test for jumping and the Strength Test for leaping (rules p. 266).

### Awareness (Basic, Exploration) — Perception
Subconsciously reacting to things that escape the conscious mind: ambushes, traps, hidden objects, subtle changes in the environment (vibration in the deck plating, the smell of the air recyclers).
Against an opponent the Test is always **Opposed** (ambushes, traps, anything involving another's actions). Pure environmental perception (scent trails, movement) is a **Standard Test**.
**Skill Use:** Free Action as a reaction.

### Barter (Basic, Interaction) — Fellowship
Negotiating better prices, trading goods and services. Can alter the **Acquisition Modifier** where the price is not fixed but negotiable (not the Promethium price at an Imperial Guard depot, but very much haggling with House Dimico over hand-crafted bolters). **All Barter Tests are Opposed.** Can support Acquisition Tests (p. 271).
**Skill Use:** about five minutes; delicate negotiations as an Extended Test.

### Blather (Advanced, Interaction) — Fellowship
Buying time through confusing or distracting chatter. Always **Opposed against the target's Willpower**, or against **Scrutiny** if the target actively suspects the tactic. Victory = the opponent remains bewildered and inactive for the whole Round; **one further Round per Degree of Success**. On defeat, everyone acts normally.
Without special Talents, up to **Fellowship Bonus** many targets can be affected. With similar values, the GM may roll a single Willpower Test for the whole group.
Opponents do **not** remain inactive when facing obvious danger or recognisable attack preparations. The character and target must share a **common language**, or the attempt fails automatically.
**Skill Use:** Full Action.

### Carouse (Basic) — Toughness
Resistance to alcohol and narcotics. Test whenever the Explorer would suffer the effects of alcohol or similar intoxicants. Failure = one degree of **Fatigue** or the substance's side effects. If rendered unconscious, he remains senseless for **1d10 minus Toughness Bonus** hours (minimum 1 hour).
**Skill Use:** Free Action on consumption.

### Charm (Basic, Interaction) — Fellowship
Improves others' opinion of the Explorer. Test only when changing an attitude or persuading someone of something. **Always Opposed against Willpower**; affects up to **Fellowship Bonus** many targets, who must be able to clearly see and hear him and share a common language.
**Skill Use:** 1 minute.
- **Special Use — Inspire:** Someone with authority can use Charm to boost a group-related test (positively or negatively). Success gives **+10** to the next Skill Test of the motivated group. Aboard a starship in combat, this is the **Put Your Backs Into It!** action (p. 218). As an Extended Test it can also be used to improve morale aboard ship (p. 226).

### Chem-Use (Advanced, Investigation) — Intelligence
Safely identifying, handling and preparing chemicals, toxins, poisons and drugs. (**Manufacturing** these from base components falls under Trade (Chymist).) Success = correctly applied; failure = the dose is wasted with no effect. With a **Medicae Injector or Narthecium**, administering a drug or antidote becomes a **Half Action**. Complex combinations = Extended Test.
**Skill Use:** Full Action.

### Ciphers (Advanced) — Intelligence
**Skill Groups:** Rogue Trader, Mercenary Cant, Nobilite Family, Astropath Sign, Underworld.
Secret codes for quickly conveying simple ideas, warnings or information: hand signals, physical markings, subtle signs. Simple messages need **no Test**; Tests are only needed for complex meanings or for signs weathered by time.
- **Rogue Trader:** every trader, ship and fleet develops its own code.
- **Mercenary Cant:** the terse command language of the Expanse's mercenary companies — each essentially unique.
- **Nobilite Encoding:** Navigator families use ancient, partly dead languages; each family has its own.
- **Astropath:** a specialised language shaped by the Soul Binding, with levels reaching down to thought-whispers.
- **Underworld:** the codes of crime lords, highly developed and differing by organisation.
**Skill Use:** Full Action.

### Climb (Basic, Movement) — Strength
Ascending/descending ropes, pipes, natural and artificial walls — **not** for ladders and the like. Surface condition and environment give bonuses/penalties. Success = movement at **half of half** the movement rate.
**Skill Use:** Half Action.

### Command (Basic) — Fellowship
Leading subordinates and triggering group actions (e.g. "battle stations"). Only affects people under the Explorer's authority. Success = orders are followed promptly.
Failure with a single person = they do not comply; further Degrees of Failure = they undertake the task without intending to carry it out, misunderstand it, or act contrary to it. With groups, Degrees of Success/Failure change the time needed; **3+ Degrees of Failure** = confused inaction.
Affects up to **Fellowship Bonus** many targets; they must see and hear (also via vox/pict-caster) and share a common language.
**Skill Use:** Half Action for simple orders, Full Action for more complex instructions.

### Commerce (Advanced) — Fellowship
Building business ventures, negotiating contracts, forming trade networks. Can be Opposed: against the counterpart's **Commerce** in negotiations, or against a gullible mark's **Scrutiny** when swindling them. Also usable as a Standard Test (running a trade venture without an opponent). **The main Skill for using Profit Factor for Acquisitions** (p. 271).
**Skill Use:** variable (see Acquisitions, p. 271) — from seconds to months.

### Common Lore (Advanced, Investigation) — Intelligence
**Skill Group:** Adeptus Arbites, Adeptus Astra Telepathica, Adeptus Mechanicus, Administratum, Ecclesiarchy, Imperial Creed, Imperial Guard, Imperial Navy, Imperium, Koronus Expanse, Navis Nobilite, Rogue Traders, Tech, War.
General information, procedures, traditions, famous figures and superstitions of a world, group, organisation or race. Difference from **Scholastic Lore** (scholarly knowledge) and **Forbidden Lore** (hidden/forbidden knowledge): Common Lore is basic knowledge from long contact with a culture. The GM determines what additional information further Degrees of Success provide.

| Specialisation | Content |
|---|---|
| Adeptus Arbites | weapon types and sub-sects of the Arbites, rank structure, procedures, principles of Imperial justice |
| Adeptus Astra Telepathica | recruitment/training of psykers, deployment of Astropaths, basics of astro-telepathic messages |
| Adeptus Mechanicus | symbols and practices, hierarchy, ranks, existence of the Sixteen Universal Laws |
| Administratum | labyrinthine procedures, rules, traditions and regulations |
| Ecclesiarchy | structure of the Adeptus Ministorum and its role in Emperor worship |
| Imperial Creed | rites, practices and figures of the Imperial Cult, common customs, festivals, holy days |
| Imperial Guard | rank system, logistics, structure, basic tactical/strategic practice, famous regiments |
| Imperial Navy | ranks, customs, uniforms, traditions, famous admirals and ships |
| Imperium | Segmenta, sectors and the best-known worlds |
| Koronus Expanse | general astrography of the Expanse, known Warp routes, regions, legends |
| Navis Nobilite | size, customs, dress and defining personalities of the Navigator families |
| Rogue Traders | the most famous Warrants of Trade, their holders and ships |
| Tech | understanding of simple litanies and rituals to placate machine spirits |
| War | great battles, notable commanders, heroes and famous stratagems of the Imperium |

**Skill Use:** Free Action.

### Concealment (Basic) — Agility
Hiding things — from small objects through vehicles to starships, or objects on one's own body. Requires a suitable environment for cover (buildings and trees for a small shuttle, an asteroid field or spatial anomaly for a starship). **Always Opposed** against the opponent's **Awareness or Scrutiny**. Anything/anyone that remains completely **motionless** gains **+10**.
**Skill Use:** Half Action.

### Contortionist (Basic, Combat) — Agility
Uses innate flexibility to escape bonds, squeeze through too-narrow openings, or fit into too-small spaces. Also an alternative to raw strength when grappling.
**Skill Use:** Full Action unless otherwise noted below.
- **Escape Bonds:** Opposed Test, Contortionist against the binder's **Intelligence** Test. The quality of the bonds and time spent affect difficulty. Duration **1 minute**, **−10 seconds** per Degree of Success.
- **Escape Grapple:** Opposed Test against the grappler's **Strength** Test. Success = freed, failure = still held.
- **Squeeze Through:** squeezing through tight spaces (maintenance shafts, warped bulkhead gaps) or stuffing oneself into a too-small space. The GM sets the difficulty by size; some spaces are simply too small. Failure = doesn't get through/in. **4+ Degrees of Failure** = stuck, until another Contortionist Test succeeds or someone pulls them out. Duration **1 minute**, **−10 seconds** per Degree of Success.

### Deceive (Basic, Interaction) — Fellowship
Deceiving others about one's true intentions: lies, half-truths, twisted information. Test only when the deception is meant to change an opinion, attitude, or action. **Always Opposed** against the target's **Scrutiny**. Affects up to **Fellowship Bonus** many targets, who must clearly see and hear (also via vox/pict-caster) and share a common language.
**Skill Use:** 1 minute, more for complex deceptions.
- **Special Use — Con:** A ship's master or officers can use deception (exaggerated battle reports, hints about what Ork Freebooters do to prisoners) to temporarily raise **crew morale in combat**. Outside combat, the Skill reduces the morale impact of long Warp passages. **Each failure gives a cumulative −10** to further attempts, since the audience sees through the deceptions (p. 293).

### Demolition (Advanced, Crafting) — Intelligence
Using the right amount of explosives for the desired effect — from a Land Raider's hatch to destroying a plasma conduit without damaging the reactor behind it. Also defusing others' explosive devices and manufacturing explosives (time fuses, detonators). Applies to **placed charges**, not grenades and thrown weapons in combat.
**Skill Use:** Full Action unless otherwise noted.
- **Manufacture Explosives:** making explosives from base components; **Extended Test**, difficulty and duration per complexity and material.
- **Place Explosives:** Success = the charge, along with the chosen trigger (tripwire, timer, proximity sensor, remote detonator), is correctly placed. Failure = the device fails to detonate when triggered (the character only notices then). **4+ Degrees of Failure** = it detonates on him! Larger tasks (mining a building, blowing a cargo bay) = Extended Test. **Note the total Degrees of Success** — they count in the Opposed Test against anyone defusing it.
- **Defuse Explosives:** Opposed Test against the layer's Demolition Test. Victory = defused. Simple failure = neither defused nor detonated. **4+ Degrees of Failure** = detonation with all consequences. Multiple charges/complex detonators = Extended Test.

### Disguise (Basic) — Fellowship
Concealing one's own features, up to assuming another's appearance. Difficulty per available material, difference from the target appearance and other factors. **Opposed against Scrutiny.** Simplest disguise: **1 minute**; elaborate deceptions up to weeks or months (prosthetics, surgery, xenos mannerisms).
**Skill Use:** 1 minute or more.

### Dodge (Basic, Combat) — Agility
As a **Reaction**, cancel a successful melee or ranged attack. Success = attack avoided, no damage (details Chapter IX).
**Skill Use:** Reaction.

### Drive (Advanced, Operator) — Agility
**Skill Groups:** Ground Vehicle, Skimmer/Hover, Walker.
Piloting ground-, hover- or skimmer-based vehicles (Cargo-8, Rhino, Land Speeder, Sentinel, etc.). Normal driving requires **no** Test — only dangerous conditions, excessive speed or risky manoeuvres do.
**Skill Use:** Half Action.

### Evaluate (Basic, Investigation) — Intelligence
Determining the approximate value of an object or group of objects (a single Power Sword or an entire cargo hold full of lasrifles). Success reveals the **Availability Modifier** (p. 272); further Degrees of Success give more information. Failure = misjudgement, the size of the error growing with the Degrees of Failure. **The GM should roll Evaluate Tests** and only communicate what the Explorer believes he knows.
Difficulty depends on access to the object and the time the seller allows. Large cargoes = Extended Test; individual items about a minute. At the GM's discretion, also usable to determine the requirements of **Endeavours** (p. 277).
**Skill Use:** 1 minute or more.

### Forbidden Lore (Advanced, Investigation) — Intelligence
**Skill Groups:** Adeptus Mechanicus, Archeotech, Daemonology, Heresy, the Inquisition, Mutants, Navigators, Pirates, Psykers, the Warp, Xenos.
Knowledge that is typically hidden, obscured, or outlawed by an organisation/society. **Mere possession of this knowledge can be dangerous for outsiders** — too much knowledge of the hidden truths of powerful Navigator houses is decidedly unhealthy outside the Navis Nobilite. Success = basic information; the GM reveals more depending on the Degree of Success.

| Specialisation | Content |
|---|---|
| Adeptus Mechanicus | deep understanding of the followers of the Machine God: rituals, customs, tenets, core philosophies, the Universal Laws |
| Archeotech | the great, lost devices of past eras and clues to their function |
| Daemonology | knowledge of the most notorious Warp entities and their twisted physical manifestations |
| Heresy | acts and practices deemed heretical, history's most despicable heretics and their deeds |
| The Inquisition | the Imperium's secret organisation, its principles and famous Inquisitors |
| Mutants | stable and unstable mutations, their cancerous influence, mutagenic development, studies and books on the subject |
| Navigators | secret knowledge of the Navis Nobilite families, their breeding programmes, common mutations, leading patriarchs |
| Pirates | the scourge of the Warp routes, their tactics, notorious ships and inhuman captains |
| Psykers | recognising psykers, the physical effects of their powers, their danger and range of ability |
| The Warp | Warp energy, its interaction with realspace, the influence of tides and eddies on star travel |
| Xenos | lesser and greater alien species known to the Imperium, their threat and appearance |

**Skill Use:** Free Action … even though the consequences of such knowledge can last a lifetime.

### Gamble (Basic) — Intelligence
Games of chance, popular among the officers and crews of trading fleets. Each participant wagers (usually equal amounts) and makes an **Opposed Test**. Whoever has the most Degrees of Success or fewest Degrees of Failure wins the pot. The Explorer may deliberately lose to a worse-placed player (as part of a Deceive action).
Anyone who has both Skills may use **Sleight of Hand instead of Gamble** to hide cards or load dice: success gives **+20**, **4+ Degrees of Failure** = caught cheating.
**Skill Use:** Full Action for a whole day's play.

### Inquiry (Basic, Investigation) — Fellowship
Gathering information through questions, conversation, or simple eavesdropping. Provides general information about an area: news, recent events and more; additional Degrees of Success bring more detailed or secret news.
Also usable as an Investigation Skill to hunt down a specific piece of information (e.g. in the ship's librarium) — practically always an **Extended Test**, difficulty and duration set by the GM (p. 264).
**Skill Use:** 1 hour or more.
- **Special Use — Hunt for Sedition:** Questioning the crew for the discontented, shirkers and mutineers. Success raises **Morale by 1d5** by removing negative influences — **but for every point of Morale gained, Population drops by 1**, as the seditious elements are purged.

### Interrogation (Advanced) — Willpower
Extracting information from an unwilling subject. Different from torture (where the victim will say anything to make it stop): skilled application of psychology, devices, sera and techniques. The GM adjusts difficulty for available tools, facilities and circumstances.
**Always Opposed** against the target's **Willpower**. Victory = **one answer plus one more per Degree of Success**. Defeat = nothing useful. **2+ Degrees of Failure**: 1d10 + Willpower Bonus damage to the target, and no further questioning for **1d5 days**. **4+ Degrees of Failure**: the same damage, and the subject gains **+30** on Willpower Tests against Interrogation by the Explorer or his allies. **Every Interrogation Test causes one degree of Fatigue** in the target.
**Skill Use:** 1d5 hours.

### Intimidate (Basic, Interaction) — Strength
Applying pressure so someone gives in to demands. Test only for actual coercion. Even though **Strength** is the associated Characteristic, subtler methods (blackmail, humiliation) may use **Intelligence or Fellowship** instead.
Affects up to **Strength, Intelligence, or Fellowship Bonus** many targets (depending on the Characteristic used); they must clearly see and hear (also via pict-/vox-caster) and share a common language.
**Skill Use:** Full Action.

### Invocation (Advanced) — Willpower
Full Action. For the duration of the Round, the Explorer frees his mind of outside influences and focuses his will (mantras, psy-foci, meditation). Success = **+1 to the final Psy Rating on a Fettered Power Test** in the **next** Round.
Failure = **−1** to the final Psy Rating on a Fettered Power Test; if this reduces the Psy Rating to zero, **the power fails**.
**Skill Use:** Full Action.

### Literacy (Advanced) — Intelligence
Reading and writing any language the Explorer speaks. Everyday material requires no Test; regional variants, damaged manuscripts, archaic usage or colloquial expressions do.
**Skill Use:** 1 minute per page of text (about 750 words).

### Logic (Basic) — Intelligence
Solving problems, deciphering puzzles, handling situations through evidence and inference (deducing a missing symbol in a sequence, solving an unwieldy equation, preparing a reasoned argument for a debate). Complex reasoning = Extended Test.
**Skill Use:** 1 minute, longer for especially complex problems.

### Medicae (Advanced) — Intelligence
Diagnosing and treating injuries: stitching, antiseptics, medical devices like the Narthecium. For individual patients, Medicae provides **First Aid** (short-term) and **Extended Care** (long-term). For larger groups of wounded, the Skill saves those who can be saved and eases the end of the others; it also helps recognise and prevent common medical problems.
- **Special Use — First Aid:** A successful Medicae Test removes damage equal to the **Intelligence Bonus** for **Lightly** Wounded characters, or **1 point of damage** for Heavily or Critically Wounded ones. **Skill Use:** Full Action.
- **Special Use — Extended Care:** speeds healing. Up to **Intelligence Bonus** many patients can be treated; **each additional patient gives a cumulative −10**. For Lightly Wounded: one Test at the end of **each day**; for Heavily/Critically Wounded: one Test at the end of **each week**. Success = the patient removes **double** the normal healing rate of damage (Critical Damage first) **plus 1 point per Degree of Success**. Failure causes no harm (normal healing rate). **2–3 Degrees of Failure** = all Lightly and Heavily Wounded patients each suffer **1 damage** (Critical Damage via Sudden Death, p. 250). **4+ Degrees of Failure** = all patients suffer **1d10 damage**, Critical Damage via Sudden Death.
- **Special Use — Diagnose:** recognising diseases and afflictions, in Explorers as well as crew. For individuals, a success gives the name of the ailment and basic treatment. For groups, a success prevents **losses in Crew Population** from disease or malnutrition on long voyages.

### Navigation (Advanced, Exploration) — Intelligence
**Skill Group:** Surface, Stellar, Warp.
Calculating a course between two points — across a continent or through the tides of the Empyrean. Success also gives an estimated travel time based on geography, cosmography, conditions, weather and Warp currents.
- **Surface:** navigation across a planet's surface with a logi-compass, map readings and geographical knowledge.
- **Stellar:** navigation through space between planets with star charts and carto-mantic rituals.
- **Warp:** the Navigator's ability to steer a ship through the tides of the Empyrean, using the Astronomican as a reference. Determines **how quickly** a ship reaches its destination — and under especially difficult conditions, **whether it does at all** (Warp Travel, p. 183).
A Navigation Test represents several hours of course-charting; for simple location-finding, one minute is enough as long as the ship is not in the Warp.
**Skill Use:** 1 minute for simple location; 1d5 hours for plotting courses or routes.

### Performer (Advanced) — Fellowship
**Skill Groups:** Dancer, Musician, Singer, Storyteller.
Entertaining and enthralling groups of spectators. Time required depends on the art form.
**Skill Use:** variable.
- **Charming Performance:** A **Difficult (−10) Performer (Storyteller) or (Singer) Test** replaces Charm to win round the audience (disposition improves by one level) or to temporarily raise **crew Morale**. When used for Morale: success gives **+5 Morale**, plus **+10 per two Degrees of Success**, for the duration of the combat.
- **Enthralling Performance:** A **Difficult (−10) Performer (Musician) or (Singer) Test** replaces a Blather Test and briefly enthralls/distracts the audience. The same rules and conditions as Blather apply.

### Pilot (Advanced, Operator) — Agility
**Skill Groups:** Personal, Flyers, Space Craft.
Piloting anything — from a personal jump pack through small atmospheric craft (landers, gun-cutters) to void-faring fighters, bombers and capital ships. Under normal conditions **no** Test is needed; storms, obstacles or dangerous manoeuvres require one. When chasing another vehicle/ship or contesting position: **Opposed Pilot Test**. Full starship rules are in Chapter VIII.
**Skill Use:** Half Action.

### Psyniscience (Advanced) — Perception
Sensing the currents and eddies of the Warp: the presence or absence of daemons, the use of psychic powers, psychic phenomena, disturbances, voids, or areas where the flow of the immaterium has been disrupted.

| Degrees of Success | Result |
|---|---|
| Standard Success | Awareness of an immaterium disruption, or the number of entities present |
| One | Approximate direction of the phenomena or creatures |
| Two | Rough location of the Warp creatures or beings affecting the immaterium |
| Three + | Exact position of the creatures or psykers present |

**Skill Use:** Full Action.
- **Special Use — Astropathic Interference:** Astropaths can use Psyniscience to block the communications of other Astropaths. **Opposed Test**; the interfering Astropath suffers **−10 per Void Unit** of distance to the target. Success = the blocked Astropath's ship cannot send or receive astropathic transmissions.

### Scholastic Lore (Advanced, Investigation) — Intelligence
**Skill Groups:** Archaic, Astromancy, Beasts, Bureaucracy, Chymistry, Cryptology, Heraldry, Imperial Warrants, Imperial Creed, Judgement, Legend, Navis Nobilite, Numerology, Occult, Philosophy, Tactica Imperialis.
Knowledge of particularly complex or esoteric subjects. Success = recalling necessary information, or researching a subject if appropriate reference material is available. Considerably deeper than Common Lore; requires both experience **and** study.

| Degrees of Success | Result |
|---|---|
| Standard Success | Basic knowledge known to scholars of the subject |
| One | Unusual information, known only to a few dedicated academics |
| Two | Obscure information, known only to serious scholars |
| Three + | Extremely rare information, known only to true experts in the field |

Scholastic Lore Tests **require no time** — the Explorer either knows it or doesn't. **Research**, on the other hand, is an Extended Test with duration and difficulty per the task (p. 231).

| Specialisation | Content |
|---|---|
| Archaic | the Imperium's dark past, outlawed eras, humanity's changes across the millennia |
| Astromancy | stars, singularities and the worlds around them; theoretical use of telescopes, astrolithic charts and the like |
| Beasts | genera and families of animals, characteristics and appearance of many semi-sentient creatures |
| Bureaucracy | the rules and regulations of governments, particularly the Adeptus Administratum, its departments and policies |
| Chymistry | chemicals, their alchemical applications, and their prevalence or rarity in the Imperium |
| Cryptology | codes, ciphers, cryptographs, secret languages and numeric keys — both creating and decoding them |
| Heraldry | the principles and coats of arms of heraldry, common seals and heraldic devices of the noblest houses and families |
| Imperial Warrants | the establishment, legal scope and use of Rogue Trader Warrants; the most famous dynastic Warrants |
| Imperial Creed | specific rites and practices of the Ecclesiarchy, from traditional temple construction to scriptural passages; allows performing the rites |
| Judgement | appropriate punishments for the countless crimes and heresies punishable under Imperial law |
| Legend | beyond archaic knowledge: the most secret parts of Imperial history — Dark Age of Technology, Age of Strife, Great Crusade, Horus Heresy |
| Navis Nobilite | family trees, contracts and history of the great Navigator houses |
| Numerology | the mysterious connection between numbers and the physical universe, from Catastrophe Theory to the Sadleirian Litany |
| Occult | occult rituals, theories and superstitions, plus the better-known mystical uses of occult objects |
| Philosophy | theories on thought, belief, existence and other imponderables; includes logic and debate |
| Tactica Imperialis | the theories of the Tactica Imperialis and other systems of troop deployment and Imperial combat technique; for devising battle plans or predicting Imperial warfare |

**Skill Use:** Free Action.

> Note: Scholastic Lore overlaps somewhat with Common Lore and Forbidden Lore but is deeper and more academic. Common Lore (Navis Nobilite) knows the Navigator families and their role; Scholastic Lore (Navis Nobilite) traces a family's lineage and historical contracts across millennia.

### Scrutiny (Basic) — Perception
Assessing people and objects: judging a person's honesty and motives, gauging personality and temperament; closely examining objects and noticing details that escape a cursory look. On starship auger returns, the Skill determines mass, speed and more.
**Opposed Test against Deceive** when trying to detect lies or deceptions. Scrutiny reveals **no** hidden secrets or carefully concealed intentions and should not replace good roleplaying. The Skill counters the Opposed Tests of many manipulative Interaction Skills (Charm, Deceive, Intimidate).
Aboard starships, this Skill operates and interprets the ship's sensors (Chapter VIII).
**Skill Use:** Full Action; special applications may take longer.

### Search (Basic, Exploration) — Perception
Finding physically hidden things, from a hidden holdout pistol to a shuttle's access code. Search is **active** investigation, whereas Awareness is passive/subconscious perception. Each Search Test covers a small room or area. If something was deliberately hidden, the Test is **Opposed against Concealment**.
**Skill Use:** 1 minute.
- **Special Use — Inspection:** finding hidden stashes, smuggled goods, stowaways and signs of sabotage on vehicles and starships. Depending on size, the GM may turn this into an Extended Test.

### Secret Tongue (Advanced) — Intelligence
**Skill Groups:** Administratum, Ecclesiarchy, Military, Navigator, Rogue Trader, Tech, Underdeck.
Understanding a particularly obscure, arcane language known only to a specific class or organisation. Some resemble codes/ciphers more than languages, some use a common tongue in unusual ways. As long as **all speakers** know the secret tongue, **no Test** is needed; adverse conditions (poor vox connection) or complex concepts require one.
A group of Explorers can even **develop their own Secret Tongue** — available as an **Elite Advance**.

| Specialisation | Content |
|---|---|
| Administratum | acronyms, jargon and procedural litanies of the Adeptus Administratum — some extremely long-winded |
| Ecclesiarchy | allegorical language born of devotion and politics, with religious metaphors and passages of holy text in archaic High Gothic |
| Military | coded phrases, jargon, hand signals, references to old battles and a surprising number of expressions for death |
| Navigator | archaic, dead tongues and dialects kept alive only by the old Nobilite families |
| Rogue Trader | agreed code phrases and inflections, understandable only to members of the same ship/fleet |
| Tech | coded binary cant with high- and low-frequency sound waves, occasionally optical pulses |
| Underdecks | a rough variant of Low Gothic from the underhives, carried into the bilges and lower decks of most ships; full of colourful slang terms |

**Skill Use:** Free Action.

### Security (Advanced, Exploration) — Agility
Overcoming mechanical locks and other physical security systems (codes and cogitators fall more under Tech-Use). The GM sets difficulty by complexity; usually one Test suffices, large interlinked systems require Extended Tests. **Without a suitable tool** (multikey or similar), **−20** applies.
**Skill Use:** 1 minute, **−10 seconds** per Degree of Success.

### Shadowing (Advanced) — Agility
Following others on foot, in vehicles, or in starships. The counterpart to Concealment, since it requires movement and blending into the surroundings: cover or crowds on foot, wrong turns or using a cargo hauler as visual cover in a vehicle, asteroids or busy space corridors in a ship. **Always Opposed against Awareness or Scrutiny.**
A single Test is enough to follow an opponent unnoticed for **one minute**.
**Skill Use:** 1 minute.

### Silent Move (Basic, Movement) — Agility
For anything requiring quiet and stealth. Difficulty depends on environment (echoing steel corridors of a cargo bay are harder than wood-panelled, carpeted officers' quarters). **Always Opposed against Awareness or Scrutiny.**
**Skill Use:** Free Action as part of movement.

### Sleight of Hand (Advanced) — Agility
Tasks combining deception and dexterity: making small objects disappear, pickpocketing, tricks. Difficulty depends on object size and observers' attentiveness. **Always Opposed against Awareness or Scrutiny.** Can be used instead of Gamble to manipulate games of chance.
Normally a **Half Action**; possible as a **Free Action** with **−10**.
**Skill Use:** Half Action.

### Speak Language (Advanced) — Intelligence
**Skill Groups:** Eldar, Explorator Binary, High Gothic, Low Gothic, Ork, Techna-Lingua, Trader's Cant.
Communicating with other speakers of the same language. As long as everyone involved shares a common tongue, **no Test** is usually needed; obscure dialects or cryptic, complex concepts require one.

| Language | Content |
|---|---|
| Eldar | no human grasps the fine undertones of this extremely complex, ancient language — but it suffices to make oneself understood |
| Explorator Binary | very similar to Techna-Lingua but drifted apart; the Mechanicus tolerates it but frowns on its use in official discourse. Questors and field operatives use it to keep conversations secret from their planet-bound brethren |
| High Gothic | the official language of the Imperium: officials, nobility, the Ecclesiarchy and high negotiations |
| Low Gothic | the common tongue of the Imperium, spoken by countless billions of ordinary citizens |
| Techna-Lingua | the official language of the Adeptus Mechanicus; a binary language optimised for rapid exchange of technical data and servitor commands |
| Trader's Cant | used by many Rogue Traders among themselves; allows quick negotiations and exchanges |

**Skill Use:** Free Action.

### Survival (Advanced, Exploration) — Intelligence
Surviving for extended periods in unusual or alien environments: finding edible plants, hunting, judging food safety, building shelters from natural or improvised materials and placing them away from flash-flood channels and predator territories. Difficulty depends on location (a barren desert is much harder than a lush tropical forest).
Also applies to **artificial** environments — hive-worlds, the depths of the underhive, the belly of vast starships: finding safe rest areas away from plasma vents, knowing which sacred unguents provide minimal nutrition.
**Skill Use:** Full Action.

### Swim (Basic, Movement) — Strength
Swimming in liquid media. Under normal conditions **no** Test; more difficult waters, unusual circumstances or great distances require one (p. 267).
**Skill Use:** Full Action.

### Tech-Use (Advanced, Exploration) — Intelligence
Using and repairing complex mechanical items, as well as figuring out unknown technical artefacts. Simple equipment under normal circumstances requires **no** Test (operating a vox-caster, opening a shuttle hatch). Tests are needed for unusual/unknown equipment, broken or malfunctioning devices, and when conditions are unfavourable (operating a vox-caster near a plasma core, coaxing the machine spirit of an alien Warp drive to ignite).
**Repairs** are Extended Tests (duration/difficulty per complexity and extent of damage). Tech-Use does **not** cover building from scratch — that requires the appropriate **Trade** Skill.
Tech-Use can also **direct Servitors**; here it functions identically to Command.
**Skill Use:** 1 minute, more for more complex systems.
- **Special Use — Security Systems:** Security covers mechanical locks, but many systems have both physical **and** electronic components, or are entirely controlled by cogitators and machine spirits. The GM determines the necessary Tests — a combination of both Skills, or a single roll with a modifier for possessing or lacking the other Skill.

### Tracking (Advanced, Exploration) — Intelligence
Following the trail of quarry. Obvious tracks require **no** Test and count as part of movement. Tests are needed when time or weather have eroded the signs, or the environment makes seeing difficult (darkness, fog). Difficulty depends on the condition of the tracks and the environment. **If the difficulty is worse than Challenging (+0), the tracker's movement rate drops to half.**
If the quarry has tried to erase its tracks: **Opposed Test against their Concealment**.
Using the Investigation and Exploration rules (p. 263), information about the quarry can be deduced: elapsed time, numbers and race.
**Skill Use:** Free Action as part of movement.

### Trade (Advanced, Crafting, Exploration) — depends on specialisation
**Skill Groups (with Characteristic):** Archaeologist (Int), Armourer (Ag), Astrographer (Ag), Chymist (Int), Cryptographer (Int), Explorator (Int), Linguist (Int), Remembrancer (Ag or Int), Shipwright (Int), Soothsayer (Fel), Technomat (Int), Trader (Fel), Voidfarer (Ag).
Creating things — from contracts to starships. At the GM's discretion, Explorers with this Skill can earn **additional Profit Factor** by plying their trade. They recognise the work of particularly famous or infamous craftsmen and recall information about items of their trade.
Trade Tests can represent hours, weeks, or months; **examining an item to recall knowledge** is a **Full Action**.

| Specialisation | Content |
|---|---|
| Archaeologist | locate, examine and analyse ancient ruins and artefacts |
| Armourer | design, upgrade and forge weaponry — from personal arms to starship batteries |
| Astrographer | create two- and three-dimensional representations of stellar locations and Warp routes |
| Chymist | create poisons, drugs and a wide variety of other compounds |
| Cryptographer | create or decode ciphers, codes and other puzzles |
| Explorator | used in the exploration of unknown stellar regions |
| Linguist | learn or decipher new languages, both spoken and written |
| Remembrancer | recount events in a variety of art forms, from sculpture to poetry |
| Scrimshawer | inscribe patterns, text and art onto materials |
| Shipwright | design, upgrade and create void-capable vessels |
| Soothsayer | "foretell" the future through a number of interpretative arts, though its effectiveness is suspect |
| Technomat | maintain and repair technological devices, though through rote memorisation rather than true understanding |
| Voidfarer | used in the day-to-day operation, logistics and defence of starships |

> **Printed list discrepancy:** the Skill Group line in the original lists
> **Trader (Fel)** but does not include **Scrimshawer**. Conversely, the
> individual descriptions immediately following include **Scrimshawer**
> while there is no separate description for **Trader**. Both are preserved
> here exactly as printed on book page 88.

**Skill Use:** variable depending on task and complexity.

### Wrangling (Advanced) — Intelligence
Taming, training and, where possible, riding animals — from spine-hounds to noble warhorse breeds. Taming and training are **Extended Tests**, duration set by the creature's nature and temperament.
Once an animal has been broken to its master's will — or a partnership has formed with particularly strong-willed beasts — directing or commanding it is a **Half Action**.
For wild animals, or those trained for hostility and aggression, a **Full Action** changes disposition by **one level per two Degrees of Success**, up to a maximum of **three levels**. GMs may impose penalties for animals that are particularly well trained or have uncommon Willpower and Intelligence.
**No use** against cyber-animals, psy-bonded animals, or any creature with true sentience.
**Skill Use:** variable depending on task and complexity.
