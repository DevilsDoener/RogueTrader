## Chapter XIV: Adversaries & Aliens (Introduction)

The galaxy of the 41st Millennium is a strange and limitless place, full of
terror and wonder alike. Equally varied are the individuals, creatures, and
alien races within it. Explorers in the Koronus Expanse and beyond encounter
mortal foes, powerful allies, and human rivals as well as unnatural beasts
and the most abhorrent horrors imaginable. The trick, of course, lies in
knowing who is who…

## Traits

Skills and Talents represent abilities and knowledge acquired over the
course of a character's life. Traits, on the other hand, are innate
abilities gained through birth and racial circumstance. They can also be
acquired later by other means, though almost never through conscious
choice. As described in Chapter I: Character Creation, player characters
acquire certain Traits as a result of their Home World, the fate revealed
through their Origin Path, and later through Madness, Corruption, and
possible Mutations. Other beings in the ROGUE TRADER universe also possess
Traits — characteristics inherited from their particular species or their
environment.

### Trait Descriptions

The following are descriptions of the individual Traits.

#### Auto-stabilised

The creature always counts as braced when firing Heavy Weapons, and may
fire in Semi- or Full-Automatic mode as a half action. A creature with this
Trait suffers no penalties for moving and firing in Semi- or Full-Automatic
mode at the same time.

#### Bestial

A creature with this Trait is animalistic and acts on instinct rather than
reason. No matter how high its Intelligence, it remains bestial. A creature
with this Trait never needs to make a Survival Test in its natural habitat.
Unless starving or desperate, a bestial creature must make a Willpower Test
when frightened, startled, or injured. On a failed Test, the creature
flees.

#### Blind

Blind creatures automatically fail all sight-based Tests as well as all
Ballistic Skill Tests. Such creatures suffer a −30 penalty on Weapon Skill
Tests and most other Tests that normally require or benefit from sight.

#### Brutal Charge

A creature with this Trait deals an additional 3 points of damage on
attacks made during a charge.

#### Burrower

A creature with this Trait can move through solid objects by digging
through them. This Trait always includes a number indicating speed. Such
creatures can dig through earth, rock, sand, and so on, and some (as noted
in the creature's description) can also dig through metal. When a creature
burrows, it leaves a tunnel behind it. Creatures at least one Size category
smaller than the burrowing creature can enter these areas unhindered. There
is a 50% chance each round that the tunnel collapses behind the creature.

#### Crawler

Worms, snakes, and similar creatures crawl and slither rather than walk. A
creature with this Trait has a base Movement equal to half its Agility
Bonus (rounded up), but it suffers no penalties for moving over difficult
terrain.

#### Daemonic

Daemonic creatures are creatures of the Warp, though they can manifest in
realspace through sheer force of will, hunger, or occult ritual. Creatures
with this Trait double their Toughness Bonus against any damage — except
damage from Force Weapons, psychic powers, holy attacks, or other creatures
with this Trait. Daemonic creatures are also immune to poison and disease.

#### Dark Sight

A creature with this Trait sees normally, even in areas of total darkness,
and never suffers a penalty for fighting in areas of poor or no
illumination.

#### Hoverer

Creatures with this Trait possess limited flight and never fly higher than
two metres. As with the Flyer Trait, this Trait always includes a number
indicating speed. See Chapter IX: Playing the Game for further details on
flying.

#### Incorporeal

Incorporeal creatures are immaterial and weightless and can pass through
solid objects such as walls and weapons. An incorporeal creature gains a
+30 bonus on Concealment Tests when hiding in something. It also
automatically passes any Silent Move Test it must make.

An incorporeal creature is also immune to normal weapons — these simply
pass through its body as if it were not there. Daemons, psychic powers,
Warp creatures, other creatures with the Incorporeal Trait, and foes with
Force Weapons can, however, harm an incorporeal creature normally. An
incorporeal creature normally cannot affect the mortal world and thus
cannot harm non-incorporeal foes, unless otherwise stated.

Incorporeal creatures cannot pass through certain devices or barriers, such
as a wall inscribed with hexagrammatic wardings or a starship's Gellar
Field.

#### Fear (variable)

A creature with this Trait possesses a disturbing appearance. The fear it
inspires always carries an associated value (1–4) expressing how terrible
the creature is to behold. The higher the Fear Rating, the harder it is for
others to resist madness at the sight of it, as shown in the following
table.

| Fear Rating | Description | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Disturbing | −0 |
| 2 | Frightening | −10 |
| 3 | Horrifying | −20 |
| 4 | Terrifying | −30 |

When a character encounters a creature with this Trait, he makes a
Willpower Test, modified by the severity of the creature's Trait. On a
success, the character may behave normally. On a failure, the character
must roll on **Table 10-4: The Shock Table** (page 294), adding +10 to the
roll for every 10 points by which the Willpower Test failed. The results of
this roll have variable effects, but typically leave the character a
quivering wreck. See Chapter X: The Games Master for further details on the
effects of Fear and Insanity.

#### Flyer

A creature with this Trait possesses the natural ability to fly, whether by
broad leathery wings, strange Warp energies, or inflatable gas sacs. This
Trait always includes a number indicating the speed at which it moves in
flight. This number replaces the Agility Bonus for Movement Actions. See
Chapter IX: Playing the Game for further details on flying.

#### From Beyond

The mind of a creature with this Trait stands beyond the petty weaknesses
and precarious sanity of a mortal mind. It is immune to Fear, Pinning,
Insanity Points, and psychic powers used to cloud, control, or deceive its
mind.

#### Machine

A creature with the Machine Trait is composed of inorganic material and is
generally more resilient than beings of flesh. Machines do not need to
breathe, are immune to the effects of vacuum, extreme cold, and any
mind-affecting psychic effect, and their Armour Points also protect against
fire damage. Machines are also more resistant to injury and possess 1 to 5
Armour Points per hit location. This number is listed alongside the Trait.

> **Trait: Mechanicus Implants** (Sidebar)
> You have been judged a suitable vessel for the following implants:
> - **Electro-graft** — a small port implanted into the nervous system.
>   With appropriate training, it allows interfacing with machine data-ports
>   and certain data networks. Electro-grafts can take many forms, such as
>   electoos, skull shunts, finger probes, or spine jacks.
> - **Electoo Inductors** — palm-sized metal skin implants that look like
>   tattoos to the uninitiated. They are wired into the nervous system and
>   draw power from the flesh's bio-electric emanations. They can emit or
>   draw energy in a variety of ways. Electoo Inductors can be any colour
>   and appear anywhere on the body — most often on the hands or
>   mechadendrites.
> - **Respirator Unit** — this implant replaces the lower face and throat
>   with tubes, cables, vox-grilles, or other augmetic parts. It purifies
>   the air breathed and grants a +20% bonus against airborne poisons and
>   gas weapons. The Respirator Unit also contains a vox-synthesiser that
>   can transmit the voice in a variety of ways. Respirators can appear as
>   simple grille units or elaborate mask-like carvings.
> - **Cyber-mantle** — a framework of metal, cabling, and impulse
>   transmitters bolted onto the spine and lower ribcage. Along with other
>   implants, this mantle serves as a subdermal anchor point. Among some
>   servants of the Omnissiah, the cyber-mantle is often referred to as
>   "the true flesh." One would have to look beneath the red robes of a
>   Tech-Priest to see what a cyber-mantle looks like — so no one admits to
>   ever having seen one.
> - **Potentia Coil** — embedded within the cyber-mantle is an energy
>   module called a Potentia Coil. This mass can store energy and generate
>   various types of fields. Coils come in many forms, from small crystal
>   stacks to bulky electrical galvinators salvaged from vehicle engines.
>   Many hunches within the Adeptus Mechanicus are attributed to a
>   primitive coil.
> - **Cranial Circuitry** — a series of linked processors, implants, and
>   cortical circuits that enhance mental faculties. Most sit in a housing
>   bolted to the skull, others within the brain itself. As devotion grows,
>   more and more of the portion of the brain concerned with useless things
>   such as emotion and intuition can be removed to make room for further
>   augmentations. Cranial circuits often look quite crude and rather old.

#### Multiple Arms

This creature possesses more than one pair of arms. It increases its
Toughness characteristic by +10 and gains a +10 bonus on Strength Tests
involving movement (such as Climb and Swim). In addition, the creature can
use the Multiple Attack action to gain two attacks in its turn.

#### Natural Armour

This creature possesses naturally tough hide or an exoskeleton that
provides it some protection against attacks. This Trait always includes a
numeric value indicating how many Armour Points the creature gains on all
hit locations.

#### Natural Weapons

This creature possesses sharp claws, teeth, spines, or other natural
weapons with which to attack its foes. It counts as armed even without a
wielded weapon. Its attacks deal 1d10 damage plus its Strength Bonus. The
creature can attack with its Natural Weapons using its Weapon Skill, but
cannot parry with them, nor can it be disarmed — unless one hacks off its
limbs! Natural Weapons always count as Primitive.

#### Phase

A creature with this Trait can turn its body into an immaterial state,
allowing it to pass ghost-like through solid objects or barriers of any
kind. This Trait functions exactly like the Incorporeal Trait, except that
the creature can become solid or immaterial as a half action.

Phase has additional restrictions. A creature in the Phase state cannot
pass through psychically charged barriers, holy wards, or energy fields
that distort space or manipulate the Warp (such as a Gellar Field or a Void
Shield).

> **Explorator Abilities in Game Terms** (Sidebar)
> The abilities of Explorators are a complicated mix of technology,
> mummery, and religion; nonetheless, it is possible to describe how these
> seemingly miraculous powers translate into game terms.
>
> From the players' perspective, Explorator abilities are represented very
> simply. In ROGUE TRADER, every character with the Explorator career
> starts with the Mechanicus Implants Trait. This represents the various
> augmentations built into the character. By spending XP on various
> Talents, the character learns to use these implants to produce all sorts
> of effects — from bio-electric energy bolts to powerful magnetic fields.
>
> The augmentations described in the Mechanicus Implants Trait are so
> deeply woven into the Explorator's body that they do not count as
> equipment. They are indistinguishable from mortal flesh. These implants
> are so integral a part of an Explorator that he would not survive their
> complete removal — nor would he want to. A life without them would be no
> life at all. In essence, an Explorator's Wounds represent damage to both
> his soft tissue and to these Trait-specific augmentations. When an
> Explorator heals damage, this also represents repair of any damage to
> this machinery as well as to his body.
>
> In addition to these "free" augmentations, Explorator player characters
> may also acquire other cybernetic implants, such as auger arrays,
> mechadendrites, and bionic limbs. Unlike the augmentations granted by the
> Mechanicus Implants Trait, these are treated like any other cybernetic
> implant. They can be targeted, damaged, removed, or upgraded — see
> Bionic Replacement Limbs & Body Parts on page 147 for further details.
>
> As always, questions about damage to or removal of implants and their
> effects on an Explorator's Talents should be decided by the Game Master.
> No doubt this wise and venerable person will arrive at a generally
> acceptable, healthy answer.

#### Quadruped

Quadrupeds are considerably faster over distance than their two-legged
counterparts. To calculate their Movement, their Agility Bonus is doubled.
This Trait is intended for creatures with hind legs and forepaws. Creatures
with more than four legs can also gain this Trait, but increase the
Agility Bonus multiplier by 1 for each additional pair of legs (six legs =
AB×3, eight legs = AB×4, and so on).

#### Regeneration

Creatures with this Trait heal very quickly. Each round, at the start of
its turn, the creature tests Toughness to remove 1 point of damage. It
loses this Trait when killed.

#### Size

**Trait Groups:** Miniscule, Puny, Scrawny, Hulking, Enormous, Massive

Creatures come in one of seven different size categories, as shown in the
following table. Size affects Movement as well as how easy or hard it is
to hit the creature in combat. (When calculating a creature's Movement, the
Size modifier is applied first, followed by other modifiers from further
Traits or Talents. Base Movement can never be reduced below 1.) For
comparison, a human is an Average-sized creature.

**Table 14-2: Size**

| Size | Modifier | Concealment | Base Movement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Miniscule | −30 | +30 | AB−3 |
| Puny | −20 | +20 | AB−2 |
| Scrawny | −10 | +10 | AB−1 |
| Average | 0 | 0 | AB |
| Hulking | +10 | −10 | AB+1 |
| Enormous | +20 | −20 | AB+2 |
| Massive | +30 | −30 | AB+3 |

#### Sonar Sense

A creature with this Trait perceives its surroundings by emitting a
frequency of sound, allowing it to pinpoint the location of any solid
object within 30 metres. Other creatures within range who pass a
Difficult (−10) Awareness Test can perceive the peculiar wailing sound.

#### Soul-bound

This creature's soul is bound to a higher purpose or being in exchange for
a measure of protection. When this Trait is first gained, it must be
determined which entity the soul is bound to. Many Imperial psykers,
Astropaths in particular, are for example bound to the Emperor, while
Chaos sorcerers may be bound to one of the Ruinous Powers. A soul-bound
psyker rolls an additional d10 when required to roll on **Table 6-3: Perils
of the Warp** (page 161), and discards the die of his choice to achieve a
more favourable result.

Upon gaining Soul-bound status, a character must choose one of the
following effects: 1d10 Insanity Points, the permanent loss of his
eyesight, the permanent loss of 1d10 points of a characteristic, or a
random Mutation (only when bound to the Ruinous Powers). In addition, a
soul-bound character is permanently indebted to the entity, which no doubt
brings with it all manner of further duties and consequences.

#### Sturdy

Sturdy creatures are hard to move and gain a +20 bonus on Tests to resist
Grappling and the Takedown Talent.

#### Strange Physiology

A creature with this Trait possesses an unusual, alien, or wholly bizarre
anatomy. All hits count as Body hits, and death occurs when damage equals
or exceeds the creature's Wounds.

#### The Stuff of Nightmares

Some Warp entities are so terribly powerful that the will sustaining their
bodies in our reality is barely possible to break or disturb. Such
entities are completely immune to the effects of poisons, disease, the
need to breathe, most environmental hazards, Bleeding, Stunning, and every
Critical Result except one that would destroy them outright — unless
caused by a psychic power, a Force Weapon, or a holy attack.

#### Toxic

A creature with this Trait is poisonous. A target struck by its Natural
Weapons must make a Toughness Test. The toxins may instead be transmitted
by contact with the creature's skin or by inhaling its stench, in which
case a Toughness Test must be made upon contact or approach. A failed
Toughness Test means the victim has been poisoned. A typical poison deals
1d10 points of damage, ignoring Armour Points. Variations, if any, are
noted in the creature's description.

#### Unnatural Characteristic

One or more of the creature's characteristics are unnatural. Each time
this Trait is gained, a characteristic is chosen and its Bonus doubled.
For example, a creature with Strength 41 normally has a Strength Bonus of
4. With this Trait, its Strength Bonus increases to 8.

This Trait can be gained multiple times. Each time, a new characteristic
may be chosen, or a previously chosen one. If the Trait is applied again to
the same characteristic, the Bonus multiplier increases by 1. For example,
one selection multiplies the Characteristic Bonus by ×2, two selections by
×3, and three selections by ×4. This Trait does not change the creature's
Movement — Movement is based on the creature's unmodified Agility Bonus.

On Opposed Characteristic Tests, on a success, the Bonus multiplier is
added to the Degree of Success.

#### Unnatural Senses

The creature can perceive its surroundings using senses other than sight
or hearing — by means of special organs, fine hairs, or some other
unsettling trait, to become aware of its surroundings. This Trait always
includes a range, usually 15 metres.

#### Unnatural Speed

The creature moves at incredible speed. To determine Movement, the
creature doubles its Agility Bonus (after modifiers from other Traits and
factors, especially Size).

#### Warp Instability

Most Warp entities have only a tenuous hold on our reality, often
sustained only by terror and bloodshed, and can be disrupted if their
manifested forms take sufficient damage. If a creature with this Trait
takes damage and fails to deal damage or Insanity Points to any other
creature by the end of its next turn, it must make a Willpower Test. The
creature suffers 1 damage on a failure, plus 1 damage per Degree of
Failure. If this would deal damage equal to or exceeding the creature's
Wounds, it is hurled back into the Warp.

#### Warp Weapon

Creatures with this Trait possess weapons that are partially immaterial
and can thus ignore such mundane things as armour or cover. Natural
Weapons and attacks made by a creature with this Trait ignore physical
armour, unless that armour is made of psychoactive materials or possesses
the Holy quality. Force Fields still work normally against these attacks.

## Mutations (Introduction — physically on PDF 372, see 14-Mutations.md for Table 14-3)

One of the most pervasive signs of corruption in the Imperium is the
manifestation of mutations. These subtle or not-so-subtle alterations of
the flesh can arise from contact with toxic pollutants, alien biospheres,
genetic manipulation, radiation, and other hazards, while others are
brought on by contact with the Warp, daemonic possession, and other
dangers of the universe. Tolerance of mutants varies from world to world.
Subtle or cosmetic changes are accepted, and such beings are known as
Abhumans or "subs"; on some worlds they form an oppressed and exploited
underclass. The truly horrifying mutants are put down on sight, purged
with a flamer wherever they are found.

### Gaining Mutations

Characters, creatures, and things acquire mutations in various ways, though
the most common cause is physical corruption. Twisted environments,
chemicals, illicit drugs, or even sheer whim can all lead to mutation, and
there is little protection to prevent a mutation once it has taken hold.
Mutations function like Traits, meaning characters normally cannot acquire
them with Experience Points.

### Mutation

Whenever a mutation occurs, roll on **Table 14-3: Mutations** to determine
which kind of mutation is gained. Duplicate results are always re-rolled.
(The full Table 14-3 is on PDF page 373 and is captured in
`14-Mutations.md`.)
