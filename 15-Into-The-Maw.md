## Introduction (book pages 379–380)

"Into the Maw" is an introductory Rogue Trader adventure for starting-level characters.
It serves as an entry point into the game's rules and themes, offers guidance for novice
GMs and players, and can act as a springboard for further adventures in the Koronus
Expanse. Explicit note in the book: players should stop reading here — the rest of the
chapter is for the GM's eyes only.

Beyond the Calixis Sector lies the Koronus Expanse, a perilous, barely charted stretch of
void. At the heart of the adventure lies the legend of the **Righteous Path**, an ancient
treasure ship that vanished into the warp long ago and is said to carry the plundered
wealth of an entire world aboard.

### The Tale of the Righteous Path

Over 2,500 years ago, in the time of Drusus and the Angevin Crusade, the warlord and
free-captain **Lorcanus Ryn** conquered world after world for the crusade aboard his
grand cruiser Righteous Path. One of these worlds was **Krystallian**, the 73rd world
brought to the light of the Imperium: an ancient human colony that had fallen to false
gods and the priests of the **Talisar**, and worshipped the "Myriad of Faces" — covered
in glittering cloud temples, of immense wealth and blasphemous grandeur.

Ryn's conquest of Krystallian took three days of fire and blood. Accounts of the world's
true wealth differ: some speak of artefacts, statues of gold and gems, and hidden
archeotech vaults; others claim the population itself was part of the plunder (pure
genetic stock, carried off in stasis coffins to be trained as elite warriors or
servants); still others whisper that Krystallian dated back to the Dark Age of
Technology and harboured secrets of incalculable worth.

Ryn filled the Righteous Path to bursting with plunder — he even tore out gun decks and
launch bays to make room, and marooned thousands of crew members. He then vanished into
the warp with his ship, and from history. Since then, many expeditions (members of the
crusade, free traders, adventurers, Imperial servants) have searched for the ship — in
vain. Krystallian is said to have once lain at the edge of what is now the Calixis
Sector, only a few light-years from the Maw; old void-farers therefore suspect that Ryn
was caught by the storms of the Koronus Passage and dragged deep out into the Expanse.

## Rules Framework: Endeavours in This Adventure

The adventure makes use of the Endeavour system (see p. 279): spread across the main
plot are numerous **Objectives** (side quests) offered by NPCs or opportunities.
Completing them earns **Achievement Points**. These count toward the overarching goal
"Find and Plunder the Righteous Path" (total Endeavour value **1,500 Achievement
Points**). Achievement Points earned beyond the Endeavour's value are converted into
additional Profit at the end: **+1 Profit for every 100 excess Achievement Points**.
Objectives are optional, but the GM should actively offer them to the players, as they
are the adventure's central source of Profit.

## PART ONE: THE RIDDLE OF THE RIGHTEOUS PATH (book pages 380–384)

> "Rogue Trader lives and dies by the strength of his deeds." — Vos Karlorn, Lord-Captain
> of the Emperor's Testament

### Overview

The Explorers arrive at Port Wander and are contacted by **Orbest Dray**, an old servant
of the dynasty. Dray hands them an astropathic mnemolith (a kind of map) that points the
way to the Righteous Path. No sooner is it handed over than Dray and the Explorers are
attacked by agents of the Rogue Trader **Hadarak Fel**, who want to steal the stone. The
theft fails, but Fel's consort-psyker **Lady Ash** manages to read the stone's message
remotely. The Explorers learn from the local Adeptus Arbites that Hadarak is behind the
attack, but for lack of proof nothing can be done against him. The Explorers must
therefore find their own way to decipher the message. Shortly afterward, Hadarak
receives them in person, sizes up his competition, and challenges them to a race through
the Koronus Passage. The section ends with the Explorers departing for the Passage.

### Arrival at Port Wander

The Explorers reach Port Wander, the vast Imperial outpost at the edge of the Calixis
Sector in the Drusian Marches, gateway to the Koronus Expanse. The station hangs in the
faint light of Rubycon II amid shattered asteroid remnants, surrounded by a flotilla of
Imperial vessels. After the usual formal welcomes, one vox message stands out: encoded
in the dynasty's personal cipher, from a man named **Orbest Dray**, who has long been
waiting for a member of the family to return and wants to deliver a message and a gift
from the Rogue Trader's great-grandfather. Meeting point: the **Court of the Dead**,
Port Wander's vast market.

> **The Great-Grandfather:** He remains unnamed in the adventure; the GM should freely
> tie him to the background of the player characters and their dynasty.

### An Ancient Message

In the crowded, stinking Court of the Dead, the Explorers meet Orbest Dray, who at first
looks like an ordinary scrap-picker but identifies himself as a servant of the Rogue
Trader line through a dynasty seal and the oaths of service. Dray was once a deck
officer aboard the **Emperor's Testament** under **Vos Karlorn**, part of the
great-grandfather's fleet. During an expedition the ship was caught in a violent warp
storm and blown far off course; in an unknown system, the ship's astropath picked up a
centuries-old (at least 1,500 years) distress call — an "echo in the warp" — from a lost
Imperial vessel. It turned out to be an astropathic marker from the legendary Righteous
Path.

Characters with **Common Lore (Koronus Expanse)** or **Scholastic Lore (Legend)** know
the legend already; otherwise Dray tells it (see above, "The Tale of the Righteous
Path"). Karlorn dutifully decided not to pursue the treasure at once, and instead
returned to Port Wander; when the Rogue Trader failed to reappear, Karlorn set out to
search for him and entrusted the map to Dray for safekeeping. Karlorn never returned —
for over 100 years, Dray has been waiting for a member of the dynasty to come back.

Dray hands the Explorers a small, gene-coded **stasis casket** bearing the dynasty's
crest. It opens in the presence of the Rogue Trader or close relatives and contains a
smooth black stone — an **astropathic mnemolith** carrying the Righteous Path's
transmission. Any psyker in the party instantly recognises what the stone is and can
read it by touch; otherwise Dray, or a character with Common Lore (Navis Nobilite) or
(Rogue Traders), recognises the item and recommends having the ship's Navigator or
Astropath read it. (For its interpretation, see "Deciphering the Riddle" below.)

### A Botched Ambush

Hadarak Fel (Fel dynasty, see NPC box below) has long been watching Dray, having learned
of his knowledge of a clue to the Righteous Path (partly through Dray's own
carelessness). He knows that Dray does not know the clue himself, but that it lies in a
gene-locked casket that only the returning Rogue Trader line can open. With the
Explorers' arrival, Hadarak sees his moment and sends his consort-psyker **Lady Ash**
along with armsmen to watch and strike. While Dray tells his tale, Lady Ash lurks in the
shadows of the crowd; her psyber-raven **Pyrexia** hides high above the Explorers.

As soon as the casket is opened, Lady Ash recognises the mnemolith and sends Pyrexia
swooping down to steal it:

- Every Explorer may attempt a **Difficult (−10) Perception Test** to spot the
  psyber-raven. Anyone who succeeds can grab it as a Half Action before the bird flies
  out of reach with the stone. A successful hit on Pyrexia makes it drop the stone.
- Even if Pyrexia escapes: the stone is too heavy to carry off and falls to the ground
  about 20 metres away, in the middle of the crowd.
- Lady Ash then sends her armsmen (as many as there are Explorers present; profile
  **Oathsworn Bodyguard**, p. 372) to attack, covering Pyrexia's escape. They form a
  loose circle roughly 30 metres around the Explorers and use ranged weapons to force
  them into cover — they don't intend to kill, only to delay.
- **Crowd rule:** For the first 3 rounds (until the crowd clears), all combatants'
  Agility Bonus (and thus movement) is halved by jostling bodies. In addition, whenever
  someone is hit, roll **1d10 — on 1–5 it's a normal hit, on 6–10 they gain 8 AP of
  cover** (bystanders soak up the shot).
- Lady Ash never personally joins the fight. If Pyrexia escapes, it flies high into the
  gantries above the market; Lady Ash lets the panicking crowd carry her away — her goal
  is already achieved, since touching the stone let her read its message through her
  bond with Pyrexia.
- A psyker in the party can sense, with a successful **Ordinary (+10) Psyniscience
  Test**, that Lady Ash scanned the stone through her familiar.
- If the Explorers attack Lady Ash directly, she flees immediately.

**Swift Justice:** After 8 rounds of combat, a 12-strong Adeptus Arbites patrol arrives;
surviving armsmen surrender. Their leader is **Arbitrator-Sergeant Targos**, humourless
and unimaginative. He questions the Explorers as a formality (such incidents are common
in Port Wander and rarely pursued), but politely asks them, given their status, to
accompany him to the Adeptus Arbites district complex. The Explorers are under no
compulsion (their rank protects them from arrest without higher authority); if they
refuse, Targos leaves unfazed with his men (and the armsmen, living and dead).

### Interested Parties

If the Explorers follow Targos, they are brought before **Precept-Marshall Kyra
Valkyran**, who seems more annoyed than interested but hears them out (such disputes
between nobles and Rogue Traders are everyday business). With Targos's information she
can identify the attackers as servants of the **Fel dynasty** and crew of Hadarak Fel —
but without hard proof that he ordered the attack, she cannot act against him because of
his Warrant of Trade; not even another Rogue Trader's word is enough. She merely warns
the Explorers to be careful and promises swift intervention should further incidents
occur.

Orbest Dray (who has by now joined the Explorers) recognises the name Hadarak Fel: he
once had contacts with Fel through his old captain Karlorn, and suspects Hadarak learned
of the mnemolith and wants to find the Righteous Path himself — which is true. Because
the stone is gene-sealed (any unauthorised attempt to open it would have destroyed it),
Hadarak could not simply steal it and instead had Dray watched. With Lady Ash's scan now
complete, he prepares to depart.

### Deciphering the Riddle

As soon as a psyker or psychically sensitive character examines the mnemolith, its
astropathic message opens as a vision: a flickering star surrounded by nebula and void,
orbited by a dust world and a frozen halo. From this, the Explorers must work out the
exact location of the system described.

A **Difficult (−10) Common Lore (Koronus Expanse) Test** lets a character recognise that
the vision's celestial phenomena could match **Winterscale's Realm** within the Expanse.
The GM should let the Explorers decide for themselves whom to approach for further
information — offering suggestions if needed. There are three ways to obtain the
necessary information in Port Wander:

1. **Petition the Imperial Navy or Administratum:** Requires a Profit of at least **45**
   to impress and bribe bureaucrats. On success they learn Magoros's location.
2. **Question void-farers:** Requires a **Challenging (+0) Inquiry Test** and some time
   spent in the seedy drinking dens of the Gilt Processionals. Success reveals Magoros's
   location — but makes their intentions known across Port Wander, which can cause
   complications later.
3. **Seek out a master map-maker (the most subtle path):** Careful inquiries lead them to
   **Magnate Scrivener Journ** in the Chambers of Gold. Journ will sell them a chart with
   the system's location and guarantees confidentiality — but first demands they carry
   out a small errand (see Objective "A Questionable Cargo").

#### Objective: A Questionable Cargo

Journ wants some proscribed texts sent to a contact in Footfall for safekeeping (against
recent Ecclesiarchy purges; the volumes are not actually heretical, but he'd feel better
knowing they're somewhere safe).
- **Conditions:** Carry Journ's texts to Footfall and deliver them to his contact.
- **Traits:** Criminal.
- **Achievement Points:** +200.

### Leaving Port

Once he has Lady Ash's information, Hadarak returns to his ship and sets sail into the
Koronus Passage, making no secret of it. The Explorers must now hurry too, lest Hadarak
beat them to the treasure.

**Part One** is an Objective with the **Exploration** Trait; completing it awards
**500 Achievement Points**.

### NPC Summaries (Part One)

- **Orbest Dray:** A former ship's officer, a bold adventurer in his youth, now bitter
  and aged while his lord and shipmates died gloriously in the void. Loyalty and his
  oath of service are all he has left. He knows the Expanse well and serves the GM as a
  source of information for the party. Statblock: **Voidfarer** profile, p. 372.
- **Hadarak Fel:** An old Rogue Trader, over a hundred years old, kept young through
  rejuvenat treatments and xenos technology, dresses flamboyantly and plays the fop or
  fool to mislead his opponents. One of the last surviving members of the Fel dynasty, a
  feared and ruthless competitor. He avoids personal combat at all costs and is deeply
  self-serving. His ship, the **Fell Hand**, is deliberately left undefined (the GM
  should use the **Sabre**'s statistics, p. 211, or build his own ship, p. 193); his
  crew is **Veteran** (p. 214). In addition to his crew, Hadarak can take one
  Manoeuvre, Shooting, or Extended Action himself each turn. Statblock under "Important
  NPCs" below.
- **Lady Ash:** A silent, pale-skinned psyker of unknown origin; whether she is truly
  mute or simply chooses not to speak is unclear. Unconditionally (but one-sidedly)
  devoted to Hadarak, who values her only as long as she is useful. A telekinetic and
  unbonded rogue psyker, never registered by Imperial authority. Almost always
  accompanied by her psyber-raven **Pyrexia**. Statblocks under "Important NPCs" below.

## PART TWO: ACROSS A STORM-WRACKED SKY (book pages 385–386)

> "Sailing the Maw is like gliding across a razor's edge between a gale and a great
> swell; one wrong move and it will slice you open." — Dronim Halberat, Master Helmsman

### Overview

The Explorers must cross the Koronus Passage and reach the Expanse. This section offers
two encounters; the GM may also consult page 341 (the Maw) and create his own
encounters. The section ends with the Explorers reaching the Expanse.

### The Edge of the Storm

As they approach the Maw through the warp, the Explorers see the boiling rift where the
Screaming Vortex and the Void Dancer's Roil meet. The Navigator detects a gathering
**empyreal eddy** across the ship's course. The Explorers have a choice:

- **Wait:** The eddy passes after a few days, but costs time. Since rumours of the great
  treasure have already spread among the crew, they grow impatient: Ship's Morale drops
  by **1d5**.
- **Push through:** Both the pilot and the Navigator must each pass a **Difficult (−10)
  Perception Test** to plot a safe course. If either test fails, the ship is buffeted by
  the worst of the eddy and the Geller Field is struck by violent warp energy. Some of
  this energy seeps into the ship, causing nauseating visions and unnatural lethargy:
  every Explorer must make a **Challenging (+0) Willpower Test**; on a failure, they
  suffer **−10 to WS and BS tests** for the next **1d5 days** while the miasma lingers
  in their bones.

### A Gathering of Wolves — The Battleground

After several days the Explorers reach the **Battleground**, the halfway point of the
Passage: a field of wrecks, twisted debris, and ancient wreckage from a long-forgotten
war — a common resting point for ships heading into the Expanse.

Shortly after arrival, the Explorers pick up a weak distress signal from a nearby
debris field: the **Penitent Traveller**, a pilgrim ship bound for holy sites of the
Expanse. It was attacked by a wolf pack called the **Stygian Reavers** and left behind
as bait for bigger prey; its main vox array is destroyed, so the pilgrims cannot warn
the Explorers directly.

**The pirates:** Two **Wolfpack Raiders** (p. 209) of the Stygian Reavers lie hidden at
the edge of the debris field, engines cold, systems on standby (Silent Running, p. 218),
waiting for someone to investigate the pilgrim ship.
- The Explorers can spot the ambush if the sensor officer passes a **Hard (−20)
  Scrutiny + Detection Test** — success denies the raiders Surprise (p. 212) in the
  first round of combat.
- Combat begins as soon as the wolf pack is discovered, or the Explorers approach the
  Penitent Traveller or try to leave.
- The pirates' first action: a **leech mine** (built from xenos technology, hidden in
  the debris) activates and homes in on the Explorers' ship at **5 VUs of speed per
  round**, realigning itself at the start of each round. The mine's energy discharge
  drains power from the target ship's plasma drive, causing **−5 Speed**, making escape
  from the wolf pack unlikely.
- If the Explorers are surprised, combat begins at **16 VUs** distance; otherwise the GM
  sets a greater distance.
- The pirates close at maximum speed, firing from prow and dorsal batteries, aiming to
  box the Explorers in, cripple their ship, and force surrender. If the target ship is
  small enough, they attempt to board it (Boarding, p. 215) once it is crippled.
- **Environmental rules:** The battle takes place in a debris-strewn, radiation-tainted
  area (an advantage for smaller ships). All manoeuvres within the debris field suffer
  **+1 Difficulty** (p. 217). The GM should also mark several "Asteroid Fields"
  (p. 227) with especially dense debris concentration and their usual effects.
- The Stygian Reavers do not fight to the death: if one of their ships is destroyed or
  crippled, or both suffer heavy damage (below half Hull Integrity), they try to break
  off and vanish into the debris field.

Once the pirates are dealt with, the Explorers can investigate the Penitent Traveller
and either rescue it or plunder it (see Objective "Pilgrims in the Storm"). They can
then leave the Passage and press on into the **Open Void**.

### Open Void

After days of hard travel, the Explorers have crossed the Koronus Passage; the warp
storms recede, and the relative calm of the immaterium stretches into the distance.
Crossing back into realspace, the Explorers are greeted by glittering stars and the open
void of the Expanse — they have reached their destination.

**Part Two** is an Objective with the **Exploration** Trait; completing it awards
**500 Achievement Points**.

### Objective: Pilgrims in the Storm

The Penitent Traveller is a small, hastily patched transport covered in religious
iconography, no longer spaceworthy after the pirate attack. Crew and passengers are
grateful for any rescue but have little to offer beyond the goodwill of the Ministorum.
Alternatively, the Explorers can leave them to their fate and plunder the ship for more
tangible reward.
- **Conditions:** Either plunder the ship, or rescue the passengers and bring them to
  Footfall.
- **Traits:** Creed.
- **Achievement Points:** +400 (plunder the ship); +200 (rescue the pilgrims).

## PART THREE: INTO WINTER'S HEART (book pages 387–393)

> "The void is the very embodiment of the unknown; a living, breathing darkness that
> hides whole worlds within its folds." — from "Travels in Darkness" by Relicartos Rynar
> Horran

### Overview

The Explorers enter the Expanse close on the heels of Hadarak Fel. After a possible stop
at Footfall, they press on to the **Magoros** system deep within Winterscale's Realm.
The system, once part of the vanished **Egarian Dominion**, is the final resting place
of the Righteous Path — but the system is large, and the Explorers must search several
worlds for clues. An unexpected danger complicates the search: **Orks** have taken root
in the system and threaten Hadarak and the Explorers alike.

### Into the Expanse

The Explorers reach Magoros without major trouble; the system lies within Winterscale's
Realm (p. 343) and is only **10 days**' clear travel from the Maw, across relatively
calm warp tides. Hadarak causes them no trouble during this time, as he himself is
racing to reach the system first.

### Forgotten Magoros

Magoros is one of hundreds of systems in the Expanse that has been explored, catalogued,
and then passed over — largely unexplored since Winterscale's own time, since it offers
little of interest to Rogue Traders. Unknown to all previous visitors, however, Magoros
holds the secret of the Righteous Path. The system's principal bodies:

- **The Flickering Eye:** Magoros's star, a dying, flickering pulsar whose periodic
  bursts of radiation blind sensors and hammer void shields.
- **Magoros Minor:** A sun-scorched world close to the star, swept clean of life, but
  holding secrets on its fire-blackened surface.
- **Magoros Prime:** A dust-choked graveyard with ruins of the Egarian Dominion and the
  gigantic, arcane **Star Mirror** — here the Explorers will find the system's secrets.
- **Magoros Secondus:** A frozen jungle moon with icy spore-towers and toxic fog, once
  settled by the Egarian Dominion.
- **The Shard Halo:** A vast asteroid field billions of kilometres across in the
  system's outer reaches — here lies the wreck of the Righteous Path.

#### The Flickering Eye

Magoros's star is a rotting solar carcass that has been bleeding its life into the void
for eons; once mighty, it has decayed into a stuttering pulsar whose weak light barely
reaches the Shard Halo and is discernible only by its erratic flickers. **The star
flickers once every 102 minutes for about 58 seconds**, sending a wave of radiation
across the system that briefly blinds sensors and sends void shields stuttering;
unprotected humans risk being fried alive. Hadarak has observed this phenomenon and uses
it when he can to mask attacks or ambush Explorers who are out of contact with their
ship — the Explorers can likewise use it for an ambush of their own.

#### Magoros Minor

A small, burnt world in hard radiation and scorching solar winds — from space, a
cracked, crimson ball crisscrossed with glowing fissures. The Egarian Dominion apparently
never settled this world. Of interest here is the wreck of a crashed **Aquila Lander**
from an earlier, failed expedition. The Explorers can find the site by scanning vox
frequencies from low orbit and picking up the lander's vox-shadow, or the GM may allow a
**Very Hard (−30) Perception Test** during a landing approach to stumble upon it by
chance.

The most important surviving part is the main cogitator; most of the data is corrupted,
but a logbook entry in binary Mechanicus chant (to be decoded by the party's Enginseer)
reveals the location of a gigantic xenos telescope called the **Star Mirror** on Magoros
Prime — which, according to the log, holds detailed maps of the system as well as every
celestial event and ship visit of at least the last thousand years. If the Explorers
need a nudge, Dray can point out that this surely includes the Righteous Path's resting
place too.

#### Magoros Prime

A bleak wasteland with a dirty grey, dust- and cloud-shrouded surface. From orbit,
extensive alien ruins are visible; Dray or Explorers with **Common Lore (Koronus
Expanse)** recognise them as remnants of the **Egarian Dominion** (p. 345). Even from
the upper atmosphere the **Star Mirror** is visible — a vast crystalline structure
wrapped in an invisible cloud of electromagnetic turbulence. Away from the northern
**Egarian Hive** and the Star Mirror, the Explorers find only empty ruins and dried-out
ocean basins.

**A Nest of Trouble:** The Egarian Hive stretches across much of the northern continent,
with the Star Mirror at its centre. Landing directly at the Mirror is impossible —
strong ionic and magnetic storms around it make flight within several kilometres
impossible; the Explorers must therefore land and cover the rest on foot. In the thin
atmosphere they can survive unprotected for a few hours, though the caustic air makes
breathing unpleasant. The ruins are a labyrinth of trenches and open pits around the
Mirror.

**Orks!** Magoros holds more than the Egarians' faded splendour: decades ago an Ork
raider fleet was stranded on Magoros Prime. Though the ships were almost entirely
destroyed and their crews nearly wiped out, the Ork spore-brood took root, and over time
several rival warbands emerged, now using simple technology in their constant tribal
wars. The most powerful band, the **Bloody Skullz**, claims the territory around the
Star Mirror for itself — proximity to the alien array has made these Orks develop
unusually fast and left them (even more than usual) mentally twisted; they are the most
dangerous and are the ones the Explorers encounter on landing (Ork profile: p. 377).

**A Lady in Trouble:** Hadarak sent Lady Ash with a landing party to the Star Mirror
while he himself remains in the Shard Halo. However, she was ambushed by Orks on the
approach. As the Explorers draw near, they hear gunfire and animal grunting; sneaking
closer, they find a large chamber where Lady Ash and two surviving armsmen are fighting
off a dozen Orks. The Explorers can intervene or use the distraction to slip through to
the Mirror.
- If they help and win, Lady Ash does not attack the Explorers (unless attacked herself
  — in which case she fights a retreat and vanishes), but instead offers a temporary
  truce and accompanies them to the Star Mirror before making off with the treasure
  ship's location.
- If they leave Lady Ash to her fate, she is overwhelmed after a few rounds (but not
  killed, as becomes clear later), and the Orks then move on to the Mirror, so the
  Explorers run into them when leaving the site.

**A Universe Reflected:** On entering the Star Mirror chamber, the Explorers are
overwhelmed by its alien splendour — as if at the centre of a sea of light, floor and
ceiling barely distinguishable, the air full of swirling images. To read the information
it holds, an Explorer must concentrate on the swirling images: **Challenging (+0)
Willpower Test**. On a failure, they suffer **1d10 Insanity Points** — but still receive
the information! On success, the viewer can leaf through centuries of celestial motion,
running time forward and backward, until they see the Righteous Path's arrival: it
plunged out of a warp rift and crashed into an asteroid cluster in the Shard Halo —
where it still lies today, frozen and waiting. The Explorers now have the **exact
location** of the Righteous Path.

#### Magoros Secondus

A frozen jungle world with a dense, icy atmosphere, hostile to human life. From orbit it
appears as a cold, cloud-wrapped eye shedding icy spores into space. Landings reveal
toppled xenos structures of the Egarian Dominion — apparently once some kind of vast
energy network. Characters with **Tech-Use** can determine with an Auspex that the
network is still active and sends a faint energy beam to Magoros Prime (following it
leads to the Star Mirror). Also visible from high altitude is a massive clearing caused
by a ship crash — landing there (void suits required) reveals another secret: wreckage
of an **Ork ship**, though no living xenos remain on site.

#### The Shard Halo

Spread across billions of kilometres lies the system's glittering crown. Without the
Righteous Path's exact location, the Explorers initially find only endless frozen rock
fields and vapour clouds here. This is also where Hadarak has hidden his ship, awaiting
the Explorers' arrival.

### Exploring the Magoros System (Sidebar)

This part of the adventure is deliberately open-ended, letting the PCs explore the
system at their own pace. Once they enter the system, they can survey all the celestial
bodies described above; the GM need not steer them in any particular direction, since in
the course of their exploration they will inevitably land on Magoros Prime and discover
the Star Mirror, leading them to the Righteous Path and the adventure's conclusion.
Hadarak hides in the Shard Halo, waiting for either his servants to find the treasure
ship or the Explorers to show him the way.

### An Icy Grave — The Wreck of the Righteous Path

Once the Explorers know the location, they can travel to the icy resting place in the
Shard Halo. From a few thousand kilometres out, they can scan the asteroid's surface and
identify the twisted wreck within. To plunder the ship, they must land — the nearest
possible landing site lies about **300 metres** from the wreck, in a clearing among the
wrecks of other ships (the asteroid is a veritable ship graveyard).

If Hadarak learned the location from Lady Ash, he already has men in place: a squad of
armsmen (the same size as the party) lies in ambush along the path to the wreck. The
Explorers can detect the ambush before it strikes (see the Concealment skill, p. 80),
otherwise they are surprised. Like the Explorers, the armsmen (Oathsworn Bodyguard
profile) wear void suits.

On reaching the ship, its core is sealed behind massive energy seals connected directly
to the bridge and still humming with power. The Explorers must either return with heavy
equipment to break the seals, or reach the bridge to open them from there.

The bridge is separated from the lower decks by airlocks; opening them (or finding them
already open, if Hadarak's men got there first) releases a breath of old, stale air —
the bridge is breathable, so void suits can be removed here. In the pale light of the
Magoros star through the observation windows, a long, half-round chamber is visible,
with the Lord-Captain's throne at one end, flanked by servitor pits full of ancient,
semi-mechanical corpses. Other notable features: a **Navigator's Well** at the centre of
the chamber and an unnamed chamber below the throne (the **Cogitator Core Vestibule**).
Everything is covered in a thick layer of dust that shrouds human remains.

Searching the bridge yields little of use (rusted equipment, degraded sidearms). The
Navigator's Well holds a desiccated corpse with a **platinum medallion of House
Asmotten**, named **Astrianna**. In the Cogitator Core Vestibule, the Explorers can gain
access to the energy seals' controls — this requires time and interaction between a
character and the core's primitive machine spirit.

### Battle on the Bridge / Battle in the Asteroids

At this point Hadarak attacks to get rid of the Explorers. If he is ahead of them
(already knowing the location through Lady Ash), the Lady and **two Battle Servitors**
(p. 374) are already waiting on the bridge; if he follows them, Lady Ash and the
servitors arrive shortly after the seals are deactivated. Lady Ash uses the servitors to
keep the Explorers at bay while attacking them with her telekinetic powers.

The bridge is poorly lit: without their own light sources, the Explorers suffer **−10
to ranged attacks**, since targets are visible only by muzzle flash and flickering
shadows. After **5 rounds of combat**, the dust carpet (once someone has moved several
metres or explosive-damage weapons have been used) turns into a dense fog that limits
normal sight to **10 metres**. As on Magoros Prime, Lady Ash does not fight to the
death, but flees into the ship's ruins to hide if possible.

At the same time, Hadarak brings the **Fell Hand** into position to attack the
Explorers' ship — the GM should alert the Explorers to the attack through panicked vox
reports, weapons fire visible at the observation windows, and tremors from impacting
munitions. The GM should resolve the boarding fight before turning to the void combat
(the fight against Lady Ash should be over well before a single round of space combat
elapses anyway).

**The space battle:** The two ships begin at **20 VUs** apart; the Fell Hand emerges
from behind a particularly large boulder. Fighting in the asteroid field is dangerous —
rocks drift through firing arcs, collide with void shields, and create sensor ghosts:
- **All Ballistic Skill tests for ship weapons suffer −10.**
- The entire battle counts as taking place in an **Asteroid Field** (p. 227), including
  all the penalties listed there.
- **Using cover behind rocks:** A captain can position his ship behind several large
  boulders — **Difficult (−10) Manoeuvring + Piloting Test** (or raise the manoeuvre's
  difficulty by one step): success raises the penalty to enemy fire to **−40**. Risk: if
  the test fails, the ship counts as rammed by a frigate with **Front Armour 20**
  (instead of the usual asteroid field penalties), as it crashes into the rocks.
- **Creating dust clouds:** A weapon component is used to pulverise asteroids into dust
  — an additional **−10 penalty** on fire from a firing arc of the user's choice.
- **Asteroid gravity:** Ships near the ship graveyard can use the gravity of the large
  asteroids: **+10 on all Piloting Tests** that move the ship closer to the rocks or
  turn it toward them.

If Hadarak's ship takes too much damage (below half Hull Integrity), he retreats into
the void and leaves the Explorers their prize.

### Conclusion

With Hadarak defeated and the Righteous Path discovered, the Explorers can claim their
prize. After deactivating the energy seals, they find cargo holds full of exotic and
alien treasures — for the purposes of this adventure, the cargo is simply converted into
Profit (see Rewards below); the GM may fill the holds with concrete treasures as he
sees fit (inspiration: "The Tale of the Righteous Path"). If Hadarak Fel survives the
battle, he withdraws to lick his wounds — possibly to return later. Despite his defeat
he holds no personal grudge against the Explorers, but will not underestimate them on a
second encounter; the GM can use him as a recurring adversary. If Lady Ash escapes, she
finds her way back to Hadarak — and is unlikely to be far from him at a future meeting.

**Part Three** is an Objective (its Trait is not clearly legible in the original);
completing it awards **500 Achievement Points**.

## Rewards (book page 392)

- Plundering the Righteous Path nets the Explorers a bonus of **+3 Profit** from selling
  the recovered artefacts and treasures.
- The players total the bonuses from their completed Objectives to determine their final
  Achievement Points. Achievement Points exceeding the **1,500** required to plunder the
  Righteous Path can be traded in for additional Profit at this point: **every 100
  excess Achievement Points = +1 Profit**.
- In addition, each character is awarded **1,000 Experience Points** for completing the
  adventure (on top of any XP already earned during it), plus **50 XP** for each
  Objective the group completed.
- If the Explorers managed to defeat either Hadarak or Lady Ash, they receive an
  additional **150 XP**.
