The following section offers a useful cross-section of humanity that an
Explorer might encounter in the Koronus Expanse — whether as enemy, ally,
partner, or rival depends entirely on shifting circumstance.

## The Masses of Humanity

While the individual entries in this section represent important,
dangerous, or otherwise interesting NPCs that Explorers may encounter, the
following statblocks serve as rough templates for the countless common
souls of humanity — themed around the hardy survivors of the Koronus
Expanse.

Humanity has spread across the stars like a plague, in numbers beyond
counting. Many are born into toil and die having known little else, while
others, devoted to the Imperium's labyrinthine bureaucracy, spend years
copying words from decaying parchment onto fresh sheets without ever
understanding a single one of them. Life beyond the Imperium's borders is
perhaps even more uncertain and dangerous — hardy colonists eke out an
existence on the edge of starvation, threatened on all sides by alien
creatures, marauding xenos, and the laughter of thirsting gods.

#### Adept

With ink-stained fingers, bent over parchment-strewn writing desks, Adepts
and Scriveners can be found in every dark nook of the Imperium and beyond —
tabulating data and recording the end of lives just as mindlessly as they
would record the day's precipitation.

**Gear:** Quills, ink pots, parchment, data-slate.

#### Bloodskinner

Many worlds along the Rim depend on a steady supply of butchered meat,

### Free Trader Captain

The Imperium's countless worlds are connected by vast, interwoven trade
networks, and many planets could not exist without imports of food,
technology, or labour. This merchant marine moves goods and tithe-goods
from one system to another, transporting incalculable quantities of
life-sustaining cargo across the stars. Free Traders are usually part of
commercial fleets and travel wherever their masters command, though some
small operators continue to act independently, while others are hardy
enough (or desperate enough) to push into border regions in search of
greater profits at the furthest edge of the Imperium. Their passages
usually go quietly, yet they know they could be called upon at any time to
give their lives in battle, and no passage through the Warp is ever safe.

### Mutant Outcast

Mutation is a fact of human existence in the 41st Millennium. On many
worlds, particularly those cut off from contact for centuries or even
millennia, the human gene pool has been irreparably damaged by generations
of exposure to pollutants and alien biospheres, but above all by genetic
manipulation and the reality-warping effects of the Warp. More and more
people are born with obvious and often severe mutations, mostly in the form
of gross physical deformities and mental aberrations. Within the Imperium,
the worst-afflicted are ruthlessly and systematically exterminated; beyond
the Imperium's borders their treatment can vary wildly — from exploitation
to dominance among worlds fallen to Chaos. On some worlds, shaped by
widespread superstition and fear, or where fanatical interpretations of the
Imperial Creed and local hatred have taken root, the slightest hint of
physical deviation usually ends at the stake. On others, mostly industrial
worlds, where mutation is sometimes regarded as an unavoidable, if
ominous, fact of life among the lower classes, entire mutant populations
are allowed to exist as a brutally oppressed and utterly expendable
workforce, with only the most excessive cases of mutation being culled.

There are numerous forms of mutant, though most commonly a basic physical
degeneration or deviation from the accepted norm of the human form is
shown. Also known as Abhuman, sub, or twist, these degenerates are often
found as slaves and outcasts on the fringes of human settlement in the
Koronus Expanse, and in abhorrent abundance on worlds touched by the Warp.

### Navy Officer

A military aristocracy of the stars spans the Officer Corps of the Imperial
Navy — an institution reaching back thousands of years in a tradition of
service, martial spirit, and sacrifice that some would call unmatched. To
serve the Imperial Navy is to be the glue that holds the Imperium together,
for without Warp travel, humanity's mighty armies would be stranded,
powerless, and swiftly torn apart by their enemies. Just as the Navy itself
holds humanity together, so too does its officer class bind and regulate
the Navy. Proud, martial, and honourable, often descended from bloodlines
that have served for centuries, they are the ones who keep crews and
indentured servants in line, uphold the traditions of service and loyalty,
and, above all, defend the front against the darkness of the void beyond.

### Oathsworn Bodyguard

The exploitation of the Expanse has amassed as much great fortune as it has
feuds and intrigue. Both are equally dangerous in a realm where scarcely
any law beyond raw power is recognised and where the void swallows the dead
without a trace. In such a world, no one, however famous or powerful, lies
beyond the reach of an assassin's hand, and the need for trustworthy
personal protection is of the utmost importance. While witless muscle and
Hired Guns of dubious origin are plentiful, a trained professional, or
"Oathsworn" as they are called in the jargon of the Expanse, is another
matter entirely. Depending on the contract, their role ranges from simple
escort duty, to laying down their own life for their employer, to acting as
his personal agents who "deal with" those who have offended their masters.
Respected bodyguards can, if they survive, rise to wealth and standing
themselves, some even entering into family contracts that endure as long as
their employer's bloodline. Others specialise in dangerous locales, using
their familiarity with the local environment to offer protection within
that territory.

### Renegade

There are many who flee the Imperium and become renegades, and just as many
reasons for doing so. Failed revolutionaries, refugees, Imperial deserters,
outlaws, heretics, and worse — all of them set out into the Expanse,
hoping to escape persecution and do as they please. Many try to found their
own societies on shadowy and undiscovered worlds, or hide at the fringes of
more established colonies, but an equally large number are drawn to
plunder and piracy in order to survive. Many human pirates and raiders
worship neither Chaos nor ally themselves with aliens, but are simply
criminals who take what they can, or who have fallen into a merciless cycle
of raiding and flight to sustain their lawless existence. There is,
however, a darker core of those who have fallen far from the light and
succumbed to the most abhorrent cravings and cruelties. These are the most
feared renegades, hated by colonist, Imperial, Rogue Trader, and

### Warp Witch

Among the void born there are psykers who flee Imperial authorities as soon
as their powers awaken, or who are born beyond their reach. Those who
survive this dark transformation are rare and often insane, yet no less
sought after in certain circles. This is because these Warp Witches
possess, among their psychic gifts, a — however crude — ability to
navigate the Warp and heed its unseen winds, though never with the
certainty and skill of a true Navigator or Astropath. Renegades and
corsairs who strike a devil's bargain with a Warp Witch may find that the
price demanded by these capricious and malicious psykers turns out even
higher than they could have guessed. Often, the powers they use to navigate
the Warp are not granted by their own gifts, but by daemons sitting
unseen upon their shoulders.

## Servitors

Servitors are fusions of flesh and machine — made from vat-grown organic
material, salvaged corpses, or mind-wiped human bodies. Millions of these
cyborg creations are found throughout the Imperium and human-settled space,
carrying out monotonous functions without deviation or complaint. Servitor
technology is said to predate the Imperium itself and is the exclusive
prerogative of the Adeptus Mechanicus. Servitors come in a seemingly
endless variety of forms, from baroque works of art for the needs of the
rich and powerful, to crude, misshapen labour units for the worst industrial
zones, to warlike monsters of steel intended as near-mindless living
weapons. The most common purpose of servitors is carrying out mundane
tasks such as attending to nobles, routine maintenance, loading cargo,
sorting endless documents, or tirelessly guarding a particular location.

### Battle Servitor (Charron-Pattern)

The Charron-Pattern is a light combat servitor, humanoid in form, commonly
found in the service of the Imperial Navy and numerous other starships as
boarding troops and defenders. Those found in the Koronus Expanse are often
very old, their inorganic components recycled through numerous bodies and
salvaged from the fallen of the battlefield. This has earned them a
reputation for unreliable Machine Spirits in combat, occasionally failing
to distinguish friend from foe. This makes some captains reluctant to
deploy them except in the direst need.

## The Xenos

> *"I have walked worlds were no man has trodden before and left none alive in
> my passing. I have beheld the idols of gods worshiped before the dawn of
> humanity and cast them down. I have tasted the blood of civilisations
> perished, and found it good."*
> — Attributed to Erasmus Haarlock, Rogue Trader

There are many sentient races beyond humanity in the galaxy, both known and
unknown. Beyond the Imperium's borders lurk numerous alien civilisations,
or "xenos," as humanity knows them. The following section offers a
selection of xenos known within the Koronus Expanse, each with its own
capacity to travel the stars, as either enemies or, possibly, allies of a
Rogue Trader.

### Eldar Corsair

Few humans know much about the enigmatic, ancient race of the Eldar, beyond
accounts of haughty contempt, fickle temperament, powerful technology, and
the unnatural, subtle cruelties of merciless raiders who strike like
ghostly phantoms. Despite these conflicting accounts, there are those
foolish and wealthy enough to seek out the extravagantly expensive services
of these so-called Corsairs. Whoever employs the skills of these highly
efficient mercenaries takes a very serious risk. Tales of their mastery and
brilliance in battle are told almost as often as those of their fickleness,
sudden betrayal, and joyful slaughter — their silken armour gleaming with
the blood of former allies.

Within the Koronus Expanse, humanity knows of two particularly notorious
groups of Eldar corsairs: the fickle and deadly mercenaries of the
"Children of Thorns" and the mysterious exterminators known as the "Crow
Spirits."

### Ork Freebooter

Imperial lore holds that the brutal Orks have contested humanity's
supremacy over the stars since time immemorial. Bred for bloodshed,
violence, and little else, the average Ork is a massive, humanoid simian
with tough, greenish-grey hide and a jutting jaw full of jagged, tooth-like
tusks. Frighteningly strong — most can effortlessly tear a human apart
with bare hands — Orks are also exceptionally resilient to injury,
largely fearless, and able to thrive unaided in many dangerous
environments. Orkoids display a startling variety of physical types and
sub-species, with the largest and most brutal invariably in command.
Despite lacking intelligence, Orks possess an innate low cunning and a
simple but effective tactical sense. Combined with a knack for scavenging
and building their own crude but highly effective technology, this makes
them a threat far exceeding the almost animalistic xenos raider they might
appear to be at first glance.

Barbaric, perpetually conflict-torn Ork empires are scattered across known
space, and the Koronus Expanse is no exception — a region called the
"Undred-Undred Teef" is home to a long-established Ork domain, from which
frequent raids and pirate attacks by plundering Ork warbands of loota-krews
and freebooter pirates are notorious.

### Kroot Mercenary

Very rarely encountered in the Koronus Expanse, the Kroot are not natives
of the local stars, but nomadic wanderers from half a galaxy away. As a
mercenary race, their savage, clan-like mentality conceals both perceptive
and intellectual abilities one would scarcely credit them with. A
carnivorous and highly adaptable species, a single Kroot stands
significantly taller than a human and bears many features betraying its
avian heritage. With a crest of spines, a beaked maw, and the ability to
move with incredible speed and sinewy physical power, a Kroot is a
formidable opponent. Their martial skill — combined with an unsettling
reputation for eating those they slay — lends these freelance warriors a
justly feared reputation, and they command a high price from trading
criminals and lapsed elements willing to flout Imperial law to hire them.

## From Beyond

> *"…And I beheld the Warp in the burning nothingness of its eyes and fell to
> my knees and wept, my faith gone asunder, cursing the name of the mother
> that gave birth to me for the truth of existence is too terrible to know…"*
> — From the Canticle of Shebanus the Fallen

Beyond our physical reality lies the Warp, a turbulent and ever-shifting
cosmos of unreality and raw energy, shaped not by shabby physical laws but
by unfathomable madness and echoing impulses and possibilities; it is this
very realm of madness that ships must cross to traverse interstellar
distances, and in its depths dwell entities formed from the stuff of
nightmares, bound by no law but their own cravings.

### Warp Predator (Ebon Geist)

These near-mindless creatures are animalistic predators whose hunger for
life is boundless. They are drawn to the soul-sparks of mortal life like
sharks to bloodied water. One such predator is the Ebon Geist, a thing
blacker than the emptiest void, a murderous shadow, thin and writhing, able
to glide through dark spaces like a nightmare fading into oblivion. The
geist murders with its icy claws, leaving nothing but corpses drained to
desiccation — the screaming shadows of its victims burned cold into the
hull where they perished.

> **The Daemon** (Sidebar)
> The forms and blasphemous appetites a daemon can display are without
> number, and literally thousands of distinct manifestations are recorded
> in the restricted archives of the Ordo Malleus of the Imperial
> Inquisition. Many reflect the nature of their patrons among the Powers of
> Chaos and act as foot soldiers in the legions of Hell, while others
> represent the congealed substance of humanity's worst fears and hidden
> evils, as well as the lingering nightmares of alien races long turned to
> dust. Regardless of their form, daemons are formed from the pure
> substance of the Warp and cannot easily maintain their hold on our
> universe without the aid of bloodshed, misery, and death.
