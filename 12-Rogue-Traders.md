## Rogue Traders — Basics

Rogue Traders are free-operating agents licensed by the Adeptus Terra, acting beyond the reach of
the Astronomican and in as-yet unvisited regions — areas where even astropaths cannot communicate.
They decide autonomously how to deal with alien cultures: annihilating dangerous species, making
contact with useful races, or plundering worlds rich in technology or minerals. This requires
substantial resources (ships, troops, technicians, settlers). Many come from high positions within
the Adeptus Terra, the Imperial Navy, the Imperial Guard, or the Inquisition; some are influential
civilians (particularly Navigators). Rogue Traders are often regarded as outsiders — some pious,
some little more than pirates and scoundrels; usually charismatic, extravagantly dressed, with
exotic equipment (Jokaero digital weapons, xenos phase weapons, Ha'Kaidan neuro-stars, and the like).

## The Warrant of Trade

The Warrant of Trade is an actual, highly venerated document — comparable to an Inquisitor's rosette
— signed/sealed by the mightiest people in the Imperium (some old lineages claim it was signed by the
Emperor Himself). It is rarely carried openly, usually kept in stasis vaults. It grants:

- Permission to travel beyond the borders of the Imperium and to negotiate there with any culture or
  being — with the full authority of the Senatorum Imperialis.
- Rank equal to Imperial Commanders, Inquisitors, and Space Marine Chapter Masters.
- The right to claim worlds and privileges by conquest; beyond the Fringes, the Rogue Trader defines
  the power structures themselves ("speaks with the authority of the Emperor").

**Backing:** Military resources come either directly from a high imperial office or from investors
who expect a share of the profits — some Rogue Traders "forget" this debt once they are outside the
Imperium (a bounty is possible).

## Roots (Origins of Rogue Traders)

- **Imperial Navy:** the most common origin — experienced captains/admirals, often "mavericks" with
  unconventional tactics; maintain contacts in their former branch of service; often dress in Navy
  dress uniform mixed with alien jewellery/weapons.
- **Imperial Guard:** command large ground forces, use the fleet for invasions; must recruit troops
  from conquered worlds when losses occur (sometimes feudal service obligation, sometimes coercion,
  rarely even alien mercenaries); practical rather than decorative armament.
- **Administratum:** Machiavellian power politicians who play subordinates off against one another;
  unassuming clothing, but always armed/protected (digital lasers, force fields).
- **Merchants:** the most skilled negotiators, equally capable as field commanders and at the
  bargaining table; opulent clothing with hidden weapons/sensors; ships like flying palaces.
- **Imperial Commanders:** deposed/dispossessed governors (e.g. after an alien invasion of their
  world), tasked with conquering a new world; their followers fight with particular tenacity
  (driven by loss); often preserve the culture of their lost homeworld to an exaggerated degree.
- **Inquisition:** rare, usually the result of internal politics (a Warrant as an "honourable"
  alternative to something worse); usually from the Ordo Xenos, retain considerable influence.

### Reasons for Granting a Warrant

- **Military Service:** reward for successful field commanders/admirals after decades of crusades —
  an outlet for their restless energy.
- **Political Expediency:** crusade leaders who want to keep conquered regions for themselves are
  kept busy with ever-new conquest targets (a Warrant), until their strength is exhausted.
- **Self-Promotion:** a Warrant obtained through bribery/called-in favours, as the personal
  ambition of a power-hungry official.
- **Virtual Exile:** a Warrant as an "honour" with which political rivals dispose of an opponent grown
  too powerful — the person concerned cannot refuse; some return decades later seeking revenge.

**Scions of the Rogue Trader Houses:** Descendants of old lineages must often first "serve their
time" (Imperial Navy, Guard, Administratum) before being considered worthy to take up the succession.

## Temperament (Character Types)

- **The Scoundrel:** a charming impostor, avoids violence as a first choice, takes great risks.
- **The Merchant Prince:** avoids military action as a waste of resources, prefers exclusive trade
  agreements; nonetheless defends his investments resolutely.
- **The Explorer:** restlessly curious, throws himself into the most dangerous environments, happily
  delegates the administrative duties of his rank to subordinates.
- **The Missionary:** spreads the Imperial Creed by force, sees xenos as something to be suppressed,
  often travels with a Ministorum force.
- **The Diplomat:** a pragmatist who negotiates even with xenos (e.g. Eldar), but knows exactly when
  words fail and weapons must speak.
- **The Psychopath:** borderline insane, judges by his own broken morality, likes to adopt grandiose
  titles (e.g. "Grand Conqueror"), often eventually vanishes without a trace.

## Trappings of Power

A newly appointed Rogue Trader typically receives a flagship (often a cruiser), accompanied by
frigates/escorts as well as cargo/support vessels; an experienced fleet commander if needed.
In addition, entire Imperial Guard regiments (with auxiliary troops), a staff of bureaucrats,
clerks, and artisans, as well as entire settler populations can be assigned.

## Conditions (Stipulations of the Warrant)

A Warrant with no conditions at all would be a sign of "virtual exile" — most conditions are of a
military or diplomatic nature:

- **Trooping the Colours:** a mandatory visit to a neglected/delinquent world as a demonstrative
  "state visit" (pomp, honour guard) to demand tribute/loyalty.
- **Punitive Strike:** violent action against resistance (orbital strike, targeted destruction of
  important targets), in extreme cases removal of the governor.
- **Settlement:** colonisation of a world reported as habitable — which may turn out on arrival to be
  uninhabitable or wrongly classified.
- **Reclamation:** military reconquest of rebellious/invasion-threatened worlds, often as part of a
  "Crusader Council" together with Imperial Navy/Guard/Space Marine commanders.
- **Exploration:** exploration of a specific region on assignment — often without the exploitation
  rights that are otherwise standard, which causes displeasure among Rogue Traders.

**Enforcement of conditions:** often ensured by accompanying imperial representatives (Administratum
prefects, Inquisition agents, Space Marine ships) or by covert means (embedded assassins as a
safeguard against betrayal).

## Lineage (Descent and Succession)

Two basic types: a personally granted Warrant versus an inherited Warrant. Warrants are hereditary
and are passed from generation to generation; when some lineages die out, new ones arise. Some Houses
are torn by internal power struggles over succession (up to and including assassination), but usually
maintain an outward appearance of propriety so as not to show weakness to rivals.

## Revocation and Forfeiture

A Warrant can be revoked if a Rogue Trader oversteps the limits of his power:

- **Empire Building:** building up one's own small realm is permissible in itself (as long as tithes
  are paid) — but if it becomes an open renunciation of imperial loyalty, a punitive crusade follows.
- **Acting Against the Imperium:** becoming embroiled in local wars/trade feuds that inadvertently
  work against imperial interests.
- **Consorting with the Xenos Terribilus:** contact with xenos systems marked as "perdita"
  (forbidden) — taboo even for Rogue Traders, violation often costs the Warrant.
- **Overstepping the Bounds:** a Rogue Trader fleet returning unannounced can be misinterpreted by
  the Imperial Navy as a threat and lead to armed confrontation.

## Rewards

The greatest rewards are wealth (through trade or conquest, often both at once), fame beyond one's
own homeworld, and above all the chance to found one's own noble line that endures across generations.
