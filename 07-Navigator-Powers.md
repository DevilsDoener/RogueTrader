# Chapter VII: Navigators

> Source: *Rogue Trader Core Rulebook*, book pages 173–186 / PDF pages 177–190.
> History and House flavour are condensed; Lineage rules, Powers, Mutations, and Warp Navigation are fully captured as a game reference.

## Navigator Gene, Warp Eye, and Navis Nobilite

Navigators are a hereditarily altered human subspecies. Their third eye perceives the warp, enables Warp Navigation, and is the source of their Powers. The Great Houses of the Navis Nobilite control their bloodlines and a large part of interstellar shipping; Novators lead individual Houses, and the Paternova stands above them.

By the rules, a Navigator counts as a **Psyker**, although he is not an ordinary psyker. Navigator Powers, however, **never trigger Psychic Phenomena or Perils of the Warp** and are unaffected by effects that only alter their chance.

**The Eye is Open:** A Navigator suffers **no Corruption Points from Warp Shock** (p. 299) — he can still gain Corruption Points normally from other sources.

## Choosing Your Lineage

### Nomadic Houses

- **Lore of the Wanderer:** Talented (Navigation (Warp)).
- **A Taste for the Warp:** spend a Fate Point to automatically succeed with a Navigator Power; if Degrees are relevant, `1d5` Degrees of Success, where a 5 counts as 0.
- **Scorn of Dirt Dwellers:** on planetary bodies, -10 on Fellowship and Fellowship-based Skills against locals.
- **Initial Mutation:** roll once randomly on Table 7-1.

### Magisterial Houses

- **Warp Focus:** once per session, force an opponent to re-roll a successful test against The Lidless Stare.
- **Pure Genes:** the Mutation Test is Routine (+20) instead of Ordinary (+10).
- **Exalted Lineage:** +10 on Interaction Skill Tests against Imperial Nobility.
- **Initial Mutation:** choose one of Strangely Jointed Limbs, Elongated Form, Pale and Hairless Flesh, or Eyes as Dark as the Void.

### Shrouded Houses

- **Destitute:** the group's Starting Profit Factor -1.
- **Gaze to Pierce the Soul:** Gaze into the Abyss as a bonus Power; it may be raised beyond Master to Paragon (same XP cost as Master), granting +30 on tests with this Power.
- **Mercantile Opportunists:** +10 Barter, Charm, and Carouse.
- **Initial Mutation:** roll once randomly on Table 7-1.

### Renegade Houses

- **The Fruits of Corrupted Blood:** one additional Navigator Power. Designate three Powers, including future ones: +10 on your own tests with them in addition to Mastery, and opposing Resistance Tests -10.
- **Unchecked Mutation:** Mutation Tests are Challenging (+0) instead of Ordinary (+10); begins with `1d5` Insanity Points.
- **Renegades:** begins with `1d5` Corruption Points; -10 when interacting with official Imperial bodies.
- **Initial Mutation:** roll twice randomly on Table 7-1.

## Gaining Navigator Powers

Each Power has the levels **Novice**, **Adept**, and **Master**. A new Power begins at Novice; each Rank, XP may be spent on a new Power or on the next Mastery level.

A Navigator begins with **The Lidless Stare** and chooses one further Power, or instead raises one of his Powers by one level.

| Mastery | Bonus on Tests with the Power |
|---|---:|
| Novice | +10 |
| Adept | +20 |
| Master | +30 |

Unless stated otherwise, using a Power is a Standard Action.

## Navigator Powers

### A Cloud in the Warp

- **Novice:** Willpower Test; Psychic Sight/Detection/Divination and Daemons/Warp Entities require a Challenging (+0) Perception Test to perceive him. Maintainable, but no other Power may be used while sustaining it.
- **Adept:** the Detection Test becomes Difficult (-10).
- **Master:** may extend the effect, by choice, to creatures within WP Bonus metres of the Navigator.

### Foreshadowing

- **Novice:** a Perception Test generates three Secrets. On the following turn, the first grants +10 on a test; each further one grants +10 but rolls `1d10`: on 7–10, its bonus becomes -10 instead. Unused Secrets expire. Using this again within the same hour causes `1d5` Insanity.
- **Adept:** an additional Secret turns negative only on a roll of 9–10.
- **Master:** Secrets remain usable for five rounds.

### Gaze into the Abyss

- **Novice:** Perception detects Warp Taint, Psy Rating, and strong Corruption/Mutation/Possession. Psykers with Dark Pacts and Daemons may use Opposed Willpower vs. Perception to hide. Also used to track powerful Psychic/Warp creatures.
- **Adept:** detects major Warp Disturbances within 100 km × PB; in Starship Combat, within PB Void Units.
- **Master:** also detects the use of Psychic Powers within 10 m × PB.

### Held in My Gaze

- **Novice:** targets a visible subject within 5 m × PB; Opposed Willpower. While held, the target must beat the Navigator in the Opposed Test each time to use a Psychic Power or Daemonic Presence. Daemons suffer 1 additional Damage from Warp Instability.
- **Adept:** Range 20 m × PB; Daemons suffer `2d10` Damage instead from Warp Instability.
- **Master:** no line of sight required; a Daemon suffering Warp-Instability Damage is immediately hurled back into the warp.

### The Course Untravelled

- **Novice:** Difficult (-10) Willpower; moves to any point normally reachable without obstruction within Perception Characteristic metres. Failure: 1 round Stunned; 3+ DoF: `1d10` rounds Stunned and 1 Insanity. Always 1 Fatigue.
- **Adept:** Challenging (+0), Range double the Perception Characteristic.
- **Master:** usable as a Free Action or Reaction.

### The Lidless Stare

Affects friend and foe who look upon the open Warp Eye, within 15 m, or 5 m in fog. No effect on unliving targets, Untouchables, Daemons, or Warp Entities. Looking away or not knowing about the Navigator grants +30 to resist.

- **Novice:** one Opposed Willpower Test against all viewers. Whoever scores fewer DoS suffers `1d10 + WP Bonus E`, ignoring Armour and Toughness, and is Stunned for 1 round. The Navigator gains 1 Fatigue; on his own failure by at least 1 DoF, 2 Fatigue.
- **Adept:** `2d10 + WP Bonus E`, Stunned for `1d5` rounds, and `1d5` Insanity.
- **Master:** those affected with Intelligence 20+ must immediately test Difficult (-10) Toughness or die. On success, `1d10` Insanity instead of `1d5`.

### Tides of Time and Space

- **Novice:** a Perception Test each round. Success: Agility Bonus for Initiative ×2 and an extra Half Action without Concentration. Failure: AB for Initiative halved, only one Half Action; 3+ DoF: vanishes for `1d5` rounds. Always 1 Fatigue, no extra Reaction.
- **Adept:** AB for Initiative ×3.
- **Master:** AB for Initiative ×4; instead of one extra Half Action, two Half Actions or one Full Action.

### Tracks in the Stars

- **Novice:** Perception, as Tracking, for a ship's warp trail in realspace, at most PB weeks old.
- **Adept:** at most PB months old; also trackable within the warp.
- **Master:** at most PB years old; older trails may be fragmentary.

### Void Watcher

- **Novice:** Perception to detect objects/hazards up to PB Void Units; outside Starship Combat, 1,000 km × PB. Provides normal observational information.
- **Adept:** 2 × PB Void Units or 10,000 km × PB; a Difficult (-10) Willpower Test provides the object's nature/details.
- **Master:** 5 × PB Void Units or 100,000 km × PB; additional information with an Ordinary (+10) Willpower Test.

## Navigator Mutations

When learning a new Power or increasing a Mastery level, the Navigator makes an **Ordinary (+10) Toughness Test**. On failure, he rolls on Table 7-1. Initial Mutations from the Lineage cannot be avoided. A duplicate mutation is re-rolled.

## Navigating the Warp

Warp Navigation consists of five Stages. The GM secretly determines the actual base duration; a Navigation (Warp) Test can give the Navigator an estimate.

### Table 7-2: Example Durations of Passage

| Subjective Warp Time | Example |
|---|---|
| 1 day | short passage between nearby systems on a stable, heavily used route |
| 5–10 days | systems of the same sub-sector with precise Navigation information |
| 30–60 days | across an entire Imperial Sector with precise data and known routes |
| 100+ days | dangerous journey across a Segmentum at high speed |
| Several Years | an odyssey across the galaxy |

On average, one day in the open warp corresponds to 12 days of realspace; the GM may deviate up to 1:1 or considerably more. Temporal arrival paradoxes are possible but not an ordinary random event.

### Stage One: Determining Duration of Passage

The GM sets the secret Base Duration. With a known route or good charts, a secret Navigation (Warp) Test estimates the duration; unknown destinations allow only a rough educated guess.

### Stage Two: Location of the Astronomican

Ordinary (+10) Awareness. Per DoS +10, per DoF -10 on Navigation (Warp) for this journey. At 3+ DoF, the Astronomican cannot be located. Without a beacon: Hellish (-60) Navigation (Warp); after a failure, no new attempt unless the GM allows it or a temporary disturbance ends.

### Stage Three: Charting the Course

A secret Ordinary (+10) Perception Test. Success reveals significant Warp Disturbances and improves the chance of avoiding Encounters; failure leaves dangers undetected.

### Stage Four: Steering the Vessel

Navigation (Warp), modified by Stage Two and Passage Difficulty:

- established route/Navis Prima: Easy (+30)
- open warp with no particular disturbance: Routine (+20)
- unknown/adverse route: Difficult (-10) or worse
- very well-known and frequently travelled route: the GM may waive the test and Encounters

### Table 7-3: Navigation Chart

| Result | Duration relative to Stage One |
|---|---|
| 3+ Degrees of Success | one quarter |
| 2 Degrees of Success | one half |
| 1 Degree of Success | three quarters |
| Success | standard duration |
| Failure | double duration |
| 1 Degree of Failure | triple duration |
| 2+ Degrees of Failure | quadruple duration |

If the Navigation (Warp) Test fails and a **9 comes up on either die**, the ship goes Off Course and emerges in the wrong system or region of space.

For every five full days of travel, roll once on Table 7-4; even shorter journeys generate one roll. Success in Stage Three grants +20 on every Encounter roll.

### Table 7-4: Warp Travel Encounters

| `1d100` | Event | Brief Consequence |
|---:|---|---|
| 01–03 | Reality Erosion | the ship's matter/gravity/reflections are unnaturally altered |
| 04–09 | Plague of Madness | contagious madness strikes the weakest-willed first |
| 10–18 | Incursion | a Daemonic Entity gets aboard and does harm, hidden or open |
| 19–26 | Lost Time | the crew subjectively experiences months instead of days; sanity suffers |
| 27–33 | Ghost Ships | phantom ships appear; real wrecks may hold valuable cargo |
| 34–39 | Shoals and Reefs | a Warp Shoal/Reef threatens the hull; the helmsman and Navigator must respond |
| 40–48 | Visitations | warp shades of lost friends/family appear, helpful or sinister |
| 49–53 | Gellar Field Fluctuations | the Field wavers; repair or return to realspace is needed |
| 54–67 | Warp Storm | a storm damages the ship or throws it off course; the Navigator decides the outcome |
| 68–75 | Whispers and Dreams | dreams and voices may contain hints, truths, or madness |
| 76–100 | All's Well | a safe journey with no incident |

A Gellar Field Failure should be treated as a serious horror event: Daemonic Incursion, Morale/Population losses, and damaged, depressurised, or burning Components are all possible consequences.

### Stage Five: Leaving the Warp

Hard (-20) Perception determines the accuracy of the exit point. Success reaches the intended location; failure deviates, becoming increasingly dangerous with more DoF.
